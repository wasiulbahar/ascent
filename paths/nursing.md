# Nursing

The science and art of care: nursing's history, its foundations, care from birth to the end of life, safety and evidence, the theory and ethics of nursing, and its future around the world. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free open textbooks and WHO and ICN guidance cover the core. This path teaches knowledge about nursing: **it is not a nursing qualification and not medical advice**. Nursing practice needs accredited education, supervised clinical training and registration.

# About nursing

Nursing is the care of people of all ages, families and communities, sick or well, across hospitals, homes, clinics and the community. Nurses and midwives are the largest group of health workers in the world. Nursing combines science, clinical skill, communication and ethics, and nursing research studies how care can be safer, kinder and more effective.

This path begins with nurses' own stories and nursing's founders, teaches the foundations of nursing practice, covers care across the life course, examines patient safety and evidence, reads nursing theory and ethics, and ends with nursing's global future.

**Trivia**
- There are about 28 million nurses worldwide, according to the WHO's 2020 report, making them the largest group of health workers.
- Florence Nightingale used statistics and diagrams to show that most soldiers in the Crimean War died of preventable disease.
- Mary Seacole, a Jamaican nurse, set up the “British Hotel” to care for soldiers in the Crimean War.
- International Nurses Day, 12 May, is Florence Nightingale's birthday.

### Timeline
- **1854–56 · Nightingale and Seacole** in the Crimean War.
- **1859 · Nightingale's *Notes on Nursing***
- **1860 · The Nightingale school** opens at St Thomas' Hospital, London.
- **1899 · The International Council of Nurses** is founded.
- **1960 · Henderson's definition of nursing** published by the ICN.
- **1984 · Benner's *From Novice to Expert***
- **1999 · *To Err Is Human*** puts patient safety on the agenda.
- **2020 · The International Year of the Nurse and the Midwife** and the COVID-19 pandemic.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know nursing the way a strong first year of a nursing degree expects, in knowledge (not clinical skill). You will be able to:

- **Explain what nurses do:** assessment, care planning, communication and advocacy.
- **Explain core principles:** infection prevention, safety, and person-centred care.
- **Describe care across life:** maternity, children, mental health, older people and the end of life.
- **Use evidence:** how nursing research improves care and safety.
- **Weigh the issues:** ethics, staffing, migration and nursing's future.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Nurses and nurse educators from more than one continent will confirm them before the path is published. Knowledge only: never a qualification to practise or medical advice.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
