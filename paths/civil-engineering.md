# Civil Engineering

How we build the world we live in: structures and bridges, materials such as concrete and steel, soils and foundations, water and sanitation, roads and transport, construction and safety, and resilient, low-carbon infrastructure. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free courses, open textbooks and engineering case studies cover the core. You need school algebra and trigonometry; some calculus helps. The path covers infrastructure in many countries, from megaprojects to village water systems, and includes failures and ethics. **It is knowledge, not a professional qualification.**

# About civil engineering

Civil engineering designs, builds and maintains the infrastructure societies depend on: buildings, bridges, tunnels, roads, railways, water supply, sanitation, dams and flood defences. It is the oldest engineering discipline.

This path begins with how structures stand up, teaches structural analysis, studies materials, explores soils and foundations, covers water, sanitation and transport, and ends with construction, safety, resilience and low-carbon infrastructure.

**Trivia**
- The Romans built over 400,000 km of roads, and some of their concrete harbour structures have lasted 2,000 years.
- Clean water and sanitation are often called the greatest public health advances in history.
- The Burj Khalifa in Dubai, completed in 2010, is over 828 metres tall.
- Cement production accounts for about 8% of global CO₂ emissions.

### Timeline
- **c. 2560 BCE · The Great Pyramid** is built at Giza.
- **c. 312 BCE · The Appian Way and first Roman aqueduct** are built.
- **c. 600 CE · The Anji Bridge** an early open-spandrel stone arch bridge, is built in China.
- **1771 · John Smeaton** founds the first society of civil engineers.
- **1818 · The Institution of Civil Engineers** is founded in London.
- **1858 · London's “Great Stink”** leads to Bazalgette's sewers.
- **1883 · The Brooklyn Bridge** opens.
- **1937 · The Golden Gate Bridge** opens.
- **1994 · The Channel Tunnel** opens.
- **2010 · The Burj Khalifa** is completed.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know civil engineering the way a strong first year of a university course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain how structures carry load:** tension, compression, arches, trusses, beams and cables.
- **Compare construction materials:** concrete, steel, timber and masonry.
- **Explain soils and foundations:** and why ground matters.
- **Explain water and transport systems:** supply, sanitation, drainage, roads and traffic.
- **Weigh construction, safety and sustainability:** failures, ethics, resilience and carbon.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Civil engineers and engineering educators from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published. Knowledge only: not a professional qualification.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages makes you a capable student of civil engineering. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Civil Engineering** certificate, you also complete:

1. **The grand quiz.** 40 game-style questions across all six stages. Pass mark 80%, with unlimited retakes.
2. **Structure problem.** Analyse loads on an unseen simple structure.
3. **Infrastructure plan.** Plan water, sanitation or transport for a described community.
4. **Capstone essay.** 2,500 words on one of the Stage 6 questions. Strong essays are read by an expert reviewer.
5. **Explain it.** Answer three unseen questions as if to a curious friend. For example: “How does a bridge hold itself up?”
