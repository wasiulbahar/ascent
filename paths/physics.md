# Physics

From the wonder of black holes and atoms to solving the problems physicists solve. About 300 to 400 hours over twelve to eighteen months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Physics has superb free material: whole university textbooks from OpenStax, MIT courses, and Feynman's own lectures. Physics is learned by doing, so from Stage 2 the quizzes include short problems with numbers. You can type the number alone, or with its units.

# About physics

Physics asks the most basic questions about the world: what things are made of, how they move, what light is, what time is. Its answers are written in mathematics, tested by experiment, and used everywhere, from your phone's GPS to the power station that charges it. The same few laws describe a falling apple, the orbit of the Moon and the glow of the Sun.

This path starts with physicists who write beautifully about big ideas, then builds the toolkit (algebra, calculus, Newton's laws), works through classical physics, relativity and the quantum, reaches the core of a university physics degree, and ends with the arguments about where physics is going.

**Trivia**
- Isaac Newton said he could see further only "by standing on the shoulders of giants".
- The Bengali physicist Satyendra Nath Bose worked out the statistics of light particles in 1924 while teaching at the University of Dhaka. Einstein translated his paper into German, and the particles called bosons are named after him.
- GPS satellites have to correct for both special and general relativity. Without the corrections, positions would drift by kilometres a day.
- Ibn al-Haytham, working in Cairo around 1020, argued that we see because light enters the eye, and tested his ideas with experiments.
- In 1918 Emmy Noether proved that every symmetry in nature comes with a conservation law: energy, momentum and charge are conserved because of symmetries.

### Timeline
- **c. 1021 · Book of Optics** Ibn al-Haytham's experimental study of light and vision.
- **1543 · Copernicus** The Sun, not the Earth, at the centre.
- **1687 · Principia** Newton's laws of motion and universal gravitation.
- **1865 · Maxwell** Electricity, magnetism and light united in four equations.
- **1905 · Einstein's miracle year** Special relativity, E = mc² and the light quantum.
- **1915 · General relativity** Gravity as the curvature of spacetime.
- **1924 · Bose** Bose's paper from Dhaka, the start of Bose–Einstein statistics.
- **1925–27 · Quantum mechanics** Heisenberg, Schrödinger and the uncertainty principle.
- **1964 · The Higgs mechanism** Proposed by several physicists; the Higgs boson is found at CERN in 2012.
- **2015 · Gravitational waves** First detected by LIGO, a century after Einstein predicted them.

# Before you start

Physics needs mathematics, and this path teaches it as you go. Here is the ladder, and when you need each rung:

- **Stages 1 and 2:** arithmetic, basic algebra and powers of ten. Khan Academy's free algebra course covers it.
- **Stage 3:** trigonometry, vectors and the basic ideas of calculus (taught in Stage 2).
- **Stage 4:** confident calculus, and complex numbers for the quantum.
- **Stages 5 and 6:** differential equations and linear algebra. Khan Academy and MIT OpenCourseWare teach both free.

Already know some physics? Take the **placement test** on this path's page to skip the stages you have mastered.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know physics the way a strong first and second year of a university physics degree expects. You will be able to:

- **Solve standard problems** in mechanics, electricity and magnetism, waves, thermodynamics, relativity and quantum mechanics.
- **Estimate** with orders of magnitude, and check an answer by its units.
- **Explain the big ideas** without equations: why the speed of light is a limit, what a quantum state is, why entropy increases.
- **Read a research summary** and explain what was found and how.
- **Understand the debates:** how physics changes (Kuhn), and whether today's theoretical physics has lost its way (Hossenfelder).
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Physicists from more than one specialism will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.

# Stage 4: Relativity and the Quantum
level: Connoisseur
intent: The two revolutions of twentieth-century physics.
gate: Score 70% or more across this stage's quizzes.

In the early 1900s classical physics broke in two places: at very high speeds, and at very small sizes. Einstein's relativity fixed the first, quantum mechanics the second. This stage gives you Einstein's own popular book, a free course that teaches physics the way physicists think, Feynman's account of light and matter, and the standard quantum mechanics textbook.

**Trivia**
- In 1924 Satyendra Nath Bose, a young lecturer at the University of Dhaka, sent Einstein a paper that derived Planck's law for light by counting photons in a new way. Einstein translated it into German and extended the idea; the result is Bose–Einstein statistics.
