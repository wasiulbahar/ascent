# Museums and Heritage

How societies keep and share their past: the history of museums, collecting and curating, conservation science, World Heritage and intangible heritage, repatriation and decolonising collections, heritage under threat, and digital museums. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Museums worldwide put collections, exhibitions and research online free, and UNESCO's heritage lists and conventions are free. The path covers museums and heritage on every continent, gives voice to source communities, and presents debates about repatriation and ownership fairly.

# About museums and heritage

Museum and heritage studies examines how societies collect, preserve, interpret and share objects, buildings, places, traditions and memories. It asks whose past is kept, who tells its story, and who it belongs to.

This path begins by visiting museums and heritage sites online, follows the history of museums, studies collecting, curating and conservation, explores World Heritage and intangible heritage, examines repatriation and decolonisation, and ends with heritage under threat and the digital future.

**Trivia**
- The Ashmolean Museum in Oxford, opened in 1683, is often called the world's first university museum.
- There are over 1,100 UNESCO World Heritage Sites in more than 160 countries.
- In 2001 the Taliban destroyed the 6th-century Buddhas of Bamiyan in Afghanistan.
- In 2021 Germany agreed to return many Benin Bronzes looted by British forces in 1897 to Nigeria.

### Timeline
- **c. 530 BCE · Princess Ennigaldi-Nanna's collection** in Ur is sometimes called the first museum.
- **1683 · The Ashmolean** opens in Oxford.
- **1753 · The British Museum** is founded.
- **1793 · The Louvre** opens to the public in revolutionary Paris.
- **1897 · British forces** loot the Benin Bronzes.
- **1954 · The Hague Convention** protects cultural property in war.
- **1970 · The UNESCO Convention** fights illicit trafficking of cultural property.
- **1972 · The World Heritage Convention** is adopted.
- **2003 · The Convention for the Safeguarding of Intangible Cultural Heritage** is adopted.
- **2018 · The Sarr–Savoy report** recommends returning African heritage from France.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know museums and heritage the way a strong first year of a university museum studies course expects. You will be able to:

- **Trace museum history:** from cabinets of curiosity to public and community museums.
- **Explain collecting, curating and conservation:** how objects are acquired, interpreted and preserved.
- **Explain World and intangible heritage:** UNESCO's lists and their critics.
- **Weigh repatriation and decolonisation:** with evidence from several sides.
- **Discuss heritage under threat and digital heritage:** war, climate, tourism and technology.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Museum professionals and heritage scholars from more than one region will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
