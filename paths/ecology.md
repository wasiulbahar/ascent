# Ecology and Conservation

How living things fit together: populations, communities and ecosystems, biodiversity and its loss, conservation's hard choices, rewilding and the debates over protecting nature. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free textbooks, global biodiversity reports and species databases cover the core. The path presents conservation debates fairly, including the views of Indigenous peoples and communities who live alongside wildlife.

# About ecology and conservation

Ecology studies how organisms interact with each other and with their environment, from single populations to whole ecosystems. Conservation biology applies ecology to protect species and habitats, and asks hard questions about how people and nature can share the planet.

This path begins with books that make ecology vivid, teaches populations and communities, explains ecosystems and biodiversity, confronts the causes of biodiversity loss, examines conservation in practice, and ends with rewilding and the big debates.

**Trivia**
- About 1.2 million species have been formally described, but estimates of the total range from several million to far more.
- Reintroducing wolves to Yellowstone in 1995 was widely credited with reshaping the park's ecosystem, though how much is still debated.
- The 2019 IPBES report warned that around one million species are threatened with extinction.
- In 2022 nearly 200 countries agreed the “30 by 30” goal: protecting 30% of land and sea by 2030.

### Timeline
- **1866 · Ernst Haeckel** coins the word “ecology”.
- **1935 · Arthur Tansley** introduces the term “ecosystem”.
- **1949 · *A Sand County Almanac*** Aldo Leopold's land ethic.
- **1962 · *Silent Spring*** warns of pesticides.
- **1964 · The IUCN Red List** of threatened species begins.
- **1967 · Island biogeography** MacArthur and Wilson.
- **1992 · The Convention on Biological Diversity** is signed at Rio.
- **1995 · Wolves** are reintroduced to Yellowstone.
- **2019 · The IPBES global assessment** warns of a million species threatened.
- **2022 · The Kunming–Montreal framework** sets the “30 by 30” goal.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know ecology and conservation the way a strong first year of a university course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain populations and communities:** growth, competition, predation and niches.
- **Explain ecosystems:** energy flow, nutrient cycles and food webs.
- **Measure biodiversity:** and explain why it matters.
- **Explain biodiversity loss:** habitat loss, overexploitation, invasive species, pollution and climate change.
- **Weigh conservation choices:** protected areas, rewilding, and people's rights and livelihoods.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Ecologists and conservation practitioners from more than one region will confirm them before the path is published.
