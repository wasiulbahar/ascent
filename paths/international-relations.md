# International relations

How states and peoples deal with each other: crises that nearly caused war, the great theories, the UN and international law, power and security, voices from the Global South, and the world order today. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Treaties, classic texts and a free theory textbook cover much of the path. It sets realist, liberal, constructivist, feminist and Global South views side by side. (The Geopolitics path complements this one with regional and strategic detail.)

# About international relations

International relations (IR) studies how states, international organisations, companies, movements and people interact across borders: war and peace, diplomacy, trade, international law, human rights and global problems such as climate change. It asks why wars happen, when cooperation works, and who makes the rules of world order.

This path begins with gripping accounts of crises and wars, teaches the main theories, studies the UN, international law and trade, examines power and security from several angles, listens to voices from the Global South, and ends with today's changing world order.

**Trivia**
- The Peace of Westphalia in 1648 is often called the origin of the modern system of sovereign states, though historians debate how much it really changed.
- The UN Security Council has five permanent members with a veto: China, France, Russia, the United Kingdom and the United States.
- The 1955 Bandung Conference in Indonesia brought together 29 newly independent and other Asian and African countries.
- Thucydides' account of the Peloponnesian War, written about 2,400 years ago, is still read in IR courses.

### Timeline
- **c. 431–404 BCE · The Peloponnesian War** recorded by Thucydides.
- **1648 · The Peace of Westphalia**
- **1795 · Kant's *Perpetual Peace***
- **1815 · The Congress of Vienna**
- **1919 · The League of Nations** is founded.
- **1945 · The United Nations** is founded.
- **1955 · The Bandung Conference**
- **1962 · The Cuban Missile Crisis**
- **1989–91 · The end of the Cold War**
- **1995 · The World Trade Organization** is founded.
- **2015 · The Paris Agreement** on climate change.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know international relations the way a strong first year of a university IR degree expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain the main theories:** realism, liberalism, constructivism, feminism and postcolonial views.
- **Explain institutions:** the UN, international law, trade rules and alliances.
- **Analyse crises and wars:** using several theories, not just one.
- **Include the whole world:** Global South perspectives and the history of decolonisation.
- **Weigh the debates:** hegemony, multipolarity, climate cooperation and the future of world order.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. IR scholars from more than one continent will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
