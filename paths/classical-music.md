# Classical Music

How to listen to and understand Western classical music and the world's great art-music traditions: from chant and Bach through Mozart, Beethoven and the Romantics to modern and living composers, with Hindustani, Carnatic, Persian, Arabic and Chinese classical music alongside. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Recordings stream free on many platforms, IMSLP offers free scores, and great courses are free to audit. The path centres listening, includes women and composers of colour long left out, and treats other classical traditions as equals, not exotic extras.

# About classical music

“Classical music” usually means the Western art-music tradition from the Middle Ages to today, written down in scores and performed by orchestras, choirs, soloists and chamber groups. Many cultures have their own classical traditions with centuries of theory and repertoire.

This path begins with ways into listening, follows Western music from chant to Bach, through Mozart and Beethoven, the Romantics and modernism, travels through classical traditions of South Asia, the Middle East and East Asia, and ends with living composers and how to write about music.

**Trivia**
- Johann Sebastian Bach wrote over 1,000 works; many were almost forgotten until Mendelssohn revived the *St Matthew Passion* in 1829.
- Beethoven was almost completely deaf when he wrote his Ninth Symphony, premiered in 1824.
- Hildegard of Bingen, a 12th-century abbess, is one of the earliest composers whose name and music survive.
- Hindustani and Carnatic music organise melody through ragas and rhythm through talas, with improvisation at their heart.

### Timeline
- **c. 600–900 · Gregorian chant** develops in Western Christian worship.
- **1150s · Hildegard of Bingen** composes chant.
- **1600 · Early opera** begins in Italy.
- **1721–50 · J. S. Bach** writes much of his greatest music in Leipzig.
- **1786–91 · Mozart** writes *Figaro*, *Don Giovanni* and *The Magic Flute*.
- **1824 · Beethoven's Ninth Symphony** premieres.
- **1874 · Verdi's Requiem** and the height of Romantic opera.
- **1913 · *The Rite of Spring*** shocks Paris.
- **1952 · John Cage's *4′33″*** challenges what music is.
- **1976 · Philip Glass's *Einstein on the Beach*** brings minimalism to opera.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know classical music the way a strong first year of a university music course expects. You will be able to:

- **Listen actively:** melody, harmony, rhythm, texture, form and timbre.
- **Trace Western music history:** medieval to contemporary, with key works.
- **Recognise major forms:** fugue, sonata form, symphony, concerto, opera.
- **Explore other classical traditions:** raga, maqam, dastgah and Chinese music.
- **Write about music:** a clear, evidence-based response to a performance or recording.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Musicians and musicologists from more than one tradition will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages makes you a capable listener and student of classical music. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Classical Music** certificate, you also complete:
