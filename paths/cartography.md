# Maps and Cartography

How humans picture the world: maps from ancient Babylon, China, the Islamic world and Polynesia, projections and their politics, surveying and the shape of the Earth, maps of empire and resistance, thematic maps and data, and GIS, satellites and digital mapping today. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Libraries such as the David Rumsey Map Collection share hundreds of thousands of historic maps free, and QGIS and OpenStreetMap are free tools. The path covers mapping traditions from many cultures and shows how maps carry power and point of view.

# About maps and cartography

Cartography is the art and science of making maps. Maps help us navigate, govern, trade and understand the world, but every map selects, simplifies and takes a point of view.

This path begins with how to read maps critically, travels through mapping traditions worldwide, studies projections and the shape of the Earth, examines maps of empire and resistance, explores thematic and data maps, and ends with GIS, satellites and digital mapping.

**Trivia**
- The Babylonian *Imago Mundi*, from about the 6th century BCE, is one of the oldest known world maps.
- Every flat map of the globe distorts something: area, shape, distance or direction.
- John Snow's 1854 map of cholera deaths in London helped found epidemiology.
- OpenStreetMap, started in 2004, is a free map of the world built by millions of volunteers.

### Timeline
- **c. 6th century BCE · The Babylonian *Imago Mundi*** maps the world.
- **c. 150 CE · Ptolemy's *Geography*** uses coordinates.
- **1154 · Al-Idrisi's *Tabula Rogeriana*** maps the known world for Roger II of Sicily.
- **1402 · The Korean *Kangnido*** maps Afro-Eurasia.
- **1569 · Mercator's projection** helps navigation.
- **1747–1815 · The Cassini survey** produces the first national map of France.
- **1802–1871 · The Great Trigonometrical Survey** maps India.
- **1854 · John Snow's cholera map** helps found epidemiology.
- **1972 · Landsat** begins imaging the Earth from space.
- **2004 · OpenStreetMap** begins.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know cartography the way a strong first year of a university geography or cartography course expects. You will be able to:

- **Read maps critically:** scale, projection, symbols and point of view.
- **Trace mapping history:** across Babylon, China, the Islamic world, Europe, Polynesia and beyond.
- **Explain projections:** what each distorts and why it matters.
- **Analyse maps of power:** empire, borders, resistance and counter-mapping.
- **Make maps:** thematic maps and simple GIS with free tools.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Cartographers and historians of cartography from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
