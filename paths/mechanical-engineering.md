# Mechanical Engineering

How machines are designed and made: forces and statics, strength of materials, dynamics and mechanisms, thermodynamics and engines, fluids and heat transfer, design, manufacturing and CAD, and sustainable engineering. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free university courses, open textbooks and free CAD tools cover the core. You need school algebra and some trigonometry; calculus helps from Stage 3. The path covers engineering in many countries and includes ethics and sustainability. **It is knowledge, not a professional qualification.**

# About mechanical engineering

Mechanical engineering designs and builds things that move or use energy: engines, cars, aircraft, turbines, robots, pumps, air conditioning and medical devices. It is one of the broadest and oldest engineering disciplines.

This path begins with how everyday machines work, teaches statics and the strength of materials, studies dynamics and mechanisms, explores thermodynamics, fluids and heat, covers design, manufacturing and CAD, and ends with sustainable engineering and the profession.

**Trivia**
- The Antikythera mechanism, a Greek geared calculating device from around 100 BCE, is often called the first analogue computer.
- James Watt's separate condenser, patented in 1769, made steam engines far more efficient.
- A modern jet engine's turbine blades work in gases hotter than the melting point of the metal they are made from, kept cool by air channels and coatings.
- Al-Jazari described over 50 mechanical devices, including water clocks and automata, in 1206.

### Timeline
- **c. 250 BCE · Archimedes** explains levers and pulleys.
- **c. 100 BCE · The Antikythera mechanism** models the heavens with gears.
- **1206 · Al-Jazari** describes ingenious machines.
- **1769 · James Watt** patents the separate condenser.
- **1824 · Sadi Carnot** founds the theory of heat engines.
- **1847 · The Institution of Mechanical Engineers** is founded.
- **1876 · Nikolaus Otto** builds a four-stroke engine.
- **1913 · Ford's moving assembly line** transforms manufacturing.
- **1960s · Computer-aided design** begins.
- **1980s · 3D printing** is invented.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know mechanical engineering the way a strong first year of a university course expects. You will be able to:

- **Analyse forces:** free-body diagrams, equilibrium and simple structures.
- **Predict strength and failure:** stress, strain, bending and fatigue.
- **Analyse motion and machines:** kinematics, dynamics, gears and mechanisms.
- **Apply thermodynamics and fluids:** energy, engines, efficiency, pumps and heat transfer.
- **Design and make:** the design process, CAD, manufacturing, ethics and sustainability.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Mechanical engineers and engineering educators from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published. Knowledge only: not a professional qualification.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
