# Child Development

How children grow from birth to adolescence: the developing brain and body, nutrition, thinking and language, attachment, emotions and play, adversity and resilience, screens, and children's rights and policies that help them thrive. About 150 to 180 hours over six to eight months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free resources from Harvard's Center on the Developing Child, the WHO, UNICEF and open textbooks cover the core. No background is needed. The path draws on research from many cultures and is for understanding, not diagnosis; talk to a health worker about a particular child's development.

# About child development

Child development studies how children change physically, mentally, emotionally and socially from birth through adolescence, and what helps them flourish.

This path begins with how babies learn and the basics of healthy growth, explores the developing brain and nutrition, studies thinking, language and understanding others, examines attachment, emotions and play, looks at adversity and resilience, and ends with adolescence, children's rights and policy.

**Trivia**
- In the first few years of life, the brain forms more than a million new neural connections every second.
- Babies can tell apart the sounds of all the world's languages, but by about their first birthday they specialise in the languages around them.
- The UN Convention on the Rights of the Child is the most widely ratified human rights treaty in history.
- Charles Darwin published notes on his own baby son's development in 1877.

### Timeline
- **1877 · Charles Darwin** publishes a sketch of an infant's development.
- **1920s · Jean Piaget** begins studying how children think.
- **1930s · Lev Vygotsky** emphasises learning through social interaction.
- **1951 · John Bowlby** reports to the WHO on maternal care and mental health.
- **1970s · Mary Ainsworth** studies attachment with the Strange Situation.
- **1989 · The UN** adopts the Convention on the Rights of the Child.
- **1998 · The ACE study** links childhood adversity to adult health.
- **2000 · The Bucharest Early Intervention Project** begins studying children raised in institutions.
- **2016 · *The Lancet*** publishes a major series on early childhood development.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will understand child development the way a strong introductory university course expects. You will be able to:

- **Describe development:** milestones, growth and the developing brain.
- **Explain thinking and language:** Piaget, Vygotsky, language learning and theory of mind.
- **Explain relationships and emotions:** attachment, temperament, self-regulation and play.
- **Understand adversity and resilience:** risks, protective factors and early intervention.
- **Connect research to policy:** children's rights, parenting support and adolescence.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Developmental psychologists, paediatricians and educators from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages gives you a strong understanding of child development. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Child Development** certificate, you also complete:

1. **The grand quiz.** 40 game-style questions across all six stages. Pass mark 80%, with unlimited retakes.

3. **Policy brief.** Write a 1,500-word brief on improving early childhood support in your community.
4. **Capstone essay.** 2,500 words on one of the Stage 6 questions. Strong essays are read by an expert reviewer.
