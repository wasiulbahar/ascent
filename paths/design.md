# Design

How designers shape the things, images and screens around us: typography and layout, design thinking, product design and the Bauhaus, digital and user-experience design, the ethics of design, and its future. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free guides on design thinking, usability and accessibility cover much of the path. Design is learned by doing: every stage asks you to sketch, make and test. Designers from many countries are included.

# About design

Design is the practice of planning things that people use and see: objects, posters, books, signs, websites, apps, services and systems. Good design solves real problems for real people, works well, is understandable, and can be beautiful. It draws on art, engineering, psychology and business.

This path begins with books that show what design is, teaches the basics of graphic design and design thinking, covers product and industrial design and the Bauhaus, moves to digital and user-experience design, reads the big ideas and critics of design, and ends with the future: sustainability, speculative design and ethics.

**Trivia**
- The Bauhaus, founded in Weimar in 1919 by Walter Gropius, lasted only fourteen years but shaped design worldwide.
- Dieter Rams's ten principles of good design end with: good design is as little design as possible.
- The “dark patterns” that trick users online were named by the designer Harry Brignull in 2010.
- Herbert Simon wrote that everyone designs who devises courses of action aimed at changing existing situations into preferred ones.

### Timeline
- **1851 · The Great Exhibition** in London shows industrial products from around the world.
- **1919 · The Bauhaus** is founded.
- **1930s · Streamlining** Raymond Loewy and industrial design in America.
- **1950s–60s · Swiss Style** Grids and clean typography.
- **1969 · Simon's *The Sciences of the Artificial***
- **1971 · Papanek's *Design for the Real World***
- **1980s · Dieter Rams's ten principles**
- **1988 · Norman's *The Design of Everyday Things***
- **2007 · The iPhone** Touchscreen interaction for the mass market.
- **2010s · Design thinking** spreads to business and government.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know design the way a strong first year of a university design course expects. You will be able to:

- **Use the basics:** type, layout, colour, hierarchy and grids.
- **Run a design process:** research, define, ideate, prototype and test.
- **Analyse products:** form, function, materials and use.
- **Design for screens:** usability, accessibility and user-experience principles.
- **Weigh the ethics:** who design serves, deceptive design, and sustainability.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Designers and design educators from more than one continent will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages makes you a capable student of design. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Design** certificate, you also complete:

1. **The grand quiz.** 40 game-style questions across all six stages. Pass mark 80%, with unlimited retakes.
2. **Design project.** Take a small real problem through research, ideas, a prototype and a test with users, and document it.
3. **Critique.** Analyse an unseen design: who it serves, how it works and how it could be better.
4. **Capstone essay.** 2,500 words on one of the Stage 6 questions. Strong essays are read by an expert reviewer.
5. **Explain it.** Answer three unseen questions as if to a curious friend. For example: “Why are some doors so confusing?”

The certificate needs every stage passed.
