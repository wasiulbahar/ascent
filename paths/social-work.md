# Social Work

How societies support people facing hardship: the history and values of social work, theories and methods, working with children and families, mental health, ageing and disability, community work and social policy, and ethics, rights and practice across the world. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Professional bodies, governments and international organisations publish much free material. The path covers social work in many countries and welfare systems, centres the voices of people who use services, and presents debates fairly. **It is knowledge only: not a professional qualification, and not advice for anyone in crisis.**

# About social work

Social work is a profession and academic discipline that promotes social change, problem solving and the wellbeing of people and communities. Social workers support children and families, people with mental illness or disabilities, older people, refugees and many others, and work for social justice.

This path begins with the profession's history and values, teaches theories and methods, studies work with children and families, explores mental health, ageing and disability, examines community work and social policy, and ends with ethics, rights and global social work.

**Trivia**
- Jane Addams co-founded Hull House in Chicago in 1889 and later won the Nobel Peace Prize (1931).
- The International Federation of Social Workers represents social workers in over 140 countries.
- The global definition of social work, agreed in 2014, centres social justice, human rights, collective responsibility and respect for diversities.
- Mary Richmond's *Social Diagnosis* (1917) was one of the first textbooks of casework.

### Timeline
- **1869 · The Charity Organisation Society** is founded in London.
- **1884 · Toynbee Hall** starts the settlement house movement.
- **1889 · Jane Addams and Ellen Gates Starr** found Hull House.
- **1917 · Mary Richmond's *Social Diagnosis*** sets out casework.
- **1928 · The International Federation of Social Workers** is founded (as a secretariat).
- **1936 · India's first school of social work** opens in Mumbai (now TISS).
- **1942 · The Beveridge Report** plans the UK welfare state.
- **1989 · The UN Convention on the Rights of the Child** is adopted.
- **2006 · The UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities** is adopted.
- **2014 · The global definition of social work** is agreed.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know social work the way a strong first year of a university social work course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain social work's purpose and values:** social justice, human rights and dignity.
- **Use theories and methods:** systems, strengths-based, person-centred and anti-oppressive practice.
- **Explain safeguarding and family support:** and the evidence about what works.
- **Describe practice areas:** mental health, ageing, disability, migration and communities.
- **Weigh ethics and policy:** dilemmas, welfare states and global differences.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Social workers, academics and people who use services from more than one country will review the path before it is published. Knowledge only: not a qualification or crisis advice.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages makes you a capable student of social work. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Social Work** certificate, you also complete:

1. **The grand quiz.** 40 game-style questions across all six stages. Pass mark 80%, with unlimited retakes.
2. **Case analysis.** Analyse an unseen case using theory, ethics and the person's strengths.
3. **Policy brief.** Propose a change to one social policy, with evidence.
4. **Capstone essay.** 2,500 words on one of the Stage 6 questions. Strong essays are read by an expert reviewer.
5. **Explain it.** Answer three unseen questions as if to a curious friend. For example: “What do social workers actually do?”
