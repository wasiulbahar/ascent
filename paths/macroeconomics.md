# Macroeconomics

How whole economies work: output and growth, money and inflation, central banks, unemployment and the business cycle, fiscal policy, financial crises, and exchange rates, debt and inequality. About 180 to 220 hours over seven to nine months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free courses, central bank explainers, IMF articles and data sites cover the core. You need school algebra and comfort with graphs; the Economics path gives the wider picture. The path presents competing schools of thought and uses examples from economies around the world.

# About macroeconomics

Macroeconomics studies the economy as a whole: total output, growth, jobs, prices, interest rates, exchange rates and government budgets. It asks why some countries grow rich, why recessions happen, and what governments and central banks can do.

This path starts with accessible introductions and data, studies GDP, growth and development, explains money, inflation and central banks, covers unemployment, cycles and fiscal policy, examines financial crises, and ends with exchange rates, debt, inequality and current research.

**Trivia**
- The modern idea of GDP was developed in the 1930s, with Simon Kuznets producing early US national income estimates.
- Zimbabwe issued a 100 trillion dollar banknote in 2009 during hyperinflation.
- The Bank of England explained in 2014 that most money is created when commercial banks make loans.
- The economist Bill Phillips built a hydraulic computer, the MONIAC, that modelled the economy with flowing water.

### Timeline
- **1776 · Adam Smith** publishes *The Wealth of Nations*.
- **1929 · The Wall Street Crash** begins the Great Depression.
- **1936 · John Maynard Keynes** publishes *The General Theory*.
- **1944 · The Bretton Woods conference** creates the IMF and World Bank.
- **1956 · Robert Solow** publishes his growth model.
- **1958 · Bill Phillips** links unemployment and wage inflation.
- **1971 · The United States** ends the dollar's convertibility into gold.
- **1990 · New Zealand** introduces formal inflation targeting.
- **1997 · The Asian financial crisis** spreads across East Asia.
- **2008 · The global financial crisis** triggers the Great Recession.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will understand macroeconomics the way a strong intermediate university course expects. You will be able to:

- **Measure economies:** GDP, growth, inflation and unemployment, and their limits.
- **Explain growth:** capital, technology, institutions and development.
- **Explain money and central banks:** how money is created, inflation and interest rate policy.
- **Explain cycles and policy:** recessions, fiscal policy, multipliers and debt.
- **Analyse crises and the world economy:** banking crises, exchange rates and inequality.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Economists and economics educators from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages gives you a strong understanding of macroeconomics. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Macroeconomics** certificate, you also complete:

1. **The grand quiz.** 40 game-style questions across all six stages. Pass mark 80%, with unlimited retakes.
