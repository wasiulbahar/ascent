# Environmental science and climate

From how the Earth system works to the evidence on climate and nature, the solutions, and the arguments over who should act and how. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. The key scientific reports, from the IPCC, IPBES, WHO and the International Energy Agency, are free. The path gives the scientific consensus and the real debates about solutions, from more than one point of view.

# About environmental science and climate

Environmental science studies how the living and non-living parts of the Earth work together: air, water, soil, climate and life. Climate science is one of its central parts. It explains why the planet is warming, what that does to people and nature, and what could be done about it.

This path begins with books that started and shaped the environmental movement, teaches how the Earth system works, reads the evidence on climate, biodiversity and pollution, weighs the solutions, takes on the economics, politics and justice of who acts, and ends with the frontier: planetary boundaries, the history of the science, and current research.

**Trivia**
- In 1896 the Swedish chemist Svante Arrhenius calculated that doubling carbon dioxide in the air would warm the Earth by several degrees.
- Charles David Keeling began measuring carbon dioxide on Mauna Loa, Hawaii, in 1958; the rising “Keeling curve” is one of science's most famous graphs.
- The 1987 Montreal Protocol, which phased out chemicals destroying the ozone layer, was the first UN treaty ratified by every country in the world.
- About a million animal and plant species are threatened with extinction, according to the 2019 global assessment by IPBES.

### Timeline
- **1896 · Arrhenius** Calculates warming from carbon dioxide.
- **1958 · The Keeling curve** begins at Mauna Loa.
- **1962 · *Silent Spring*** helps start the modern environmental movement.
- **1972 · Stockholm** The first UN conference on the human environment.
- **1987 · The Montreal Protocol** protects the ozone layer.
- **1988 · The IPCC** is founded.
- **1992 · The Rio Earth Summit** creates the climate and biodiversity conventions.
- **2015 · The Paris Agreement** aims to keep warming well below 2 °C.
- **2023 · IPCC Sixth Assessment** synthesis report.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know environmental science and climate the way a strong first year of a university environmental-science degree expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain the Earth system:** energy, carbon and water cycles, ecosystems and the greenhouse effect.
- **Read the evidence:** what the IPCC, IPBES and WHO reports say, and how sure they are.
- **Compare solutions:** energy, food, land and adaptation, with their costs and limits.
- **Weigh the politics:** markets, commons, treaties, and who should pay.
- **Use the numbers:** emissions per person and per country, and the scale of the changes needed.
