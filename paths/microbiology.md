# Microbiology

The invisible majority of life: bacteria, viruses, fungi and other microbes, how they were discovered, how they cause and prevent disease, the microbiome, vaccines and antibiotics, and the pandemics that shaped history. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free textbooks, public health agencies and courses cover the core. The path explains vaccine science with evidence, presents real scientific uncertainties honestly, and gives weight to the global burden of infectious disease. **It is not medical advice.**

# About microbiology

Microbiology studies microorganisms: bacteria, archaea, viruses, fungi and protists, most too small to see without a microscope. Microbes were the first life on Earth, recycle nutrients, live inside us in vast numbers, cause infectious diseases and give us medicines, foods and tools such as CRISPR.

This path begins with lively books on the microbial world, teaches the basics of microbes and how they were discovered, explains infection and immunity, explores the microbiome and microbial ecology, examines vaccines, antibiotics and resistance, and ends with pandemics and emerging diseases.

**Trivia**
- Antonie van Leeuwenhoek was the first to describe bacteria, in the 1670s, using microscopes he built himself.
- A human body contains roughly as many bacterial cells as human cells.
- Smallpox, which killed hundreds of millions in the 20th century alone, was declared eradicated in 1980.
- Alexander Fleming noticed in 1928 that a mould, Penicillium, killed bacteria on a plate he had left out.

### Timeline
- **1670s · Van Leeuwenhoek** first describes bacteria.
- **1796 · Edward Jenner** tests a smallpox vaccine using cowpox.
- **1854 · John Snow** traces cholera to a water pump in London.
- **1860s · Louis Pasteur** helps establish germ theory.
- **1882 · Robert Koch** identifies the tuberculosis bacterium.
- **1928 · Fleming** discovers penicillin.
- **1977 · Carl Woese** identifies archaea as a separate domain of life.
- **1980 · Smallpox** is declared eradicated.
- **1983 · HIV** is identified as the cause of AIDS.
- **2020 · mRNA vaccines** against COVID-19 are authorised.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know microbiology the way a strong first year of a university microbiology course expects. You will be able to:

- **Describe microbes:** bacteria, archaea, viruses, fungi and protists, and how they differ.
- **Explain infection and immunity:** how microbes cause disease and how the body defends itself.
- **Explain the microbiome:** and microbes' roles in ecosystems.
- **Explain vaccines and antibiotics:** how they work, and why resistance evolves.
- **Read outbreaks:** how diseases spread and how public health responds.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Microbiologists and infectious-disease specialists from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published. Knowledge only: not medical advice.
