# Mobile App Development

How to build apps for phones: Android and iOS and cross-platform tools, mobile design and accessibility, apps that work offline and on cheap phones, data and architecture, testing, release and privacy, and the mobile economy from app stores to mobile money. About 180 to 220 hours over seven to nine months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Official courses and documentation from Google and Apple are free. Android development works on any computer; building iOS apps needs a Mac, so Android and cross-platform tools are taught first. Publishing to app stores involves fees. The path designs for the full range of phones people actually use worldwide.

# About mobile app development

Mobile app development is building software for smartphones and tablets. Apps must fit small screens, touch input, limited batteries, unreliable networks and a huge range of devices, while respecting privacy.

This path starts with mobile-first thinking and beginner courses, teaches the main platforms and languages, covers mobile design, accessibility and designing for low-cost phones, moves to data, architecture and back-ends, then testing, release, privacy and performance, and ends with the mobile economy and research.

**Trivia**
- The first handheld mobile phone call was made by Martin Cooper of Motorola in 1973.
- IBM's Simon, sold in 1994, is often called the first smartphone; it had a touchscreen and apps.
- Apple's App Store opened in 2008 with around 500 apps.
- M-Pesa, launched in Kenya in 2007, let people send money by text message and spread mobile money across Africa.

### Timeline
- **1973 · Martin Cooper** makes the first handheld mobile phone call.
- **1994 · IBM Simon** goes on sale as an early smartphone.
- **2007 · The iPhone** is launched; M-Pesa starts in Kenya.
- **2008 · Apple's App Store and the first Android phone** launch.
- **2014 · Apple** introduces the Swift language.
- **2017 · Google** makes Kotlin an official Android language.
- **2018 · Flutter 1.0** is released for cross-platform apps.
- **2021 · Jetpack Compose** becomes stable; Apple introduces App Tracking Transparency.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will build apps the way a capable junior mobile developer does. You will be able to:

- **Build native apps:** for Android with Kotlin and Compose, and understand iOS with Swift.
- **Build cross-platform apps:** with Flutter or React Native, and choose wisely between approaches.
- **Design for mobile:** platform guidelines, accessibility and low-cost devices.
- **Handle data:** local storage, offline use, APIs and clean architecture.
- **Ship responsibly:** testing, store release, privacy, performance and battery use.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Mobile developers and computer science educators from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages makes you a capable mobile developer. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Mobile App Development** certificate, you also complete:

1. **The grand quiz.** 40 game-style questions across all six stages. Pass mark 80%, with unlimited retakes.
2. **App project.** Build and test a complete app with offline data, accessibility and a clear privacy policy.
3. **Release plan.** Prepare store listing, screenshots and a testing and release checklist.
4. **Capstone report.** 2,500 words on one of the Stage 6 questions. Strong reports are read by an expert reviewer.
5. **Explain it.** Answer three unseen questions as if to a client. For example: “Should we build for Android, iOS or both?”

The certificate needs every stage passed.
