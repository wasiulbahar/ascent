# Materials Science

Why materials behave as they do and how to design new ones: atoms, bonds and crystals, metals, ceramics, polymers and composites, how materials fail, semiconductors and nanomaterials, and sustainable materials for the future. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free university courses, open textbooks and materials databases cover the core. The path draws on materials from many cultures and eras, from ancient steel to graphene, and presents debates about mining, recycling and sustainability fairly.

# About materials science

Materials science studies how the structure of materials, from atoms to grains, determines their properties, and how to process and design materials for uses from bridges and batteries to phones and implants.

This path begins with books that make materials vivid, teaches atomic structure and bonding, studies the main classes of materials, explains mechanical properties and failure, explores electronic, optical and nano materials, and ends with sustainability and materials of the future.

**Trivia**
- Archaeologists name whole eras after materials: the Stone, Bronze and Iron Ages.
- Wootz steel, made in South India from around the 6th century BCE, was famed as the material of Damascus blades.
- Graphene, a single layer of carbon atoms, was isolated in 2004 using sticky tape; its discoverers won the 2010 Nobel Prize in Physics.
- Concrete is the most widely used human-made material, and cement production causes about 8% of global CO₂ emissions.

### Timeline
- **c. 3300 BCE · The Bronze Age** begins in the Near East.
- **c. 1200 BCE · The Iron Age** spreads.
- **c. 6th century BCE · Wootz steel** is made in South India.
- **1856 · The Bessemer process** makes cheap steel.
- **1907 · Bakelite** the first fully synthetic plastic.
- **1912 · X-ray diffraction** reveals crystal structures.
- **1947 · The transistor** is invented using semiconductors.
- **1986 · High-temperature superconductors** are discovered.
- **2004 · Graphene** is isolated.
- **2019 · The Nobel Prize in Chemistry** honours lithium-ion batteries.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know materials science the way a strong first year of a university materials course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain structure and bonding:** atoms, bonds, crystals and defects.
- **Compare material classes:** metals, ceramics, polymers, composites and semiconductors.
- **Explain mechanical behaviour:** stress, strain, strength, toughness and failure.
- **Explain functional materials:** electronic, magnetic, optical and nano materials.
- **Weigh sustainability:** mining, recycling, embodied carbon and new materials.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Materials scientists and engineers from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
