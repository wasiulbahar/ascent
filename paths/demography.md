# Demography

The science of populations: how births, deaths and migration shape societies, how demographers measure them, why families are getting smaller, how life expectancy rose, migration and cities, and the big debates about population, ageing, the economy and the planet. About 120 to 150 hours over five to six months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free UN, World Bank and research data and tools cover nearly everything. You need school maths; spreadsheets help. The path uses data from every region and treats population questions without alarmism, weighing human rights alongside numbers.

# About demography

Demography studies the size, structure and change of human populations through births, deaths and migration. It underpins planning for schools, pensions, health, housing and the environment.

This path starts with the big picture of population change, teaches life tables, fertility rates and censuses, explores why fertility falls and how policies try to change it, studies mortality and health transitions, covers migration, refugees and cities, and ends with debates about Malthus, demographic dividends, ageing and the planet.

**Trivia**
- The world's population reached 8 billion on 15 November 2022, according to the UN.
- India overtook China as the world's most populous country in 2023.
- South Korea has the lowest fertility rate in the world, well under one child per woman.
- Edmond Halley, of comet fame, published one of the first life tables in 1693.

### Timeline
- **1662 · John Graunt** analyses London's bills of mortality.
- **1693 · Edmond Halley** publishes a life table.
- **1790 · The United States** holds its first census.
- **1798 · Thomas Malthus** publishes his essay on population.
- **1945 · Frank Notestein** describes the demographic transition.
- **1971 · Abdel Omran** proposes the epidemiological transition.
- **1980 · China** begins its one-child policy.
- **1994 · The Cairo population conference** puts reproductive rights at the centre of policy.
- **2022 · The world population** reaches 8 billion.
- **2023 · India** becomes the most populous country.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will understand demography the way a strong introductory course expects. You will be able to:

- **Measure populations:** rates, life tables, fertility, population pyramids and censuses.
- **Explain transitions:** demographic and epidemiological transitions across the world.
- **Analyse fertility:** why it falls, what policies do, and their human rights limits.
- **Analyse migration:** migrants, refugees and urbanisation.
- **Weigh big debates:** Malthus, the demographic dividend, ageing and population and environment.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Demographers and population researchers from more than one region will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
