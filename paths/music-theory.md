# Music theory

How music works: from rhythm, scales and chords to harmony and form, the theories of the world's musical traditions, jazz and popular music, and the science of listening. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Excellent free lessons, ear-training tools and scores cover the basics. The path treats European, Indian, Arabic, African and other traditions as theories in their own right. An instrument helps but is not required.

# About music theory

Music theory explains how music is built and why it affects us: rhythm and metre, scales and modes, melody, harmony, texture and form. Every musical tradition has its own theory, from Western harmony to Indian raga, Arabic maqam and the interlocking rhythms of West Africa.

This path begins with books on why music moves us, teaches the basics with free tools, covers harmony and form in the Western tradition, studies the theories of other great traditions, moves to modern, jazz and popular music, and ends with the frontier: the mind, mathematics and technology of music.

**Trivia**
- Pythagoras is said to have found that strings whose lengths form simple ratios, like 2:1 for an octave, sound consonant together.
- An Indian raga is not just a scale: it includes characteristic phrases, ornaments and a mood, and many are linked to a time of day.
- Arabic maqam music uses intervals between the semitones of a piano, often called quarter tones.
- The flutes from Geissenklösterle in Germany, made of bird bone and mammoth ivory about 40,000 years ago, are among the oldest known musical instruments.

### Timeline
- **c. 40,000 years ago · Bone and ivory flutes** Among the oldest known instruments.
- **c. 6th century BCE · Pythagoras** Ratios and consonance, as tradition records.
- **c. 200 BCE–200 CE · The *Natyashastra*** An Indian treatise including music theory.
- **9th–10th century · Al-Kindi and al-Farabi** Arabic writings on music theory.
- **c. 1025 · Guido of Arezzo** Staff notation and solmisation.
- **1722 · Rameau's *Treatise on Harmony***
- **1722 · Bach's *Well-Tempered Clavier*, Book 1**
- **1908–09 · Schoenberg** Atonal music.
- **1940s · Bebop** Jazz harmony grows more complex.
- **1980s · MIDI and digital audio** change how music is made.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know music theory the way a strong first year of a university music degree expects. You will be able to:

- **Read and hear the basics:** notation, rhythm, metre, scales, intervals and chords.
- **Analyse harmony and form:** cadences, chord progressions, phrases and common forms.
- **Explain other traditions:** raga, maqam, gamelan and African rhythm on their own terms.
- **Analyse modern, jazz and popular music:** extended harmony, modes and song form.
- **Explain why music moves us:** expectation, the brain and the mathematics of sound.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Music theorists and performers from several traditions will confirm them before the path is published.
