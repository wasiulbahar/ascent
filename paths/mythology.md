# Mythology and folklore

The stories humans have told about gods, heroes, monsters and the origins of the world: ancient myths, epics from every continent, folk and fairy tales, the theories that explain them, and myth in modern stories. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Many ancient texts and folk-tale collections are free. The path gives the myths of Africa, Asia, the Americas, Oceania and Europe equal weight, and treats living traditions, such as Aboriginal Dreaming stories and Hindu epics, with respect for the communities who hold them.

# About mythology and folklore

Mythology studies the sacred and traditional stories of peoples: how the world began, the deeds of gods and heroes, the origins of customs and the meaning of death. Folklore studies the living traditions of ordinary people: folk tales, legends, songs, customs and beliefs, including modern urban legends.

This path begins with great retellings and a famous television series on myth, reads ancient myths and epics, travels to traditions on every continent, studies folk and fairy tales, reads the theories that explain myth, and ends with myth in modern novels and urban legends.

**Trivia**
- The *Epic of Gilgamesh*, from ancient Mesopotamia, includes a flood story older than the Bible's and is among the oldest surviving works of literature.
- The *Mahabharata* is around ten times the length of the *Iliad* and *Odyssey* combined.
- The Brothers Grimm collected and published folk tales, including “Cinderella” and “Hansel and Gretel”, from 1812.
- Vladimir Propp found that Russian fairy tales are built from the same 31 “functions”, such as the hero leaving home.

### Timeline
- **c. 2100 BCE onwards · The *Epic of Gilgamesh*** in Mesopotamia.
- **c. 8th century BCE · Homer and Hesiod** write down Greek myth.
- **c. 400 BCE–400 CE · The *Mahabharata* and *Ramayana*** take shape in India.
- **712 · The *Kojiki*** records Japanese myth.
- **13th century · The *Prose Edda*** records Norse myth.
- **13th century onwards · The epic of Sundiata** is told by griots in West Africa.
- **1812 · The Brothers Grimm** publish their tales.
- **1835 · The *Kalevala*** Finland's national epic.
- **1890 · Frazer's *The Golden Bough***
- **1928 · Propp's *Morphology of the Folktale***

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know mythology and folklore the way a strong first year of a university mythology or folklore course expects. You will be able to:

- **Know the great myths:** from Mesopotamia, Egypt, Greece, India, the Norse world and more.
- **Read epics from many traditions:** Gilgamesh, the Indian epics, Sundiata, the *Kalevala* and others.
- **Analyse folk and fairy tales:** their structures, types and meanings.
- **Use the theories:** ritual, structuralism, the hero's journey, and their critics.
- **Trace myth today:** in novels, films and urban legends.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Scholars of myth and folklore from more than one continent, and members of living traditions, will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
