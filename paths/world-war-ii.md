# World War II

The deadliest war in history, told from every front: its causes, the war in Europe, Asia and the Pacific, Africa and the home fronts, the Holocaust and other atrocities, and how the war remade the world and is remembered. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Museums such as the US Holocaust Memorial Museum and the Imperial War Museums, and many archives, are free online. The path tells the war from many countries, including China, the Soviet Union, India, Africa and occupied peoples, not only the Western allies. Some material describes atrocities directly.

# About the Second World War

The Second World War, from 1939 to 1945 (from 1937 in China), was fought across Europe, Asia, Africa and the oceans, and killed an estimated 70 to 85 million people, most of them civilians. It included the Holocaust, the genocide of six million Jews, and ended with the first use of atomic bombs.

This path begins with gripping accounts of the war, explains its causes, follows the fighting in Europe and Asia, confronts the Holocaust and other atrocities, explores home fronts, empires and resistance, and ends with the war's legacies and memory.

**Trivia**
- The Soviet Union lost an estimated 24 to 27 million people, more than any other country.
- China's war with Japan began in 1937, two years before war broke out in Europe.
- About 2.5 million Indians served in the British Indian Army, the largest volunteer army in history.
- The Battle of Stalingrad, 1942–43, was among the bloodiest battles ever fought.

### Timeline
- **1931 · Japan** invades Manchuria.
- **1933 · Hitler** comes to power in Germany.
- **1937 · Japan** begins full-scale war in China; the Nanjing massacre.
- **1939 · Germany** invades Poland; Britain and France declare war.
- **1941 · Germany invades the Soviet Union;** Japan attacks Pearl Harbor.
- **1942 · The Wannsee Conference** coordinates the Holocaust.
- **1942–43 · Stalingrad** and El Alamein turn the tide.
- **1944 · D-Day** Allied landings in Normandy.
- **1945 · Germany surrenders in May;** atomic bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki; Japan surrenders.
- **1945–46 · The Nuremberg trials** try Nazi leaders.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know the Second World War the way a strong first year of a university history course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain the causes:** from the First World War's aftermath to Nazi and Japanese expansion.
- **Follow the war on every front:** Europe, the Soviet Union, Asia, the Pacific and Africa.
- **Explain the Holocaust:** how it happened and how it was carried out.
- **Explain home fronts and empires:** civilians, colonies, resistance and collaboration.
- **Weigh legacies and memory:** the postwar order and how countries remember the war.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Historians of the war from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
