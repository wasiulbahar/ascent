# Oceans

The ocean that covers most of our planet: how it works, its currents and its role in climate, the extraordinary life within it, fishing and pollution, and how the high seas are governed. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free textbooks, NOAA and UN resources, and global data cover much of the path. The path includes coastal and island communities around the world whose lives depend on the sea.

# About the oceans

The ocean covers about 71% of Earth's surface and holds about 97% of its water. Ocean science, or oceanography, studies its physics (currents, waves and tides), chemistry, geology and life, and its huge role in climate: it has absorbed most of the extra heat and about a quarter of the carbon dioxide from human activity. Billions of people rely on the sea for food and livelihoods.

This path begins with books and films that reveal the ocean's wonder, teaches ocean science, explores currents, climate and coasts, dives into marine life, examines fishing and pollution, and ends with how the ocean is governed and protected.

**Trivia**
- Only a few dozen people have ever visited the deepest point of the ocean, the Challenger Deep, nearly 11 kilometres down.
- The ocean has absorbed over 90% of the extra heat trapped by greenhouse gases since the 1970s.
- In 1992 thousands of plastic bath toys fell from a container ship in the Pacific, and their drift helped scientists track ocean currents.
- The 2023 High Seas Treaty is the first to protect marine life in the ocean beyond any country's control, about two-thirds of the ocean.

### Timeline
- **c. 1500 BCE onwards · Polynesian navigators** settle the Pacific using waves, stars and birds.
- **1872–76 · HMS *Challenger*** The first global oceanographic expedition.
- **1912 · Wegener** proposes continental drift.
- **1960 · The *Trieste*** reaches the Challenger Deep.
- **1977 · Hydrothermal vents** and their ecosystems are discovered.
- **1982 · UNCLOS** The Law of the Sea treaty is signed.
- **2010 · The Census of Marine Life** is completed.
- **2021–2030 · The UN Ocean Decade**
- **2023 · The High Seas Treaty** is agreed.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know ocean science the way a strong first year of a university oceanography or marine-science course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain how the ocean works:** currents, tides, waves, chemistry and the sea floor.
- **Explain the ocean and climate:** heat, carbon, acidification and sea-level rise.
- **Describe marine life:** from plankton and coral reefs to whales and the deep sea.
- **Analyse human impacts:** fishing, plastic, pollution and coastal change.
- **Weigh ocean governance:** the Law of the Sea, the High Seas Treaty and protected areas.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Ocean scientists and coastal-community experts from more than one continent will confirm them before the path is published.
