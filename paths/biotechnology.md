# Biotechnology

How living cells and molecules are used to make things: the core tools from recombinant DNA to CRISPR, medicines made by cells, crops and the environment, industrial and synthetic biology, and the ethics, regulation and biosecurity that govern it all. About 200 to 250 hours over eight to ten months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free Nobel materials, scientific reports and learning resources cover the core. You need school biology and chemistry; the Biology, Genetics and Biochemistry paths help. The path presents debates such as genetically modified crops by weighing the evidence, and it never covers hands-on lab protocols outside supervised settings.

# About biotechnology

Biotechnology uses living systems, from microbes to enzymes and DNA, to make products and solve problems. It brews beer and bakes bread, but also makes insulin, vaccines, cancer drugs, pest-resistant crops, enzymes for washing powder and biofuels.

This path begins with what biotechnology is and how students engineer biology, teaches the core tools, follows medicines made by cells, examines crops and the environment, explores industrial and synthetic biology, and ends with ethics, regulation, biosecurity and research.

**Trivia**
- The word “biotechnology” was coined in 1919 by the Hungarian engineer Károly Ereky.
- Before 1982, insulin for people with diabetes came from the pancreases of pigs and cattle.
- Sequencing the first human genome cost about $3 billion; today a genome can be sequenced for a few hundred dollars.
- Every year, student teams worldwide compete in iGEM to design and build genetically engineered biological systems.

### Timeline
- **Ancient times · People** use yeast and microbes to make bread, beer, cheese and yoghurt.
- **1919 · Károly Ereky** coins the word “biotechnology”.
- **1928 · Alexander Fleming** discovers penicillin.
- **1973 · Cohen and Boyer** make the first recombinant DNA organisms.
- **1975 · The Asilomar Conference** sets early safety guidelines for genetic engineering.
- **1982 · Human insulin made by bacteria** is approved as a medicine.
- **1983 · Kary Mullis** invents the polymerase chain reaction (PCR).
- **1994 · The Flavr Savr tomato** becomes the first genetically engineered food sold in shops.
- **2003 · The Human Genome Project** is completed.
- **2012 · Charpentier and Doudna** show CRISPR–Cas9 can cut DNA at chosen sites.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know biotechnology the way a strong undergraduate course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain the toolkit:** recombinant DNA, PCR, sequencing and genome editing.
- **Explain medical biotechnology:** recombinant proteins, biologics, gene therapy and biosimilars.
- **Weigh agricultural biotechnology:** the evidence on genetically engineered crops and their regulation.
- **Explain industrial and synthetic biology:** fermentation, engineered microbes and biomanufacturing.
- **Discuss governance:** safety, ethics, regulation and biosecurity.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Biotechnologists, bioethicists and educators from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
