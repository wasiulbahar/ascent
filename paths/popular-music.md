# Popular and world music

The music most people actually listen to: blues, jazz, rock, reggae, Afrobeat, film songs, hip hop, K-pop and electronic music, the ideas scholars use to study them, and the business of music today. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free archives, concert videos and the music itself (on free streaming tiers and radio) cover much of the path. Every stage asks you to listen. Music from Africa, Asia, the Caribbean and Latin America has the same weight as music from the US and UK. (The Classical music and Music theory paths complement this one.)

# About popular and world music

Popular music is the music made for and loved by large audiences, spread by recordings, radio, film and the internet. World music studies, and the wider field of ethnomusicology, look at music in every culture. Together they ask how musical styles arise, travel and mix, what music means to communities, and how the music business works.

This path begins with books on why music matters and how it reached us, traces the roots of blues and jazz, travels through great popular musics of the world, follows hip hop, K-pop and electronic music, reads the scholars who study popular music, and ends with streaming, hit factories and the music business today.

**Trivia**
- Hip hop is often dated to 11 August 1973, when DJ Kool Herc played a back-to-school party in the Bronx, New York.
- India's film industries produce thousands of songs a year; film songs have long been the country's most popular music.
- The folklorist Alan Lomax recorded thousands of songs around the world from the 1930s to the 1990s; much of his archive is now free online.
- Streaming accounts for the majority of recorded-music revenue worldwide, according to the IFPI.

### Timeline
- **1877 · Edison's phonograph** Sound can be recorded.
- **1920s · Recorded blues and jazz** spread across the US and abroad.
- **1950s · Rock and roll**
- **1960s · Bossa nova and Tropicália** in Brazil; the Beatles worldwide.
- **1970s · Reggae and Afrobeat** Bob Marley and Fela Kuti.
- **1973 · Hip hop** begins in the Bronx.
- **1980s · House and techno** in Chicago and Detroit.
- **1990s · The MP3** and the end of the CD era.
- **2010s · Streaming and K-pop** reshape the global music business.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know popular and world music the way a strong first year of a university popular-music or ethnomusicology course expects. You will be able to:

- **Trace musical roots:** blues, jazz and the African diaspora in popular music.
- **Range across the world:** Afrobeat, reggae, film music, Brazilian music, K-pop and more.
- **Listen analytically:** rhythm, sound, production and lyrics in context.
- **Use the scholarship:** ethnomusicology, subculture, authenticity and the culture industry.
- **Understand the business:** recording, streaming, hit-making and who gets paid.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Musicians, ethnomusicologists and popular-music scholars from several continents will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
