# Abstract Algebra

The mathematics of structure and symmetry: groups from the Rubik's cube to crystals, rings and fields, Galois theory and why some equations and constructions are impossible, and applications to physics, codes and cryptography. About 180 to 220 hours over seven to nine months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. A free textbook, recorded Harvard lectures, free software and explainers cover the core. You need comfort with proofs (the Discrete mathematics path) and some linear algebra. Work every exercise you can: algebra is learned by doing.

# About abstract algebra

Abstract algebra studies structures defined by operations and rules, such as groups, rings and fields. These structures capture symmetry, arithmetic and solving equations in a single language.

This path starts with symmetry and visual groups, builds group theory, moves to rings and fields with applications to codes, tells the story of Galois theory and impossible problems, explores symmetry in crystals, physics and cryptography, and ends with representation theory, computer algebra and research.

**Trivia**
- Évariste Galois wrote down key results the night before he was killed in a duel in 1832, aged 20.
- The Rubik's cube has about 43 quintillion possible positions, all described by one group.
- The Monster, the largest sporadic simple group, has about 8 × 10⁵³ elements.
- There are exactly 17 kinds of wallpaper pattern symmetry, and many appear in Islamic art such as the Alhambra's tiles.

### Timeline
- **c. 820 · Al-Khwarizmi** writes the book that gives algebra its name.
- **1824 · Niels Henrik Abel** proves there is no general formula for fifth-degree equations.
- **1832 · Évariste Galois** writes his theory the night before his death.
- **1837 · Pierre Wantzel** proves angle trisection with ruler and compass is impossible.
- **1854 · Arthur Cayley** defines an abstract group.
- **1891 · Evgraf Fedorov** classifies the 230 crystal space groups.
- **1918 · Emmy Noether** links symmetries and conservation laws.
- **1960 · Reed and Solomon** invent error-correcting codes based on finite fields.
- **1982 · Robert Griess** constructs the Monster group.
- **2004 · The classification of finite simple groups** is completed.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know abstract algebra the way a strong first university course expects. You will be able to:

- **Work with groups:** subgroups, cosets, Lagrange's theorem, homomorphisms and quotients.
- **Work with rings and fields:** ideals, polynomial rings and finite fields.
- **Explain Galois theory:** why the quintic has no formula and why some constructions are impossible.
- **See symmetry everywhere:** crystals, patterns, physics and puzzles.
- **Apply algebra:** error-correcting codes, cryptography and computer algebra.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Mathematicians and mathematics educators from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
