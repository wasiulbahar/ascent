# Psychology

From the surprises of your own mind to the experiments, theories and controversies of a science. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Excellent free material exists, including a complete textbook and a famous university course. The path takes in how culture shapes the mind, and it puts psychology's own crisis of trust, the failure of many famous findings to replicate, at the centre of its final stage.

# About psychology

Psychology is the scientific study of mind and behaviour: how we perceive, think, remember, feel, learn, decide and relate to other people, and what happens when these go wrong. It ranges from brain cells to whole cultures, and its methods range from laboratory experiments and brain scans to surveys and long-term studies of children growing up.

This path begins with books that change how you see your own mind, teaches the basics and how psychological studies work, covers the main branches (cognitive, developmental, social and clinical), asks how culture shapes the mind, reaches the university core, and ends with the arguments about how much of psychology we can trust.

**Trivia**
- Wilhelm Wundt opened one of the first psychology laboratories, in Leipzig in 1879, often taken as the birth of experimental psychology.
- Most psychology studies have tested people from Western, educated, industrialised, rich and democratic societies, who make up a small minority of humanity. Researchers call them "WEIRD".
- A psychologist, Daniel Kahneman, won the 2002 Nobel prize in economics, for showing how people's judgements depart from pure logic.
- In 2015 a team of 270 researchers repeated 100 published psychology studies and found clear results again in only about a third of them.

### Timeline
- **1879 · Wundt's laboratory** Experimental psychology begins in Leipzig.
- **1890 · William James** *The Principles of Psychology*.
- **1900 · Freud** *The Interpretation of Dreams* launches psychoanalysis.
- **1913 · Behaviourism** John B. Watson argues psychology should study only behaviour.
- **1950s–60s · The cognitive revolution** The mind is studied as an information processor.
- **1961 · Milgram** The obedience experiments at Yale.
- **1979 · Prospect theory** Kahneman and Tversky on decisions under risk.
- **2010 · WEIRD** A landmark paper shows how narrow psychology's samples are.
- **2015 · The replication crisis** A large project finds many results don't repeat.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know psychology the way a strong first and second year of a university psychology degree expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain the main branches:** biological, cognitive, developmental, social and clinical psychology.
- **Read a psychology study critically:** its design, sample, statistics and limits.
- **Describe classic findings and their critics,** from Milgram to the marshmallow test.
- **Explain how culture shapes thinking,** and why narrow samples are a problem.
- **Weigh the debates:** the replication crisis, and what counts as a mental illness.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages makes you a capable student of psychology. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Psychology** certificate, you also complete:

1. **The grand quiz.** 40 game-style questions across all six stages. Pass mark 80%, with unlimited retakes.
2. **Study critique.** Analyse an unseen published study: design, sample, statistics and limits.
3. **Design a study.** Plan an experiment to test a claim, with its controls and ethics.
4. **Capstone essay.** 2,500 words on one of the Stage 6 questions. Strong essays are read by an expert reviewer.
5. **Explain it.** Answer three unseen questions as if to a curious friend. For example: "Why do we remember some things and forget others?"

The certificate needs every stage passed. Stages you continued past with a warning must be completed first.
