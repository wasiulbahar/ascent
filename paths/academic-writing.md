# Academic Writing

How to write clearly and convincingly for university and research: reading papers, clear sentences, arguments and structure, sources and citation, the writing process and peer review, and writing responsibly with AI. About 150 to 180 hours over six to eight months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free university writing centres cover most of the core. The path works for every discipline and for writers whose first language is not English. You learn most by writing, so every stage asks you to draft and revise.

# About academic writing

Academic writing is how scholars share ideas and evidence: essays, reports, theses and journal articles. Good academic writing is clear, well argued and honest about its sources.

This path starts with reading academic texts and joining the conversation, teaches clear sentences and paragraphs, builds arguments and structures, covers sources and integrity, follows a paper through drafting and peer review, and ends with style, open publishing and AI tools.

**Trivia**
- The first scientific journals, the *Journal des sçavans* and the *Philosophical Transactions*, both began in 1665.
- The word “essay” comes from Montaigne's *Essais* (1580), meaning “attempts”.
- *Nature* only made external peer review routine for all papers in 1973.
- In 1936 Einstein angrily withdrew a paper from *Physical Review* after it was sent to an anonymous referee.

### Timeline
- **1580 · Michel de Montaigne** publishes his *Essais*.
- **1665 · The first scientific journals** begin in Paris and London.
- **1906 · *The Chicago Manual of Style*** is first published.
- **1929 · The first APA guidelines** appear in *Psychological Bulletin*.
- **1958 · Stephen Toulmin** publishes *The Uses of Argument*.
- **1981 · Joseph Williams** publishes *Style: Ten Lessons in Clarity and Grace*.
- **1991 · arXiv** starts sharing preprints.
- **2006 · Graff and Birkenstein** publish *They Say / I Say*.
- **2023 · Publishing ethics bodies** rule that AI tools cannot be authors.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will write the way strong university work and early research expect. You will be able to:

- **Read academic texts:** efficiently, and place your work in the conversation.
- **Write clear prose:** sentences, paragraphs and flow that readers can follow.
- **Build arguments:** claims, evidence, warrants and counterarguments, in standard structures.
- **Use sources honestly:** searching, paraphrasing, quoting and citing in common styles.
- **Manage the process:** drafting, revising, feedback, peer review and responsible use of AI.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Writing teachers and researchers from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages makes you a capable academic writer. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Academic Writing** certificate, you also complete:

1. **The grand quiz.** 40 game-style questions across all six stages. Pass mark 80%, with unlimited retakes.
2. **Revision portfolio.** Submit a first draft, reviewer comments and your revised version with a reflection.
3. **Literature review.** Write a 2,000-word literature review on a question in your field.
4. **Capstone essay.** 2,500 words of original argument. Strong essays are read by an expert reviewer.
5. **Explain it.** Answer three unseen questions as if to a new student. For example: “When should I quote instead of paraphrase?”

The certificate needs every stage passed.
