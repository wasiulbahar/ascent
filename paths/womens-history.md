# Women's History

Women's lives across the world and through time: rulers, workers, writers and scientists from ancient to modern, women's work and family, religion and law, suffrage and liberation movements on every continent, and how historians recover lives left out of the record. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Many women's own writings, archives and museum collections are free online. The path is global, includes women of different classes, races and faiths, and presents debates within women's history and feminism fairly.

# About women's history

Women's history recovers the experiences of women, half of humanity, who were often left out of traditional histories focused on kings, wars and states. It asks how women lived, worked, believed, created and resisted, and how ideas about gender shaped whole societies.

This path begins with lively histories and women's own voices, travels through the ancient and medieval worlds, studies work, family and law in the early modern era, follows suffrage and women's movements across the world, explores the 20th century, and ends with how historians find women in the archives.

**Trivia**
- New Zealand became the first self-governing country to give women the vote in national elections, in 1893.
- Murasaki Shikibu's *The Tale of Genji*, written around 1010 in Japan, is often called the world's first novel.
- Ada Lovelace published what is often called the first computer program, for Babbage's Analytical Engine, in 1843.
- Women won the vote in Saudi Arabia's municipal elections in 2015.

### Timeline
- **c. 2300 BCE · Enheduanna** Akkadian priestess, is the first named author.
- **c. 1478–1458 BCE · Hatshepsut** rules Egypt as pharaoh.
- **c. 1010 · Murasaki Shikibu** writes *The Tale of Genji*.
- **1405 · Christine de Pizan** writes *The Book of the City of Ladies*.
- **1792 · Mary Wollstonecraft** publishes *A Vindication of the Rights of Woman*.
- **1848 · The Seneca Falls Convention** calls for women's rights in the US.
- **1893 · New Zealand** gives women the vote.
- **1949 · Simone de Beauvoir** publishes *The Second Sex*.
- **1979 · CEDAW** the UN women's rights convention, is adopted.
- **1995 · The Beijing Conference** declares “women's rights are human rights”.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know women's history the way a strong first year of a university course expects. You will be able to:

- **Trace women's lives:** across periods and continents.
- **Explain women's work, family and law:** and how they changed.
- **Compare women's movements:** suffrage, nationalism, labour and liberation worldwide.
- **Read women's own sources:** letters, diaries, petitions and literature.
- **Weigh debates:** intersectionality, agency and how to write women into history.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Historians of women and gender from more than one region will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
