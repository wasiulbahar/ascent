# Pacific Studies

The histories, cultures and futures of the Pacific Islands, Oceania: voyaging and settlement, Polynesian, Melanesian and Micronesian societies, colonial rule, nuclear testing and independence, Pacific arts and literature, and climate change and sovereignty today. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Pacific universities, museums and regional organisations publish much free material. The path centres Pacific Islander scholars and writers, covers the region's great diversity, and respects that some knowledge belongs to communities.

# About Pacific studies

Oceania covers thousands of islands across the Pacific, home to Polynesian, Melanesian and Micronesian peoples and over 1,000 languages. Its peoples were the world's greatest ocean voyagers, and today face colonial legacies and the existential threat of rising seas.

This path begins with Pacific voices and the idea of a “sea of islands”, studies voyaging and settlement, explores Pacific societies, follows colonisation and nuclear testing, examines independence and politics, and ends with arts, climate change and the Pacific's future.

**Trivia**
- Papua New Guinea alone has over 800 languages, more than any other country.
- Polynesians settled Hawaiʻi, Aotearoa New Zealand and Rapa Nui (Easter Island), the corners of the “Polynesian Triangle”.
- The US, Britain and France conducted over 300 nuclear tests in the Pacific between 1946 and 1996.
- Tuvalu's highest point is less than 5 metres above sea level.

### Timeline
- **c. 50,000 years ago · People** reach New Guinea and Australia.
- **c. 1500–1000 BCE · Lapita voyagers** spread into Remote Oceania.
- **c. 1000–1300 CE · Polynesians** settle Hawaiʻi, Rapa Nui and Aotearoa.
- **1768–79 · James Cook's voyages** bring sustained European contact.
- **1840 · The Treaty of Waitangi** in Aotearoa New Zealand.
- **1893 · The Hawaiian Kingdom** is overthrown.
- **1946–96 · Nuclear testing** in the Marshall Islands, Kiribati and French Polynesia.
- **1962 · Samoa** becomes the first Pacific Island state to gain independence.
- **1976 · Hōkūleʻa** sails from Hawaiʻi to Tahiti by traditional navigation.
- **2023 · A Vanuatu-led UN vote** asks the International Court of Justice for a climate opinion.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know Pacific studies the way a strong first year of a university course expects. You will be able to:

- **Describe Pacific diversity:** peoples, languages, societies and environments.
- **Explain voyaging and settlement:** archaeology, navigation and oral tradition.
- **Explain colonial histories:** missions, plantations, annexation and nuclear testing.
- **Discuss independence and politics:** sovereignty, regionalism and geopolitics.
- **Engage with Pacific arts and climate futures:** literature, art, climate justice and migration.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Pacific Islander scholars and community members from several island nations will review the path before it is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
