# Russian and Eastern European Studies

The history, cultures and politics of Russia, Ukraine, Poland, the Baltic states, the Balkans, the Caucasus and Central Europe: medieval Rus' and empires, great literature, revolution and the Soviet Union, communism and its fall, and the region today. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Many classic novels and historical documents are free in translation. The path treats the region as many nations, not as Russia's periphery, includes historians and writers from Ukraine, Poland, the Baltics and the Balkans, and presents contested histories and current wars through evidence and several perspectives.

# About Russian and Eastern European studies

Eastern Europe and Russia stretch from the Baltic to the Black Sea and across Eurasia. The region's history includes medieval Kyivan Rus', the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, Ottoman, Habsburg and Russian empires, two world wars fought largely on its soil, the Soviet experiment, communism's collapse in 1989–91, and new independent states.

This path begins with great literature and accessible histories, studies medieval and imperial history, follows the Russian revolutions and the Soviet Union, examines the Second World War and the Holocaust in the region, follows communism's fall, and ends with the region today.

**Trivia**
- Kyivan Rus', a medieval state centred on Kyiv, adopted Christianity in 988; Ukraine, Belarus and Russia all trace roots to it.
- The Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth's 1791 constitution was one of the first written national constitutions in Europe.
- Russia's serfs were emancipated in 1861, two years before Lincoln's Emancipation Proclamation.
- The Baltic Way of 1989 saw about two million people join hands across Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania.

### Timeline
- **988 · Volodymyr of Kyiv** adopts Christianity.
- **1240 · The Mongols** sack Kyiv.
- **1569 · The Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth** is formed.
- **1795 · Poland** is partitioned out of existence.
- **1861 · Russia** emancipates its serfs.
- **1917 · The Russian revolutions** bring the Bolsheviks to power.
- **1932–33 · The Holodomor** famine kills millions in Ukraine.
- **1939–45 · The Second World War** devastates the region.
- **1989 · Revolutions** end communist rule across Eastern Europe.
- **1991 · The Soviet Union** dissolves into 15 states.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know Russia and Eastern Europe the way a strong first year of a university area studies course expects. You will be able to:

- **Trace the region's history:** from medieval states to today.
- **Read its great literature:** Russian, Polish, Ukrainian, Czech and Balkan writers.
- **Explain the Soviet experiment:** revolution, terror, war and stagnation.
- **Explain 1989–91 and after:** transition, democracy, oligarchy and war.
- **Weigh contested histories:** using sources from several nations.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Scholars from Russia, Ukraine, Poland, the Baltics, the Balkans and elsewhere will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
