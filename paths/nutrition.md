# Nutrition

The science of food and the body: nutrients and energy, how nutrition evidence works and why it confuses, eating across life, the world's food systems and hunger, and the future of what we eat. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. A free textbook and free WHO, FAO and university guidance cover the core. Nutrition is full of fads and disputes, so the path stresses how to judge evidence. **It is not personal dietary or medical advice**: for your own diet or health, see a qualified professional.

# About nutrition

Nutrition studies how food nourishes the body: energy, carbohydrates, fats, proteins, vitamins and minerals, and how diet affects growth, health and disease. Public-health nutrition looks at whole populations, and food-systems research asks how the world grows, trades and wastes food, and why hundreds of millions still go hungry while others face diet-related disease.

This path begins with books that changed how people think about food, teaches the basics of nutrients, covers how nutrition evidence is gathered and why it is hard, looks at nutrition through life and in the body, studies food systems, hunger and policy, and ends with the future of food for people and planet.

**Trivia**
- The Global Burden of Disease study estimated that unhealthy diets were linked to about 11 million deaths worldwide in 2017, more than tobacco.
- Scurvy, caused by lack of vitamin C, killed many sailors until citrus was added to naval rations; James Lind's 1747 trial with lemons and oranges is one of the first controlled experiments.
- The WHO recommends exclusive breastfeeding for a baby's first six months.
- The economist Amartya Sen showed that famines can happen even when there is enough food, because people lose the means to obtain it.

### Timeline
- **1747 · James Lind** tests citrus against scurvy at sea.
- **1912 · Casimir Funk** proposes “vitamines”.
- **1943 · The first official dietary allowances** are published in the United States.
- **1981 · Sen's *Poverty and Famines***
- **1990s · Dietary guidelines** spread around the world.
- **2015 · Sustainable Development Goal 2** Zero hunger by 2030.
- **2019 · The EAT–Lancet Commission** proposes a “planetary health diet”.
- **2019 · The first controlled trial of ultra-processed food** at the US National Institutes of Health.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know nutrition the way a strong first year of a university nutrition course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain nutrients and energy:** what each does and how much the body needs.
- **Judge nutrition evidence:** trials, observational studies, and why headlines contradict each other.
- **Explain nutrition across life:** from infancy to old age, and in sport.
- **Explain food systems:** hunger, malnutrition, obesity and food policy around the world.
- **Weigh the debates:** ultra-processed food, sustainable diets and the future of food.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Nutrition scientists and dietitians from more than one continent will confirm them before the path is published. Knowledge only: not personal dietary advice.
