# Linguistics

From the sounds of speech to grammar, meaning, how children learn to talk, how languages change and die, and how computers process language. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free open textbooks, atlases and video series cover much of the field. The path draws examples from many of the world's roughly 7,000 languages, not just English.

# About linguistics

Linguistics is the scientific study of language. It asks how speech sounds are made and organised, how words and sentences are built, how meaning works, how children acquire language so fast, how languages vary between communities and change over centuries, and what language tells us about the mind.

This path begins with books that make language fascinating, teaches the core: sounds, words and sentences, moves to meaning, use and acquisition, looks at language in society, follows languages through history, and ends with endangered languages and language technology.

**Trivia**
- Papua New Guinea has more than 800 languages, more than any other country.
- Pirahã, spoken in the Amazon, has been described as having no exact words for numbers, a claim linguists still debate.
- Nicaraguan Sign Language arose in the 1970s and 1980s among deaf children brought together in new schools, letting linguists watch a language being born.
- About 40% of the world's languages are endangered, many with fewer than 1,000 speakers.

### Timeline
- **c. 4th century BCE · Pāṇini** writes a precise grammar of Sanskrit.
- **8th century · Sibawayh** writes a foundational grammar of Arabic.
- **1786 · William Jones** notes Sanskrit's kinship with Greek and Latin.
- **1916 · Saussure's *Course in General Linguistics*** is published.
- **1921 · Sapir's *Language***
- **1957 · Chomsky's *Syntactic Structures***
- **1966 · Labov's New York study** founds modern sociolinguistics.
- **1970s–80s · Nicaraguan Sign Language** emerges.
- **2010s · Neural language models** transform computational linguistics.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know linguistics the way a strong first year of a university linguistics degree expects. You will be able to:

- **Describe speech sounds:** using the International Phonetic Alphabet.
- **Analyse words and sentences:** morphology and syntax, with examples from several languages.
- **Explain meaning and use:** semantics, pragmatics and how context shapes what we say.
- **Explain variation and change:** accents, dialects, language families and how languages evolve.
- **Weigh the debates:** innateness, linguistic relativity, endangered languages and language technology.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Linguists working on languages from several families will confirm them before the path is published.

3. **Transcription.** Transcribe a short recording using the International Phonetic Alphabet.
4. **Capstone essay.** 2,500 words on one of the Stage 6 questions. Strong essays are read by an expert reviewer.
5. **Explain it.** Answer three unseen questions as if to a curious friend. For example: “Why do people in different places have different accents?”

The certificate needs every stage passed.
