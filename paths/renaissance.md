# The Renaissance

The age of rebirth, roughly 1350 to 1600: humanism and the recovery of ancient learning, Florence and the Medici, Leonardo and Michelangelo, printing, the Reformation, science and exploration, and the Renaissance's connections with the Islamic world, Africa and Asia. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Museums such as the Uffizi and the National Gallery and many Renaissance texts are free online. The path questions the idea of a sudden European “rebirth”, shows the Renaissance's debts to Byzantine and Islamic scholarship, and includes its darker side: slavery, conquest and witch trials.

# About the Renaissance

The Renaissance, French for “rebirth”, names a period of European history, roughly 1350 to 1600, when scholars and artists turned to ancient Greece and Rome for models. It produced extraordinary art, new ideas about the human being, the printing press, the Reformation and the beginnings of modern science.

This path begins with lively histories and the art itself, studies humanism and its sources, explores Florence, Venice and the courts, follows the great artists, examines print and the Reformation, and ends with science, exploration and the Renaissance in a global frame.

**Trivia**
- Leonardo da Vinci's notebooks, written in mirror writing, run to over 7,000 surviving pages.
- Gutenberg's printing press, around 1450, helped produce perhaps 20 million books in Europe by 1500.
- Many Greek texts reached Italian scholars through Byzantine émigrés and earlier Arabic translations.
- Michelangelo painted the Sistine Chapel ceiling between 1508 and 1512, largely standing, not lying on his back.

### Timeline
- **1341 · Petrarch** is crowned poet laureate in Rome.
- **1397 · Manuel Chrysoloras** begins teaching Greek in Florence.
- **c. 1450 · Gutenberg's press** begins printing in Mainz.
- **1453 · Constantinople** falls to the Ottomans.
- **1492 · Columbus** reaches the Americas; Jews are expelled from Spain.
- **1503–19 · Leonardo** paints the *Mona Lisa*.
- **1508–12 · Michelangelo** paints the Sistine Chapel ceiling.
- **1513 · Machiavelli** writes *The Prince*.
- **1517 · Martin Luther** publishes his 95 theses.
- **1543 · Copernicus and Vesalius** publish works that transform astronomy and anatomy.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know the Renaissance the way a strong first year of a university course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain humanism:** its sources, ideals and limits.
- **Read Renaissance art:** perspective, patronage and meaning.
- **Explain politics and society:** city-states, courts, women and the poor.
- **Explain print and Reformation:** how ideas spread and divided Europe.
- **Place the Renaissance globally:** its exchanges with the Islamic world, Africa, Asia and the Americas.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Historians and art historians from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.
