# Number Theory

The study of whole numbers: divisibility and modular arithmetic, the mystery of the primes, equations in integers from Pell to Fermat, number theory at work in codes and check digits, and the people and open problems that drive the field. About 150 to 180 hours over six to eight months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. A free textbook, problem sites, video explainers and the online encyclopedia of sequences cover the core. You need school algebra and comfort with proofs (the Discrete mathematics path helps). Experimenting with numbers by hand and in code is part of every stage.

# About number theory

Number theory studies the integers and their properties, especially prime numbers. Once called the “queen of mathematics” for its purity, it now secures the internet.

This path starts with stories of primes and Fermat's Last Theorem, teaches divisibility, congruences and classic results, explores the distribution of primes and famous open problems, studies equations in integers, applies number theory to cryptography and everyday codes, and ends with the people of number theory and current research.

**Trivia**
- The largest known prime, found in 2024, has more than 41 million digits.
- Pierre de Fermat wrote in a margin in 1637 that he had a proof “which this margin is too small to contain”; it took until 1995 to prove.
- When Hardy called 1729 a dull taxi number, Ramanujan replied it was the smallest number expressible as a sum of two cubes in two different ways.
- In 2013 Yitang Zhang proved there are infinitely many pairs of primes less than 70 million apart.

### Timeline
- **c. 300 BCE · Euclid** proves there are infinitely many primes.
- **c. 250 · Diophantus** writes *Arithmetica*.
- **628 · Brahmagupta** studies Pell's equation.
- **1637 · Pierre de Fermat** writes his famous margin note.
- **1801 · Carl Friedrich Gauss** publishes *Disquisitiones Arithmeticae*.
- **1859 · Bernhard Riemann** links the primes to the zeta function.
- **1896 · Hadamard and de la Vallée Poussin** prove the prime number theorem.
- **1913 · Srinivasa Ramanujan** writes to G. H. Hardy.
- **1995 · Andrew Wiles** publishes his proof of Fermat's Last Theorem.
- **2013 · Yitang Zhang** proves bounded gaps between primes.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know number theory the way a strong first university course expects. You will be able to:

- **Work with divisibility:** gcd, the Euclidean algorithm and unique factorisation.
- **Use modular arithmetic:** congruences, Fermat's little theorem and the Chinese remainder theorem.
- **Explain the primes:** the prime number theorem, famous conjectures and how primes are found.
- **Solve equations in integers:** linear Diophantine equations, Pell's equation and quadratic residues.
- **Apply number theory:** cryptography, primality tests and check digits.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Mathematicians and mathematics educators from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
