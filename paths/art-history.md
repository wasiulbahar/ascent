# Art history

From learning to look to the art of every continent, the methods art historians use, and the arguments about who owns the past. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Museums put millions of images and essays online for free, so much of this path costs nothing. Best of all, visit a museum or gallery near you whenever you can.

# About art history

Art history studies the things people have made to be looked at: paintings, sculptures, buildings, prints, textiles, photographs and more, from cave walls to today. It asks what a work shows, how it was made, what it meant to the people who made and saw it, and why it still matters.

This path begins with books and films that teach you to look, gives you the tools to describe and write about art, travels through the art of the world's great traditions, follows painting from the Renaissance to the modern age, reads the methods art historians use, and ends with today's debates about museums, markets and returning what was taken.

**Trivia**
- Cave paintings on the island of Sulawesi, Indonesia, at least 45,000 years old, are among the oldest known pictures of animals.
- The *Mona Lisa* was stolen from the Louvre in 1911 and missing for more than two years; the theft made it the world's most famous painting.
- In 1917 Marcel Duchamp submitted a factory-made urinal, signed “R. Mutt”, to an art exhibition. He called it *Fountain*.
- British soldiers looted thousands of objects, now called the Benin Bronzes, from the Kingdom of Benin in 1897; some have since been returned to Nigeria.

### Timeline
- **c. 43,000 BCE or earlier · Cave art in Sulawesi** Among the oldest known figurative pictures.
- **c. 2560 BCE · The Great Pyramid of Giza**
- **447–432 BCE · The Parthenon** built in Athens.
- **c. 210 BCE · The Terracotta Army** buried with China's first emperor.
- **5th century CE · The Ajanta caves** Buddhist paintings in India.
- **c. 691 · The Dome of the Rock** completed in Jerusalem.
- **1550 · Vasari** *Lives of the Artists*, the first great art history.
- **1874 · The first Impressionist exhibition** in Paris.
- **1917 · Duchamp's *Fountain***
- **2018 · The Sarr–Savoy report** calls for African heritage to be returned.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know art history the way a strong first year of a university art-history degree expects. You will be able to:

- **Look closely and describe:** composition, colour, material and technique, in clear words.
- **Place a work in its world:** who made it, for whom, and what it meant then.
- **Range across traditions:** African, Chinese, Indian, Islamic, European and modern art.
- **Use the methods:** iconography, social history, feminist and other approaches.
- **Weigh the debates:** museums, restitution and the art market.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Art historians and curators from more than one tradition will confirm them before the path is published.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages makes you a capable student of art history. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Art history** certificate, you also complete:

1. **The grand quiz.** 40 game-style questions across all six stages. Pass mark 80%, with unlimited retakes.
2. **Visual analysis.** Describe and interpret an unseen work: its form, subject, making and context.
3. **Comparison.** Compare two works from different traditions that treat the same theme.
4. **Capstone essay.** 2,500 words on one of the Stage 6 questions. Strong essays are read by an expert reviewer.
5. **Explain it.** Answer three unseen questions as if to a curious friend. For example: “Why is a urinal in a museum?”

The certificate needs every stage passed.

# Bridges to other subjects

- **History:** the societies that made the art.
- **Philosophy:** aesthetics and the nature of beauty.
- **Literature:** myths, scripture and poems that art illustrates.
- **Classical music:** parallel movements, from Baroque to modern.
- **Sociology:** museums, markets and taste.
