# Anthropology

From fieldwork and the variety of human cultures to human evolution, gifts and kinship, the great debates, and anthropology in today's world. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Several classics and modern studies are free. The path includes the discipline's founders, its colonial past and its critics, and anthropologists from many parts of the world.

# About anthropology

Anthropology studies human beings in all their variety: how people in different societies live, believe, make a living, raise families and govern themselves, and how humans evolved. Its signature method is fieldwork: living with a community for a long time to understand life from the inside.

This path begins with books that show what anthropology reveals, teaches fieldwork and ethnography, covers human evolution and biology, explores economy, kinship and religion, reads the classic theories and their critics, and ends with anthropology of today's world: global markets, social media and epidemics.

**Trivia**
- Bronisław Malinowski spent much of 1915–1918 in the Trobriand Islands, and his long stay helped make fieldwork anthropology's core method.
- Ancient DNA shows that most people outside Africa carry about 1–2% Neanderthal DNA.
- Margaret Mead's *Coming of Age in Samoa* (1928) was a bestseller, and a fierce later debate over its accuracy became a lesson in fieldwork itself.
- The word “taboo” comes from Tongan *tapu*, recorded by Captain Cook's expedition in 1777.

### Timeline
- **c. 300,000 years ago · The earliest known *Homo sapiens*** Fossils from Jebel Irhoud, Morocco.
- **1377 · Ibn Khaldun** studies social cohesion among nomads and city-dwellers.
- **1871 · Tylor's *Primitive Culture*** defines culture.
- **1922 · Malinowski's *Argonauts of the Western Pacific*** Fieldwork becomes the method.
- **1925 · Mauss's *The Gift***
- **1934 · Benedict's *Patterns of Culture***
- **1955 · Lévi-Strauss's *Tristes Tropiques***
- **1973 · Geertz's *The Interpretation of Cultures***
- **1986 · *Writing Culture*** questions how ethnography is written.
- **2010 · The Neanderthal genome** is published.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know anthropology the way a strong first year of a university anthropology degree expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain culture and fieldwork:** participant observation, ethnography and cultural relativism.
- **Explain human evolution:** our origins, migrations and the evidence of fossils and DNA.
- **Analyse economy, kinship and religion:** gifts, debt, family and ritual across societies.
- **Read the classic theories:** functionalism, structuralism, interpretation, and their critics.
- **Apply anthropology today:** to markets, technology, health and global change.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Anthropologists working in several regions will confirm them before the path is published.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages makes you a capable student of anthropology. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Anthropology** certificate, you also complete:

1. **The grand quiz.** 40 game-style questions across all six stages. Pass mark 80%, with unlimited retakes.
2. **Mini-fieldwork.** Observe one everyday setting for several hours, with permission, and write field notes and a short interpretation.
3. **Comparison.** Compare how two societies handle one practice, such as gift-giving or marriage.
4. **Capstone essay.** 2,500 words on one of the Stage 6 questions. Strong essays are read by an expert reviewer.
5. **Explain it.** Answer three unseen questions as if to a curious friend. For example: “Why do people give gifts?”

The certificate needs every stage passed.
