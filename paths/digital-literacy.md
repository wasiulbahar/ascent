# Digital Literacy

How to live, learn and work well online: core skills, finding and checking information, staying secure, protecting your privacy, recognising misinformation, deepfakes and AI, and being a responsible, creative digital citizen. About 80 to 100 hours over three to four months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free tutorials, security guides, games and frameworks cover nearly everything. No experience is needed; a phone or a shared computer is enough. Practise each skill on your own devices and accounts.

# About digital literacy

Digital literacy is the ability to use digital technology confidently, safely, critically and creatively: finding reliable information, protecting yourself, communicating respectfully and making things.

This path starts with core skills and frameworks, teaches searching and checking information, covers passwords, scams and security, explores privacy and the data economy, tackles misinformation, deepfakes and AI tools, and ends with copyright, online harassment, accessibility and contributing to the shared web.

**Trivia**
- A 2016 Stanford study found most students struggled to tell sponsored content from real news articles.
- Wikipedia exists in more than 300 languages, written by volunteers.
- Simple passwords such as “123456” still top lists of the most common passwords every year.
- Playing short “prebunking” games that expose manipulation tactics can make people better at spotting misinformation.

### Timeline
- **1989 · Tim Berners-Lee** proposes the World Wide Web.
- **1998 · Google** is founded.
- **2001 · Wikipedia** is launched.
- **2002 · Creative Commons** releases its first licences.
- **2016 · “Post-truth”** is named a word of the year.
- **2018 · The EU's GDPR** takes effect; the Cambridge Analytica scandal breaks.
- **2018 · Researchers** release the *Bad News* prebunking game.
- **2022 · ChatGPT** brings generative AI to the public.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will use digital technology the way a confident, careful digital citizen does. You will be able to:

- **Use core tools:** files, email, documents, video calls and online services.
- **Find and check information:** search well, verify sources and read laterally.
- **Stay secure:** strong passwords, two-factor authentication, updates and spotting scams.
- **Protect privacy:** understand data collection, adjust settings and use your rights.
- **Think critically and act responsibly:** misinformation, AI, copyright, wellbeing and respect online.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Librarians, educators and security experts from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages makes you a confident digital citizen. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Digital Literacy** certificate, you also complete:

1. **The grand quiz.** 40 game-style questions across all six stages. Pass mark 80%, with unlimited retakes.
2. **Security check-up.** Secure your own accounts and devices and document what you changed.
3. **Fact-check portfolio.** Verify five real claims with SIFT and record your sources.
4. **Capstone report.** 2,500 words on one of the Stage 6 questions. Strong reports are read by an expert reviewer.
5. **Explain it.** Teach a friend or family member three digital safety skills.

The certificate needs every stage passed.
