# Drawing

How to draw what you see and imagine: seeing like an artist, line, form, perspective and light, figures and faces, learning from masters across cultures, materials from charcoal to digital, and building a lifelong drawing habit. About 150 to 200 hours over six to nine months, most of it spent drawing.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free video lessons, structured courses, timed reference tools and museum collections cover the core. You only need paper and a pencil; free software is offered for digital drawing. Draw every day, even for ten minutes. The path draws on traditions from Europe, East Asia, the Islamic world and beyond.

# About drawing

Drawing is making marks to show what we see, think or imagine. It is the foundation of painting, design, illustration, architecture, animation and comics, and a powerful way of seeing and thinking.

This path begins with learning to see and building a habit, teaches line, form, perspective and value, moves to figures, faces and anatomy, studies masters from several traditions, explores materials and digital tools, and ends with storytelling, life drawing, scientific illustration and sharing your work.

**Trivia**
- A cave painting in Sulawesi, Indonesia, showing people and a pig, is more than 51,000 years old, among the oldest known figurative art.
- Nicolas-Jacques Conté invented the modern pencil lead, a mix of graphite and clay, in 1795.
- About 7,000 pages of Leonardo da Vinci's notebooks survive, full of drawings of machines, anatomy and nature.
- Hokusai published his famous sketchbooks, the *Hokusai Manga*, in 15 volumes.

### Timeline
- **c. 51,000 years ago · Artists in Sulawesi** paint figures on cave walls.
- **c. 36,000 years ago · Artists at Chauvet Cave** draw animals in charcoal.
- **1415 · Filippo Brunelleschi** demonstrates linear perspective in Florence.
- **c. 1490 · Leonardo da Vinci** draws the *Vitruvian Man*.
- **1679 · *The Mustard Seed Garden Manual*** teaches Chinese painting and drawing.
- **1795 · Nicolas-Jacques Conté** invents the modern pencil lead.
- **1814 · Hokusai** publishes the first volume of his *Manga* sketchbooks.
- **1920 · George Bridgman** publishes *Constructive Anatomy* for artists.
- **2009 · Inktober** starts as a daily ink drawing challenge.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will draw with the confidence of a committed amateur or early art student. You will be able to:

- **See like an artist:** observe shapes, edges, proportions and spaces accurately.
- **Use the fundamentals:** line, form, perspective, value and composition.
- **Draw people:** gesture, proportion, anatomy and portraits.
- **Learn from traditions:** European, East Asian, Islamic and other drawing practices.
- **Practise sustainably:** with materials, digital tools, storytelling and a lasting habit.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Artists and art teachers from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages makes you a confident draughtsperson. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Drawing** certificate, you also complete:

1. **The grand quiz.** 40 game-style questions across all six stages. Pass mark 80%, with unlimited retakes.
2. **Sketchbook.** Fill a sketchbook over at least three months, showing daily practice.
3. **Portfolio.** Twelve finished drawings: still life, perspective, figure, portrait, a master copy and free choice.
4. **Capstone essay.** 2,500 words on one of the Stage 6 questions or on a drawing tradition. Strong essays are read by an expert reviewer.
5. **Explain it.** Teach a friend to draw a simple object in perspective with shading.

The certificate needs every stage passed.
