# Oral Health

Teeth, gums and the mouth as part of whole-body health: the biology of teeth and decay, prevention with fluoride and less sugar, how dentistry treats disease, oral health inequality and neglected conditions, and the history and future of dental care. About 100 to 130 hours over four to six months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free WHO, dental federation and research resources cover the core. No background is needed. This path is for understanding, not treatment: see a dental or health worker about your own mouth. It looks at oral health in every kind of setting, from well-equipped clinics to places with no dentist at all.

# About oral health

Oral health covers the teeth, gums and mouth. Oral diseases such as tooth decay and gum disease are among the most common conditions in the world, yet they are largely preventable.

This path begins with the global picture and basic biology, studies decay, gum disease and the oral microbiome, covers prevention with fluoride, diet and brushing, explains how dentistry treats disease and serious conditions such as oral cancer and noma, examines inequality and links to whole-body health, and ends with history, careers and research.

**Trivia**
- Oral diseases affect around 3.5 billion people worldwide, according to the WHO.
- Untreated tooth decay in permanent teeth is the single most common health condition on Earth.
- Teeth drilled around 9,000 years ago at Mehrgarh in South Asia are among the earliest evidence of dentistry.
- Atraumatic restorative treatment, using hand tools and no electricity, was developed in Tanzania in the 1980s.

### Timeline
- **c. 7000 BCE · People at Mehrgarh** drill teeth, an early sign of dentistry.
- **1728 · Pierre Fauchard** publishes *The Surgeon Dentist*.
- **1840 · The first dental college** opens in Baltimore.
- **1844 · Horace Wells** uses nitrous oxide for pain-free extraction.
- **1890 · Willoughby Miller** explains how bacteria and sugar cause decay.
- **1945 · Grand Rapids** becomes the first city to fluoridate its water.
- **1980s · Atraumatic restorative treatment** is developed in Tanzania.
- **2013 · The Minamata Convention** agrees to phase down mercury, including in dental amalgam.
- **2021 · The World Health Assembly** passes a resolution on oral health.
- **2023 · The WHO** adds noma to its list of neglected tropical diseases.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will understand oral health the way a strong public health or introductory dental course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain oral biology:** teeth, saliva, the oral microbiome, and how decay and gum disease develop.
- **Explain prevention:** fluoride, sugar reduction, brushing and community programmes.
- **Explain treatment:** fillings, minimal intervention dentistry and serious oral conditions.
- **Analyse oral health inequality:** who lacks care, and links with general health.
- **Discuss the profession:** history, teams, sustainability and research.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Dentists and oral health researchers from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
