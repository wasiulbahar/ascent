# Philosophy of Mind

What minds are and how they fit into the physical world: the mind–body problem, consciousness and the “hard problem”, thought and meaning, free will and the self, animal and machine minds. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy and many classic papers are free. The path presents dualists, physicalists, functionalists and sceptics fairly, includes perspectives from Buddhist and Indian philosophy, and lets you decide.

# About the philosophy of mind

Philosophy of mind asks what minds are. How can thoughts, feelings and experiences exist in a world of atoms? Is the mind the brain, or something more? Could a computer think, or an octopus feel? Do we have free will, and what is a self?

This path begins with lively books and thought experiments, teaches the classic positions on the mind–body problem, tackles consciousness and the hard problem, explores thought, meaning and machines, examines the self and free will, and ends with animal minds and today's debates.

**Trivia**
- René Descartes's “I think, therefore I am” appeared in French in 1637, before its famous Latin form.
- In Frank Jackson's 1982 thought experiment, Mary, a scientist who knows everything about colour but has never seen it, leaves her black-and-white room and sees red for the first time.
- Alan Turing proposed his “imitation game”, now called the Turing test, in 1950.
- Thomas Nagel's 1974 essay “What Is It Like to Be a Bat?” became one of the most cited papers in philosophy.

### Timeline
- **c. 500 BCE · The Buddha** teaches the doctrine of no-self (anattā).
- **c. 350 BCE · Aristotle's *On the Soul*** treats the soul as the form of the body.
- **1641 · Descartes's *Meditations*** argue mind and body are distinct.
- **1714 · Leibniz's mill** argues perception can't be explained mechanically.
- **1949 · Gilbert Ryle** attacks the “ghost in the machine”.
- **1950 · Alan Turing** asks whether machines can think.
- **1974 · Thomas Nagel** asks what it is like to be a bat.
- **1980 · John Searle's Chinese room** argument.
- **1995 · David Chalmers** names the “hard problem” of consciousness.
- **2024 · The New York Declaration on Animal Consciousness** is signed by scientists and philosophers.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know the philosophy of mind the way a strong first year of a university course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain the main positions:** dualism, physicalism, functionalism and their rivals.
- **Explain consciousness debates:** the hard problem, qualia and the knowledge argument.
- **Explain thought and meaning:** intentionality, and whether machines can think.
- **Discuss the self and free will:** with Western, Buddhist and Indian views.
- **Write a philosophical argument:** stating premises, objections and replies.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Philosophers of mind from more than one tradition will confirm them before the path is published.
