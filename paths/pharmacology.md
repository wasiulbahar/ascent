# Pharmacology

How medicines work: the history of drug discovery, how drugs act on the body (pharmacodynamics) and how the body handles drugs (pharmacokinetics), major drug classes, clinical trials and safety, and access, pricing and the future of medicines. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free textbooks, regulators' resources and WHO materials cover the core. The path explains medicines with evidence, includes medicines from many traditions and the global problem of access, and presents debates about pricing and regulation fairly. **It is knowledge only: never use it to choose, start, stop or change any medicine. Ask a doctor or pharmacist.**

# About pharmacology

Pharmacology is the science of drugs and how they interact with living systems: what they do to the body, what the body does to them, and how they are discovered, tested, regulated and used safely.

This path begins with the dramatic history of drug discovery, teaches how drugs act and how the body handles them, surveys major drug classes, explains clinical trials and drug safety, and ends with access, pricing, antibiotics and the future of medicines.

**Trivia**
- Aspirin's ancestor, salicin, comes from willow bark, used for pain for thousands of years.
- The WHO Model List of Essential Medicines, first published in 1977, now lists about 500 medicines.
- The thalidomide disaster of the late 1950s and early 1960s, which caused birth defects in over 10,000 babies, led to modern drug safety laws.
- Artemisinin, discovered by Tu Youyou from a traditional Chinese remedy, transformed malaria treatment; she won the 2015 Nobel Prize.

### Timeline
- **c. 1550 BCE · The Ebers Papyrus** lists hundreds of remedies.
- **1805 · Morphine** is isolated from opium.
- **1897 · Aspirin** is synthesised at Bayer.
- **1921–22 · Insulin** is discovered and first used.
- **1928 · Penicillin** is discovered by Fleming.
- **1938 · The US Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act** requires safety testing.
- **1957–61 · Thalidomide** causes birth defects worldwide.
- **1962 · The Kefauver–Harris Amendment** requires proof of effectiveness.
- **1972 · Tu Youyou's team** isolates artemisinin.
- **1977 · The first WHO Essential Medicines List** is published.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know pharmacology the way a strong first year of a university pharmacology course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain how drugs act:** receptors, enzymes, agonists, antagonists and dose–response.
- **Explain pharmacokinetics:** absorption, distribution, metabolism and excretion.
- **Describe major drug classes:** pain, infection, heart, mind and cancer medicines.
- **Explain drug development and safety:** trials, regulation and adverse effects.
- **Weigh access and policy:** pricing, generics, essential medicines and resistance.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Pharmacologists, pharmacists and doctors from more than one country will review the path before it is published. Knowledge only: never medical advice.
