# Evolution

How life changes over time: Darwin's great idea, natural selection and genes, fossils and the tree of life, human origins, evolution in action today, and the debates at the frontier. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Darwin's own books, free courses and fossil databases cover the core. The path presents real scientific debates about how evolution works fairly, and explains why evolution itself is the accepted foundation of biology.

# About evolution

Evolution is the change in living things over generations. Through natural selection, genetic drift and other processes, populations adapt and new species arise. All life on Earth shares common ancestors, and evolution is the idea that ties the whole of biology together.

This path begins with lively books on why evolution is true, reads Darwin himself, explains the genetics of evolution, explores fossils and the tree of life, traces human origins, and ends with evolution observed today and the open questions researchers still argue about.

**Trivia**
- Darwin and Alfred Russel Wallace announced the theory of natural selection together in 1858, a year before *On the Origin of Species*.
- Life on Earth is at least 3.5 billion years old, and possibly older.
- Biologists have watched evolution happen in real time, including in a laboratory experiment with E. coli running since 1988.
- Whales evolved from land mammals; fossils show ancestors with legs that lived around 50 million years ago.

### Timeline
- **1809 · Lamarck** proposes an early theory of evolution.
- **1858 · Darwin and Wallace** announce natural selection.
- **1859 · *On the Origin of Species*** is published.
- **1871 · *The Descent of Man*** applies evolution to humans.
- **1930s–40s · The modern synthesis** unites Darwin and genetics.
- **1974 · “Lucy”** an early human relative, is found in Ethiopia.
- **1988 · Lenski's E. coli experiment** begins.
- **2004 · *Tiktaalik*** a fish with limb-like fins, is found in the Arctic.
- **2010 · Neanderthal DNA** is found in living humans.
- **2015 · *Homo naledi*** is announced from South Africa.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know evolution the way a strong first year of a university evolutionary biology course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain natural selection:** variation, inheritance and differential survival.
- **Explain other mechanisms:** genetic drift, sexual selection and gene flow.
- **Read the evidence:** fossils, anatomy, DNA and direct observation.
- **Trace the tree of life:** common ancestry, speciation and human origins.
- **Weigh debates:** open questions about how evolution works.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Evolutionary biologists and palaeontologists from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
