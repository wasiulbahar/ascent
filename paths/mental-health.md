# Mental Health and Wellbeing

How minds flourish and struggle: what mental health is, common conditions, the evidence behind everyday wellbeing, therapy and medication, mental health systems around the world, and how to read the research. About 180 to 220 hours over eight to ten months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free guides from the WHO, national health services and universities cover the core. This path is for understanding, not diagnosis or treatment. If you are struggling or thinking about suicide, contact local emergency services or a crisis line now; findahelpline.com lists free, confidential lines in many countries.

# About mental health

Mental health is more than the absence of illness: it is the ability to cope with stress, work, relate to others and enjoy life. Mental health conditions such as depression and anxiety are common, treatable and found in every country.

This path begins with first-person accounts and the basics, explains major conditions and how they are classified, tests popular wellbeing advice against evidence, explains therapy and medication, looks at mental health systems and stigma worldwide, and ends with reading research critically.

**Trivia**
- The World Health Organization estimated that in 2019 about one in eight people worldwide lived with a mental disorder.
- Zimbabwe's Friendship Bench trains lay health workers, often grandmothers, to deliver talking therapy on park benches.
- The word “psychiatry” was coined by the German physician Johann Christian Reil in 1808.
- A 2018 analysis of 522 trials found all 21 antidepressants studied worked better than placebo for adults with major depression.

### Timeline
- **1790s · Philippe Pinel** promotes humane “moral treatment” in Paris asylums.
- **1808 · Johann Christian Reil** coins the word “psychiatry”.
- **1952 · Chlorpromazine** becomes the first widely used antipsychotic; the first DSM is published.
- **1960s · Aaron Beck** develops cognitive therapy for depression.
- **1979 · Jon Kabat-Zinn** starts mindfulness-based stress reduction.
- **1980 · DSM-III** introduces explicit diagnostic criteria.
- **2006 · The UN** adopts the Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities.
- **2016 · A Friendship Bench trial** shows lay-delivered therapy reduces depression in Zimbabwe.
- **2022 · ICD-11** takes effect; the WHO publishes its World Mental Health Report.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will understand mental health the way a strong introductory university course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain mental health:** as a spectrum, and the major conditions and how they are classified.
- **Weigh wellbeing advice:** sleep, exercise, connection and mindfulness, against the evidence.
- **Explain treatments:** psychological therapies and medication, what they do and their limits.
- **Understand systems and stigma:** the treatment gap and approaches from around the world.
- **Read research critically:** trials, reviews and claims about apps and quick fixes.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Clinicians, researchers and people with lived experience from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages gives you a strong understanding of mental health. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Mental Health and Wellbeing** certificate, you also complete:

1. **The grand quiz.** 40 game-style questions across all six stages. Pass mark 80%, with unlimited retakes.
