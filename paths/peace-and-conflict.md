# Peace and Conflict

Why wars and violence happen and how they end: theories of conflict, genocide and its prevention, nonviolent resistance, peacekeeping and peace processes, and justice and reconciliation after violence. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free conflict databases, UN reports and peace agreements cover the core. The path studies conflicts from every continent, avoids taking sides in current wars, and presents debates about intervention, justice and peace fairly. Some material describes violence directly.

# About peace and conflict studies

Peace and conflict studies asks why groups fight, how violence can be prevented, and how societies rebuild after war. It draws on political science, history, sociology, psychology and law, and studies everything from civil wars and genocide to nonviolent movements and peace agreements.

This path begins with powerful accounts of war and peace, teaches theories of why conflict happens, confronts genocide, explores nonviolent resistance, studies peacekeeping and peace processes, and ends with justice and reconciliation after violence.

**Trivia**
- The word “genocide” was coined by the lawyer Raphael Lemkin in 1944.
- Researchers Erica Chenoweth and Maria Stephan found that nonviolent campaigns from 1900 to 2006 succeeded about twice as often as violent ones.
- The first armed UN peacekeeping force was sent to Egypt's Sinai during the 1956 Suez Crisis.
- Johan Galtung distinguished “negative peace”, the absence of violence, from “positive peace”, the presence of justice.

### Timeline
- **1648 · The Peace of Westphalia** ends the Thirty Years' War.
- **1899 · The Hague Convention** sets early laws of war.
- **1945 · The United Nations** is founded.
- **1948 · The Genocide Convention** is adopted.
- **1949 · The Geneva Conventions** are revised.
- **1994 · The Rwandan genocide** kills an estimated 500,000 to 800,000 people.
- **1995 · Srebrenica** and the Dayton Accords end the Bosnian war.
- **1996 · South Africa's Truth and Reconciliation Commission** begins hearings.
- **1998 · The Good Friday Agreement** in Northern Ireland.
- **2016 · Colombia's peace agreement** with the FARC.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know peace and conflict studies the way a strong first year of a university course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain why conflict happens:** grievance, greed, identity, security dilemmas and the state.
- **Explain genocide:** how it happens, and why prevention so often fails.
- **Explain nonviolent action:** how it works and when it succeeds.
- **Explain peacemaking:** mediation, peace agreements and peacekeeping.
- **Weigh justice after violence:** trials, truth commissions and reconciliation.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Peace researchers and practitioners from more than one region will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
