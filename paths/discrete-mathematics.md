# Discrete Mathematics

The mathematics of separate, countable things, and the foundation of computer science: logic and proof, counting, recurrences, graphs, number theory and cryptography, and computation itself. About 180 to 220 hours over seven to nine months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Several excellent free textbooks and MIT's course cover the whole path. You need school algebra; no calculus is required. Writing your own proofs, and having them checked, is the heart of this path.

# About discrete mathematics

Discrete mathematics studies structures made of separate pieces: integers, logical statements, sets, networks and finite processes. It is the language of computer science, cryptography, networks and puzzles.

This path begins with what mathematical thinking is, teaches logic and proof, covers counting and recurrences, studies graphs, introduces number theory and the cryptography that protects the internet, and ends with Boolean logic, automata, computability and famous problems.

**Trivia**
- At any party of six people, there are always three who all know each other or three who are all strangers.
- The four colour theorem, proved in 1976 by Kenneth Appel and Wolfgang Haken, was the first major theorem proved with heavy use of a computer.
- Claude Shannon's 1937 master's thesis showed that switching circuits can carry out Boolean logic, laying a foundation for digital computers.
- Paul Erdős wrote about 1,500 papers with hundreds of co-authors; mathematicians still count their “Erdős number”.

### Timeline
- **c. 300 BCE · Euclid** proves there are infinitely many primes.
- **1736 · Leonhard Euler** solves the Königsberg bridges problem.
- **1852 · Francis Guthrie** poses the four colour problem.
- **1854 · George Boole** publishes *The Laws of Thought*.
- **1930 · Frank Ramsey** publishes the theorem behind Ramsey theory.
- **1936 · Alan Turing** defines computability with Turing machines.
- **1937 · Claude Shannon** links Boolean logic to switching circuits.
- **1976 · Appel and Haken** prove the four colour theorem; Diffie and Hellman publish public-key exchange.
- **1977 · Rivest, Shamir and Adleman** invent RSA encryption.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know discrete mathematics the way a strong first course for mathematics and computer science students expects. You will be able to:

- **Reason logically:** propositional and predicate logic, sets and functions.
- **Write proofs:** direct, contradiction, contrapositive and induction.
- **Count:** permutations, combinations, pigeonhole, inclusion–exclusion and recurrences.
- **Use graphs:** paths, trees, colouring and their applications.
- **Apply number theory and computation:** modular arithmetic, RSA, automata and computability.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Mathematicians and computer science educators from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.

Finishing all six stages makes you fluent in discrete mathematics. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Discrete Mathematics** certificate, you also complete:
