# Veterinary Science

How animals' bodies work and how we keep them healthy: comparative anatomy and physiology, animal behaviour and welfare, infectious disease and One Health, farm, companion and wild animals, and the ethics of how humans use animals. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free textbooks, veterinary organisations and global animal health bodies cover the core. The path covers working, farm, companion and wild animals across many countries, and presents debates about animal use fairly. **It is knowledge only: not veterinary advice and not a professional qualification.**

# About veterinary science

Veterinary science studies the health of animals: how their bodies work, what makes them ill, and how to prevent and treat disease. Vets care for pets, farm animals, working animals and wildlife, and protect human health too, since many diseases pass between animals and people.

This path begins with lively stories of veterinary life, teaches comparative anatomy and physiology, studies behaviour and welfare, explores infectious disease and One Health, examines farm, companion and wild animal care, and ends with ethics and the future of the profession.

**Trivia**
- The world's first veterinary school was founded in Lyon, France, in 1761.
- Rinderpest, a deadly cattle disease, was declared eradicated in 2011, only the second disease ever eradicated after smallpox.
- About 60% of known human infectious diseases, and about 75% of emerging ones, come from animals.
- Dogs were domesticated from wolves at least 15,000 years ago.

### Timeline
- **c. 1900 BCE · The Kahun Veterinary Papyrus** in Egypt describes animal care.
- **1761 · The first veterinary school** opens in Lyon.
- **1885 · Louis Pasteur** uses a rabies vaccine in a human after animal studies.
- **1924 · The OIE (now WOAH)** is founded to fight animal disease.
- **1965 · The UK's Brambell Report** leads to the “Five Freedoms” of animal welfare.
- **1986 · BSE (“mad cow disease”)** is identified in the UK.
- **2004 · The One Health concept** is set out in the Manhattan Principles.
- **2011 · Rinderpest** is declared eradicated.
- **2018 · The “Zero by 30” plan** aims to end dog-mediated human rabies deaths by 2030.
- **2022 · WOAH, FAO, UNEP and WHO** launch a joint One Health plan.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know veterinary science the way a strong first year of a university veterinary or animal science course expects. You will be able to:

- **Compare animal bodies:** anatomy and physiology across species.
- **Explain behaviour and welfare:** and how to assess an animal's wellbeing.
- **Explain animal disease:** infection, immunity, vaccination and zoonoses.
- **Describe animal care:** farm, companion, working and wild animals.
- **Weigh the ethics:** animal use, welfare standards and the vet's role.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Veterinarians and animal scientists from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published. Knowledge only: not veterinary advice or a qualification.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages makes you a capable student of veterinary science. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Veterinary Science** certificate, you also complete:

1. **The grand quiz.** 40 game-style questions across all six stages. Pass mark 80%, with unlimited retakes.
2. **Welfare assessment.** Assess an animal's welfare from an unseen description using the Five Domains.
3. **Disease brief.** Profile one animal disease: cause, spread, prevention and One Health impact.
4. **Capstone essay.** 2,500 words on one of the Stage 6 questions. Strong essays are read by an expert reviewer.
5. **Explain it.** Answer three unseen questions as if to a curious friend. For example: “Why can't dogs eat chocolate?”

The certificate needs every stage passed, and is knowledge only: not veterinary advice or a qualification.
