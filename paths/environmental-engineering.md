# Environmental Engineering

How engineers protect people and the planet: clean drinking water and sanitation, wastewater treatment, air quality, solid waste and contaminated land, life cycle thinking and climate adaptation, and environmental justice and emergencies. About 200 to 250 hours over eight to ten months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. WHO, UN, World Bank and agency guidelines, free courses and official reports cover the core. You need school chemistry, biology and algebra. The path draws on cases from many countries and treats access to water, sanitation and clean air as questions of rights and justice.

# About environmental engineering

Environmental engineering applies science and engineering to protect human health and the environment: supplying safe water, treating sewage, cleaning air, managing waste, cleaning up pollution and planning for a changing climate.

This path starts with sanitation and the state of the world's water, teaches drinking water and wastewater treatment, covers air pollution, solid waste and contaminated land, explores life cycle assessment, impact assessment and climate adaptation, and ends with justice, emergencies, nature-based solutions and research.

**Trivia**
- In a 2007 *BMJ* readers' poll, sanitation was voted the greatest medical milestone since 1840.
- London's sewers, built by Joseph Bazalgette after the “Great Stink” of 1858, are still in use today.
- The Great Smog of London in December 1952 killed thousands of people in a few days.
- About 2 billion people still lack safely managed drinking water.

### Timeline
- **1858 · London's “Great Stink”** leads to Bazalgette's sewer system.
- **1908 · Jersey City** begins continuous chlorination of a public water supply.
- **1914 · Ardern and Lockett** develop the activated sludge process.
- **1952 · The Great Smog** kills thousands in London.
- **1962 · Rachel Carson** publishes *Silent Spring*.
- **1970 · The US Environmental Protection Agency** is created.
- **1987 · The Montreal Protocol** agrees to phase out ozone-destroying chemicals.
- **2010 · The UN General Assembly** recognises the human right to water and sanitation.
- **2014 · Flint, Michigan** switches water source, leading to lead contamination.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know environmental engineering the way a strong undergraduate course expects. You will be able to:

- **Treat water:** drinking water treatment, distribution and quality standards.
- **Treat wastewater:** sewers, activated sludge and safe sanitation for all.
- **Control air pollution:** sources, health effects, standards and controls.
- **Manage waste and land:** landfills, recycling, plastics and remediation.
- **Plan sustainably and fairly:** life cycle and impact assessment, adaptation and environmental justice.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Environmental engineers and public health educators from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages makes you a capable student of environmental engineering. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Environmental Engineering** certificate, you also complete:

1. **The grand quiz.** 40 game-style questions across all six stages. Pass mark 80%, with unlimited retakes.
2. **Design problem.** Size water or wastewater treatment for a small community.
3. **Site assessment.** Assess the environmental risks and justice issues of a real local site.
4. **Capstone report.** 2,500 words on one of the Stage 6 questions. Strong reports are read by an expert reviewer.
5. **Explain it.** Answer three unseen questions as if to a friend. For example: “How does dirty water become safe to drink?”

The certificate needs every stage passed. It is not a professional engineering qualification.
