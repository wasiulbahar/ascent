# Climate Science

The physical science of Earth's climate: the greenhouse effect, the atmosphere and oceans, past climates from ice cores, climate models and how they are tested, extreme weather and attribution, tipping points, and the IPCC's assessments. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. IPCC reports, NASA and NOAA data, and several excellent books are free. The path focuses on the physical science, distinguishing well-established findings from genuine uncertainties, and explains how scientists know what they know. Policy is covered in the Environment and Energy paths.

# About climate science

Climate science studies Earth's climate system: the atmosphere, oceans, ice, land and life, and how they exchange energy and carbon. It explains why the planet is warming, how much more it may warm, and what that means for weather, sea level and ecosystems.

This path begins with clear introductions, teaches the physics of the greenhouse effect, studies the atmosphere and oceans, reads past climates from ice and rock, explains climate models and their tests, and ends with extremes, attribution, tipping points and the IPCC's assessments.

**Trivia**
- John Tyndall measured how carbon dioxide and water vapour absorb heat in 1859; Eunice Foote had shown CO₂ warms air in sunlight in 1856.
- Svante Arrhenius estimated in 1896 that doubling CO₂ would warm Earth by several degrees.
- The Keeling Curve, measured at Mauna Loa since 1958, shows CO₂ rising from about 315 to over 420 parts per million.
- Ice cores from Antarctica record about 800,000 years of atmospheric CO₂.

### Timeline
- **1856 · Eunice Foote** shows CO₂ warms air in sunlight.
- **1859 · John Tyndall** measures heat absorption by gases.
- **1896 · Svante Arrhenius** estimates the warming from doubled CO₂.
- **1938 · Guy Callendar** links rising CO₂ to observed warming.
- **1958 · Charles David Keeling** begins CO₂ measurements at Mauna Loa.
- **1967 · Manabe and Wetherald** build an early climate model.
- **1988 · The IPCC** is founded.
- **2004 · The first event attribution study** links the 2003 European heatwave to climate change.
- **2021 · Manabe and Hasselmann** win the Nobel Prize in Physics.
- **2021–23 · The IPCC's Sixth Assessment Report** calls human influence “unequivocal”.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know climate science the way a strong first year of a university climate science course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain the greenhouse effect:** radiation, energy balance and feedbacks.
- **Explain the climate system:** atmosphere, oceans, ice and carbon.
- **Read past climates:** ice cores, sediments and other proxies.
- **Explain climate models:** how they work and how they are tested.
- **Weigh evidence and uncertainty:** extremes, attribution, sensitivity and tipping points.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Climate scientists from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.
