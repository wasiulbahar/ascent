# Latin American studies

Latin America and the Caribbean through their own voices: Indigenous civilisations and conquest, independence and nation-building, revolutions and dictatorships, great literature and ideas, and the region today. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Many classic texts and reports are free. The path puts Latin American and Caribbean writers and Indigenous voices at the centre, covers Spanish-, Portuguese-, French- and English-speaking countries, and presents contested history (such as revolutions and dictatorships) from several sides.

# About Latin American studies

Latin American studies explores the peoples, histories, cultures, politics and economies of Mexico, Central and South America and the Caribbean: great Indigenous civilisations, conquest and colonial rule, slavery and its legacies, independence, revolutions and dictatorships, extraordinary literature and music, and societies marked by both creativity and deep inequality.

This path begins with a famous novel, a classic polemic, a revolutionary's road trip and a concise history, studies the conquest from Indigenous and Spanish sources, follows independence and nation-building, explores the twentieth century, reads the region's great thinkers, and ends with Latin America today.

**Trivia**
- Haiti, after a revolution led by formerly enslaved people, became independent in 1804, the first Black republic and the second independent nation in the Americas.
- Brazil is the only Portuguese-speaking country in the Americas and has the largest population of African descent outside Africa.
- Hundreds of Indigenous languages are still spoken in Latin America, including Quechua, Guaraní, Nahuatl and many Mayan languages.
- Gabriel García Márquez, Pablo Neruda, Gabriela Mistral, Octavio Paz and Mario Vargas Llosa all won the Nobel Prize in Literature.

### Timeline
- **c. 250–900 CE · Classic Maya civilisation**
- **1325 · Tenochtitlan** is founded by the Mexica (Aztecs).
- **1492 · Columbus reaches the Caribbean**
- **1521 · Tenochtitlan falls** to Spanish and Indigenous allied forces.
- **1532 · Pizarro captures the Inca emperor Atahualpa**
- **1804 · Haiti's independence**
- **1810–1825 · Wars of independence** in Spanish America.
- **1888 · Brazil abolishes slavery** the last country in the Americas to do so.
- **1910 · The Mexican Revolution** begins.
- **1959 · The Cuban Revolution**
- **1973 · The coup in Chile** overthrows Salvador Allende.
- **1980s–90s · Return to democracy** across much of the region.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know Latin American studies the way a strong first year of a university Latin American studies course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain the region's long history:** Indigenous civilisations, conquest, colony and independence.
- **Explain the twentieth century:** revolutions, dictatorships, human rights and democracy.
- **Read great literature and ideas:** from the *Popol Vuh* to magical realism and liberation theology.
- **Analyse economy and society:** dependency, inequality, race and Indigenous rights.
- **Understand the region today:** its diversity, politics and environment, avoiding easy generalisations.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Scholars of Latin America and the Caribbean, especially scholars from the region, will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
