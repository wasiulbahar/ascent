# Human Resource Management

How organisations hire, develop, pay and look after people fairly: evidence-based recruitment, motivation, performance and pay, learning and careers, labour rights, health and wellbeing, and people analytics, hybrid work and AI. About 150 to 180 hours over six to eight months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free professional factsheets, ILO standards, research papers and courses cover the core. Employment law differs between countries, so the path teaches international standards and principles and points you to your own country's rules. It weighs evidence over fashionable HR trends.

# About human resource management

Human resource management (HRM) is how organisations attract, select, develop, reward and support the people who work for them, and how they handle the relationship between employers and workers.

This path starts with what HR does and the idea of decent work, teaches fair and evidence-based hiring, covers motivation, performance and pay, explores learning and careers, studies labour rights, harassment and wellbeing, and ends with evidence-based practice, analytics, hybrid work and AI in hiring.

**Trivia**
- The International Labour Organization, founded in 1919, is the only UN agency where governments, employers and workers all have a vote.
- In a famous US study, CVs with names common among white Americans received about 50% more callbacks than identical CVs with names common among Black Americans.
- A 2022 re-analysis found structured interviews were among the best predictors of job performance.
- The Hawthorne studies of the 1920s and 1930s helped turn management's attention to workers' social needs.

### Timeline
- **1911 · Frederick Taylor** publishes *The Principles of Scientific Management*.
- **1919 · The International Labour Organization** is founded.
- **1924–1932 · The Hawthorne studies** examine workers' productivity and social needs.
- **1943 · Abraham Maslow** proposes his hierarchy of needs.
- **1959 · Frederick Herzberg** describes motivators and hygiene factors.
- **1998 · The ILO** adopts its Declaration on Fundamental Principles and Rights at Work.
- **2019 · The ILO** adopts Convention 190 on violence and harassment at work.
- **2024 · The EU AI Act** treats AI used in hiring as high-risk.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will understand people management the way a strong HR course and early professional practice expect. You will be able to:

- **Hire fairly:** evidence-based selection, structured interviews and reducing bias.
- **Motivate and reward:** goals, feedback, performance management and fair pay.
- **Develop people:** training, evaluation, onboarding and careers.
- **Protect workers:** labour rights, harassment prevention, health and wellbeing.
- **Use evidence and technology well:** people analytics, hybrid work and AI, with fairness in mind.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. HR professionals and organisational psychologists from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages makes you a capable people professional. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Human Resource Management** certificate, you also complete:

1. **The grand quiz.** 40 game-style questions across all six stages. Pass mark 80%, with unlimited retakes.
2. **Hiring design.** Design a fair, evidence-based hiring process for a real role.
3. **Policy review.** Review an organisation's policy on harassment, wellbeing or pay against ILO and WHO guidance.
4. **Capstone report.** 2,500 words on one of the Stage 6 questions. Strong reports are read by an expert reviewer.
5. **Explain it.** Answer three unseen questions as if to a new manager. For example: “How do I give a fair performance review?”

The certificate needs every stage passed. It is not a legal or professional HR qualification.
