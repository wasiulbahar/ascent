# Astrophysics

The physics of the universe: how light reveals the cosmos, how stars live and die, black holes and gravitational waves, galaxies, dark matter and the expanding universe, and planets around other stars. About 220 to 270 hours over nine to eleven months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free courses, mission data, citizen science and Nobel materials cover the core. You need school physics and algebra; calculus helps from Stage 2. The Astronomy path is a gentler start; this path focuses on the physics behind what we observe.

# About astrophysics

Astrophysics uses physics to understand stars, galaxies, black holes and the universe itself. Almost everything we know comes from analysing light and other signals that have travelled across space for millions or billions of years.

This path begins with a university-style tour and citizen science, teaches how light and telescopes work, explains the lives of stars and the origin of elements, studies black holes and gravitational waves, explores galaxies, dark matter and cosmology, and ends with exoplanets and current research.

**Trivia**
- In 1925 Cecilia Payne showed stars are made mostly of hydrogen and helium, but was persuaded to call her result “almost certainly not real”. She was right.
- The first pulsar, found by Jocelyn Bell Burnell in 1967, was jokingly nicknamed LGM-1, for “little green men”.
- A teaspoon of neutron star material would weigh around a billion tonnes on Earth.
- The Event Horizon Telescope linked radio dishes across the planet to act like an Earth-sized telescope.

### Timeline
- **1859 · Kirchhoff and Bunsen** show spectral lines reveal chemical elements.
- **1915 · Albert Einstein** publishes general relativity.
- **1925 · Cecilia Payne** shows stars are mostly hydrogen and helium.
- **1929 · Edwin Hubble** finds the universe is expanding.
- **1939 · Hans Bethe** explains how stars shine by nuclear fusion.
- **1957 · Burbidge, Burbidge, Fowler and Hoyle** explain how stars make the elements.
- **1965 · Penzias and Wilson** detect the cosmic microwave background.
- **1967 · Jocelyn Bell Burnell** discovers pulsars.
- **1995 · Mayor and Queloz** find a planet orbiting a Sun-like star.
- **1998 · Two teams** discover the universe's expansion is accelerating.
- **2015 · LIGO** detects gravitational waves.
- **2019 · The Event Horizon Telescope** releases the first image of a black hole.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know astrophysics the way a strong undergraduate course expects. You will be able to:

- **Read light:** spectra, Doppler shifts and what telescopes across the spectrum reveal.
- **Explain stars:** fusion, the H–R diagram, stellar evolution and the origin of elements.
- **Explain compact objects:** white dwarfs, neutron stars, black holes and gravitational waves.
- **Explain cosmology:** galaxies, dark matter, the expanding universe and the cosmic background.
- **Explain exoplanets:** how they are found and what we know about them.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Astrophysicists and astronomy educators from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
