# Cell Biology

The life of cells: their structure and membranes, the molecular machines that copy genes, make proteins and harvest energy, signalling, division and death, stem cells and development, and the tools that let us see inside them. About 200 to 250 hours over eight to ten months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free open textbooks, Nobel Prize materials and research talks cover the core. You need school biology and chemistry; the Biology and Biochemistry paths help. The path includes the ethics of using human cells.

# About cell biology

Cell biology studies the cell, the basic unit of life. Every living thing is made of cells, and nearly every disease, from cancer to infection, starts with cells going wrong.

This path begins with the wonder of cells, studies their structure, membranes and organelles, explains their molecular machinery, follows the cell cycle and cell death, explores stem cells and development, and ends with the microscopes and maps that drive current research.

**Trivia**
- The human body contains roughly 30 to 37 trillion cells, plus a similar number of bacterial cells.
- Robert Hooke named “cells” in 1665 because cork under his microscope looked like the small rooms monks lived in.
- Mitochondria carry their own DNA, a clue that they descend from bacteria taken in by ancient cells.
- HeLa cells, taken without consent from Henrietta Lacks in 1951, are still grown in laboratories around the world.

### Timeline
- **1665 · Robert Hooke** names cells in *Micrographia*.
- **1670s · Antonie van Leeuwenhoek** sees single-celled organisms.
- **1838–1839 · Schleiden and Schwann** propose that all living things are made of cells.
- **1855 · Rudolf Virchow** argues every cell comes from a cell.
- **1931 · Ernst Ruska** helps build the first electron microscope.
- **1951 · HeLa cells** become the first human cells grown continuously.
- **1961 · Peter Mitchell** proposes how mitochondria make ATP.
- **1967 · Lynn Margulis** argues organelles came from engulfed bacteria.
- **2001 · Hartwell, Hunt and Nurse** win the Nobel Prize for cell cycle control.
- **2006 · Shinya Yamanaka** reprograms adult cells into stem cells.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know cell biology the way a strong undergraduate course expects. You will be able to:

- **Describe cells:** membranes, organelles, the cytoskeleton and how prokaryotes and eukaryotes differ.
- **Explain machinery:** gene expression, protein targeting, energy production and signalling.
- **Explain the life cycle of cells:** division, checkpoints, apoptosis and autophagy.
- **Explain stem cells and cancer:** development, reprogramming and the hallmarks of cancer.
- **Understand methods:** microscopy, cryo-electron microscopy, cell culture and its ethics.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Cell biologists and biology educators from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
