# Sustainable Development

How to improve lives now without wrecking the future: the Sustainable Development Goals and their history, poverty, health, education and gender, climate, energy, food and nature, cities, finance and governance, and the big debates about growth and justice. About 150 to 180 hours over six to eight months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free UN, World Bank and research reports and data cover nearly everything. No background is needed. The path presents evidence from every region and gives a fair hearing to competing views, from green growth to degrowth.

# About sustainable development

Sustainable development means meeting the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own. It links ending poverty and improving health and education with protecting the climate and nature.

This path starts with the global goals and the data behind them, traces the ideas from the 1970s to today, studies poverty, health, education and gender, examines climate, energy, food and biodiversity, covers cities, finance, Indigenous rights and measuring progress, and ends with debates about growth, justice and what works.

**Trivia**
- The Brundtland Report of 1987 gave the most widely used definition of sustainable development.
- The Sustainable Development Goals, agreed in 2015, have 17 goals and 169 targets to reach by 2030.
- The share of people in extreme poverty fell from over a third in 1990 to under a tenth by the late 2010s.
- The Human Development Index was created in 1990 by Mahbub ul Haq, working with Amartya Sen.

### Timeline
- **1972 · The Stockholm Conference** puts the environment on the UN agenda; *The Limits to Growth* is published.
- **1987 · The Brundtland Report** defines sustainable development.
- **1990 · The UNDP** publishes the first Human Development Report.
- **1992 · The Rio Earth Summit** adopts conventions on climate and biodiversity.
- **2000 · The Millennium Development Goals** are agreed.
- **2009 · Scientists** propose planetary boundaries.
- **2015 · The SDGs and the Paris Agreement** are adopted.
- **2022 · Countries** agree a biodiversity framework and a loss and damage fund.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will understand sustainable development the way a strong university course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain the framework:** the SDGs, their history and how progress is measured.
- **Analyse human development:** poverty, health, education and gender equality.
- **Analyse environmental limits:** climate, energy, food systems and biodiversity.
- **Explain how change happens:** cities, finance, governance and Indigenous rights.
- **Weigh debates:** green growth, degrowth and climate justice, with evidence.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Development and sustainability experts from more than one region will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
