# Toxicology

The science of poisons and safe doses: how chemicals enter and harm the body, testing and risk assessment, pollutants, pesticides and drugs, famous poisonings and disasters, and how regulation protects people and nature. About 120 to 150 hours over five to six months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free WHO, agency and open-textbook resources cover the core. You need school biology and chemistry. This path is for understanding, not for handling or testing chemicals; in a poisoning, contact emergency services or a poison centre immediately.

# About toxicology

Toxicology studies how chemicals, from medicines and pesticides to metals and natural toxins, harm living things, and at what doses. Its central idea is that the dose makes the poison.

This path begins with the history of poisons and the dose–response idea, explains how bodies absorb, process and remove chemicals, covers testing and risk assessment, studies pollutants, pesticides, metals and drugs, examines disasters that shaped regulation, and ends with new methods and research.

**Trivia**
- Paracelsus wrote in the 16th century that “the dose makes the poison”: everything is toxic in large enough amounts.
- Mercury pollution in Minamata, Japan, poisoned thousands of people in the 1950s and gave its name to a global mercury treaty.
- Rachel Carson's *Silent Spring* (1962) helped lead to bans on DDT for agricultural use in many countries.
- Lead was added to petrol for decades; the last country stopped selling leaded petrol for cars in 2021.

### Timeline
- **c. 1538 · Paracelsus** argues the dose makes the poison.
- **1813 · Mathieu Orfila** founds modern toxicology with his treatise on poisons.
- **1956 · Minamata disease** is officially identified in Japan.
- **1962 · Rachel Carson** publishes *Silent Spring*.
- **1984 · The Bhopal gas leak** kills thousands.
- **2001 · The Stockholm Convention** targets persistent organic pollutants.
- **2007 · The EU's REACH law** requires safety data for chemicals.
- **2013 · The Minamata Convention** is agreed to cut mercury pollution.
- **2021 · Leaded petrol** is ended worldwide.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will understand toxicology the way a strong introductory university course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain dose and response:** thresholds, LD50 and why the dose makes the poison.
- **Explain how bodies handle chemicals:** absorption, distribution, metabolism and excretion.
- **Explain testing and risk:** hazard versus risk, exposure assessment and safety limits.
- **Explain major toxicants:** metals, pesticides, air pollutants, drugs and natural toxins.
- **Discuss regulation:** lessons from disasters, international treaties and new testing methods.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Toxicologists and public health experts from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
