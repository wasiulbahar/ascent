# Religion

The world's religions and how scholars study them: beliefs, practices, scriptures and communities across many traditions, the great theories, non-religion, and religion in today's world. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Many scriptures and classic studies are free. This path studies religion as scholars do: it describes and compares traditions fairly, including non-religious worldviews, and **does not argue for or against any faith**. Where possible, traditions are introduced by writers from within them.

# About the study of religion

The study of religion looks at the beliefs, practices, scriptures, rituals, ethics and communities of the world's religious traditions, and at non-religious worldviews too. It uses history, anthropology, sociology, psychology and philosophy, and tries to understand each tradition on its own terms before comparing.

This path begins with books that survey the world's religions, teaches how scholars study religion, introduces the Abrahamic traditions, then Asian, African and Indigenous traditions, reads the great theories and the critics of religion, and ends with religion and non-religion in today's world.

**Trivia**
- About 84% of the world's people identified with a religious group in 2010, according to the Pew Research Center; about 16% did not.
- The *Rigveda*, among the oldest religious texts still in use, was composed in early Sanskrit more than 3,000 years ago.
- Indonesia has the largest Muslim population of any country.
- The philosopher Karl Jaspers called the period around 800–200 BCE, when many great traditions took shape, the “Axial Age”.

### Timeline
- **c. 1500–1200 BCE · The *Rigveda*** is composed.
- **c. 6th–5th century BCE · The Buddha** and the rise of Buddhism and Jainism in India.
- **c. 6th–5th century BCE · Confucius and Laozi** in China, as tradition records.
- **c. 30 CE · Jesus of Nazareth**
- **610–632 CE · The Qur'an's revelation** to Muhammad, in Muslim belief.
- **1517 · Luther's theses** and the Protestant Reformation.
- **1893 · The World's Parliament of Religions** in Chicago.
- **1912 · Durkheim's *Elementary Forms of Religious Life***
- **1962–65 · The Second Vatican Council**
- **2010s · The religiously unaffiliated** grow in many countries while religion grows in others.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know the study of religion the way a strong first year of a university religious-studies course expects. You will be able to:

- **Describe the major traditions:** their beliefs, practices, texts and communities, fairly and accurately.
- **Use scholarly tools:** dimensions of religion, the sacred and the profane, and comparison.
- **Read key texts:** from several scriptures and classic studies.
- **Weigh the theories:** Durkheim, Weber, James, Hume and the critics of religion.
- **Understand religion today:** growth and decline, secularism, conflict and coexistence.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Scholars of religion, including members of several traditions and non-religious scholars, will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
