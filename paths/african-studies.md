# African studies

Africa's history, ideas and present, told as much as possible by Africans: great literature, deep history and medieval empires, slavery, colonialism and independence, African philosophy and thought, politics and economy, and Africa today. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Classic texts, African Union documents and survey data are free. Africa has 54 countries and thousands of languages and cultures, so the path avoids treating it as one place, and puts African writers and scholars at the centre while including outside views and debates.

# About African studies

African studies is the study of Africa's peoples, histories, societies, cultures, languages, politics and economies. It covers the continent where humanity began, its ancient and medieval states, the Atlantic slave trade and colonial rule, independence movements, African literature and philosophy, and the continent's diverse present, with the youngest population of any region.

This path begins with novels, memoir and history that open the subject, explores Africa's deep past and medieval empires, studies slavery, colonialism and independence, reads African philosophy and literature, examines politics and development debates, and ends with Africa today: trade, climate, the arts and the future.

**Trivia**
- Africa has 54 countries and between 1,500 and 2,000 languages.
- Mansa Musa of Mali made a famous pilgrimage to Mecca in 1324, spending so much gold in Cairo that its value fell there for years.
- Ghana in 1957 became one of the first sub-Saharan African colonies to win independence, inspiring movements across the continent.
- Africa has the world's youngest population: the median age is under twenty.

### Timeline
- **c. 300,000 years ago · *Homo sapiens*** in Africa.
- **c. 3100 BCE · Ancient Egypt** is unified.
- **c. 1st–7th century CE · The kingdom of Aksum** in today's Ethiopia and Eritrea.
- **c. 11th century · Great Zimbabwe** begins to rise.
- **1324 · Mansa Musa's pilgrimage** from Mali.
- **16th–19th century · The Atlantic slave trade** About 12 million people shipped across the Atlantic.
- **1884–85 · The Berlin Conference** European powers divide Africa.
- **1957 · Ghana's independence**
- **1963 · The Organisation of African Unity** is founded.
- **1994 · The end of apartheid** in South Africa.
- **2021 · The African Continental Free Trade Area** begins trading.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know African studies the way a strong first year of a university African-studies course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain Africa's long history:** from human origins and ancient states to medieval empires.
- **Explain slavery, colonialism and independence:** and their lasting effects.
- **Read African literature and thought:** novels, philosophy and political writing.
- **Analyse politics and development:** states, aid, democracy and economic debates.
- **Avoid the single story:** understanding the diversity of 54 countries and their peoples.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Scholars of Africa, especially Africa-based scholars, will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
