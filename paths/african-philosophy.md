# African Philosophy

Philosophy from Africa and its diaspora: ancient Egyptian and Ethiopian thought, oral traditions and sage philosophy, the debate over ethnophilosophy, Ubuntu and communal ethics, personhood, decolonising the mind, Africana political philosophy, and contemporary African philosophers. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Encyclopedia entries, many articles and some classic texts are free online. The path centres African philosophers and their debates with each other, covers the continent's diversity and the diaspora, and presents disagreements fairly.

# About African philosophy

African philosophy is the philosophical thought of African peoples and the African diaspora, written and oral, ancient and modern. It asks questions about personhood, community, knowledge, God, freedom, justice and what it means to decolonise thought.

This path begins with accessible introductions and the debate over what African philosophy is, studies ancient and classical sources, examines ethnophilosophy, sage philosophy and their critics, explores Ubuntu, personhood and ethics, follows political philosophy and decolonisation, and ends with contemporary African philosophers in global debate.

**Trivia**
- The Ethiopian philosopher Zera Yacob wrote the *Hatata* in 1667, a rational inquiry into God and ethics.
- Kwasi Wiredu, a Ghanaian philosopher, argued for “conceptual decolonisation”: rethinking ideas inherited in colonial languages.
- Ubuntu is often summed up by the saying “a person is a person through other persons” (umuntu ngumuntu ngabantu).
- Anton Wilhelm Amo, born in present-day Ghana, taught philosophy at German universities in the 1730s.

### Timeline
- **c. 2400 BCE · The Instruction of Ptahhotep** an Egyptian wisdom text.
- **1667 · Zera Yacob's *Hatata*** is written in Ethiopia.
- **1734 · Anton Wilhelm Amo** completes his philosophy dissertation in Germany.
- **1945 · Placide Tempels's *Bantu Philosophy*** sparks the ethnophilosophy debate.
- **1960s · Nkrumah, Nyerere and Senghor** develop African political thought.
- **1976 · Paulin Hountondji's *African Philosophy: Myth and Reality*** criticises ethnophilosophy.
- **1980s · Henry Odera Oruka** develops sage philosophy.
- **1986 · Ngũgĩ wa Thiong'o's *Decolonising the Mind*** argues for African languages.
- **1996 · Kwasi Wiredu's *Cultural Universals and Particulars*** calls for conceptual decolonisation.
- **1996–2003 · South Africa's Truth and Reconciliation Commission** draws on Ubuntu.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know African philosophy the way a strong first year of a university course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain the debate over African philosophy:** ethnophilosophy, sage philosophy and professional philosophy.
- **Read key thinkers:** from Zera Yacob to Wiredu, Gyekye, Oruka and Hountondji.
- **Explain Ubuntu and communitarian ethics:** and debates about personhood.
- **Discuss African political philosophy:** nationalism, socialism, decolonisation and democracy.
- **Bring African philosophy into global dialogue:** on ethics, knowledge and justice.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. African philosophers from several regions of the continent and the diaspora will confirm them before the path is published.
