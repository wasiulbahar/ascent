# Fashion

Clothes as art, industry and identity: dress history across the world, textiles and making, the great designers, how the fashion system works, fashion and identity, and the urgent questions of labour, waste and sustainability. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Museum collections such as the Met's Costume Institute and the V&A are free online, as are many industry reports. The path covers dress traditions from every continent, not just Paris and New York, and gives full weight to garment workers and the environmental costs of fashion.

# About fashion

Fashion studies looks at what people wear and why: clothing as craft and art, as a global industry employing tens of millions, and as a way people express identity, status, belief and belonging.

This path begins with ways into fashion history, studies textiles and garment making, follows dress history across the world, explores designers and the fashion system, examines fashion, identity and culture, and ends with labour, sustainability and the future of fashion.

**Trivia**
- The fashion industry employs tens of millions of people worldwide, most of them women in garment production.
- The Rana Plaza factory collapse in Bangladesh in 2013 killed over 1,100 garment workers.
- Indigo dye has been used for at least 6,000 years; the oldest known indigo-dyed cloth, from Peru, is about 6,000 years old.
- An estimated 92 million tonnes of textile waste are produced each year.

### Timeline
- **c. 4000 BCE · Indigo-dyed cotton** is made in Peru.
- **c. 2nd century BCE · The Silk Road** carries Chinese silk west.
- **1700s · Indian cotton chintz** sweeps European fashion.
- **1858 · Charles Frederick Worth** opens the first couture house in Paris.
- **1920s · Coco Chanel** popularises simpler, freer women's clothes.
- **1947 · Dior's New Look** revives full skirts after the war.
- **1960s · Youth and street styles** challenge couture.
- **1980s · Japanese designers** such as Rei Kawakubo transform Paris.
- **2000s · Fast fashion** grows rapidly.
- **2013 · Rana Plaza** collapses, prompting the Fashion Revolution movement.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know fashion the way a strong first year of a university fashion studies course expects. You will be able to:

- **Read clothes:** fabric, cut, construction and meaning.
- **Trace dress history:** across periods and continents.
- **Explain the fashion system:** design, production, retail and media.
- **Discuss fashion and identity:** gender, class, religion, subculture and power.
- **Weigh fashion's costs:** labour rights, waste and sustainability.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Fashion historians, designers and researchers from more than one region will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages makes you a capable student of fashion. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Fashion** certificate, you also complete:

1. **The grand quiz.** 40 game-style questions across all six stages. Pass mark 80%, with unlimited retakes.
2. **Object analysis.** Analyse an unseen museum garment: material, construction, date and meaning.
3. **Supply chain study.** Trace a garment's supply chain and its social and environmental costs.
4. **Capstone essay.** 2,500 words on one of the Stage 6 questions. Strong essays are read by an expert reviewer.
5. **Explain it.** Answer three unseen questions as if to a curious friend. For example: “Why do fashions change?”

The certificate needs every stage passed.
