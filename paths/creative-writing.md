# Creative writing

Learning to write fiction, poetry, memoir and scripts: the writing habit, reading like a writer, the craft of story and verse, other forms, and the life and business of writing. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months, mostly spent writing.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free courses, magazines and poetry resources cover much of the path, and the most important tool, a notebook, costs almost nothing. Every stage asks you to write. Writers and forms from many traditions are included.

# About creative writing

Creative writing is the art of making stories, poems, plays, scripts and literary non-fiction. It is learned by reading closely, writing often, sharing work with others and revising hard. Its craft includes voice, character, plot, point of view, imagery, rhythm and form.

This path begins with writers on the writing life, teaches how to read like a writer, covers the craft of fiction, then poetry, then other forms such as screenwriting and memoir, and ends with revision, the writing life and getting published.

**Trivia**
- Ernest Hemingway said he rewrote the ending of *A Farewell to Arms* 39 times.
- The ghazal, a poetic form from Arabic and Persian, became loved in Urdu and later in English through poets like Agha Shahid Ali.
- E. M. Forster distinguished story from plot: “The king died and then the queen died” is a story; “the queen died of grief” makes a plot.
- Many famous novels were rejected many times before publication.

### Timeline
- **c. 1000 · Murasaki Shikibu's *The Tale of Genji*** often called the first novel.
- **1903–08 · Rilke's *Letters to a Young Poet***
- **1918 · Strunk's *The Elements of Style*** privately printed.
- **1927 · Forster's *Aspects of the Novel***
- **1936 · The Iowa Writers' Workshop** The first creative-writing degree programme.
- **1949 · Campbell's *The Hero with a Thousand Faces***
- **1994 · Lamott's *Bird by Bird***
- **2000s · Online writing communities** and self-publishing grow.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will write with the craft a strong first year of a university creative-writing course expects. You will be able to:

- **Keep a writing practice:** daily habits, notebooks and finishing drafts.
- **Read like a writer:** noticing how published writers make their effects.
- **Write fiction:** character, point of view, scene, dialogue and structure.
- **Write poetry:** image, sound, line, metre and fixed forms from several traditions.
- **Revise and share:** giving and using feedback, and knowing how publishing works.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Writers and writing teachers from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages makes you a capable creative writer. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Creative writing** certificate, you also complete:

1. **The grand quiz.** 40 game-style questions across all six stages. Pass mark 80%, with unlimited retakes.
2. **Portfolio.** A portfolio of revised work: a short story, five poems and one other form.
3. **Close reading.** Analyse the craft of an unseen short passage.
4. **Capstone essay.** 2,500 words on one of the Stage 6 questions. Strong essays are read by an expert reviewer.
5. **Explain it.** Answer three unseen questions as if to a curious friend. For example: “What does “show, don't tell” really mean?”

The certificate needs every stage passed.
