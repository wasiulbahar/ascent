# Physiotherapy and Rehabilitation

How people recover and keep moving: movement science, pain, back pain and arthritis, sports injuries, stroke and lung and heart rehabilitation, care across life and in communities, and evidence-based practice. About 150 to 180 hours over six to eight months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free WHO, professional and research resources, including the PEDro evidence database, cover the core. You need school biology; the Anatomy and physiology path helps. This path is for understanding, not treatment: see a qualified health professional about your own injuries or pain.

# About physiotherapy

Physiotherapy (physical therapy) helps people move, function and live well despite injury, illness, disability or ageing, using exercise, education, manual therapy and assistive devices.

This path begins with what physiotherapy is and why rehabilitation matters globally, studies movement science and pain, covers back pain, arthritis and sports injuries, explores stroke, spinal cord, lung and heart rehabilitation, looks at care for children, older people and communities, and ends with evidence, telerehabilitation and research.

**Trivia**
- An estimated 2.4 billion people worldwide live with a condition that could benefit from rehabilitation.
- Low back pain is the leading cause of disability worldwide.
- Per Henrik Ling founded an institute in Stockholm in 1813 that taught medical gymnastics, an early root of physiotherapy.
- The PEDro database lists tens of thousands of physiotherapy trials and reviews, free for anyone to search.

### Timeline
- **1813 · Per Henrik Ling** founds the Royal Central Institute of Gymnastics in Stockholm.
- **1894 · British nurses** found a society that becomes the Chartered Society of Physiotherapy.
- **1916 · Polio epidemics** drive the growth of physical therapy.
- **1951 · The World Confederation for Physical Therapy** is founded.
- **1999 · PEDro** launches as a free physiotherapy evidence database.
- **2010 · The WHO** publishes guidelines on community-based rehabilitation.
- **2017 · The WHO** launches Rehabilitation 2030.
- **2020 · The International Association for the Study of Pain** revises its definition of pain.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will understand physiotherapy the way a strong introductory course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain movement:** muscles, joints, exercise physiology, motor learning and gait.
- **Explain pain:** modern pain science and why pain doesn't always mean damage.
- **Explain musculoskeletal care:** back pain, osteoarthritis and sports injuries, using evidence.
- **Explain neurological and cardiorespiratory rehab:** stroke, spinal cord injury, lungs and heart.
- **Discuss systems and evidence:** community rehabilitation, assistive products and evidence-based practice.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Physiotherapists and rehabilitation experts from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages gives you a strong understanding of physiotherapy. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Physiotherapy and Rehabilitation** certificate, you also complete:

1. **The grand quiz.** 40 game-style questions across all six stages. Pass mark 80%, with unlimited retakes.
2. **Evidence summary.** Summarise the best evidence on one condition using PEDro.
3. **Programme design.** Design an exercise or rehabilitation programme for a group, with safety and progression.
4. **Capstone essay.** 2,500 words on one of the Stage 6 questions. Strong essays are read by an expert reviewer.
5. **Explain it.** Answer three unseen questions as if to a patient. For example: “Should I rest my sore back?”

The certificate needs every stage passed. It is not a clinical qualification.
