# Plant Biology

The science of plants: their structure and photosynthesis, how they sense, grow and reproduce, their evolution and diversity, how people domesticated and depend on them, and their future in a changing climate. About 180 to 220 hours over seven to nine months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free textbooks, botanic garden science, Nobel materials and plant identification apps cover the core. You need school biology. Get outside: identifying and observing plants near you is part of every stage. The path includes Indigenous and traditional plant knowledge alongside laboratory science.

# About plant biology

Plant biology, or botany, studies plants: how they are built, how they capture energy from sunlight, how they sense their surroundings and reproduce, how they evolved, and how they feed, heal and shelter people.

This path starts with ways of seeing and naming plants, studies structure and photosynthesis, explores hormones, senses and reproduction, traces evolution and diversity, examines domestication, famine and medicine, and ends with plants in a changing climate and current research.

**Trivia**
- Theophrastus, a student of Aristotle, wrote some of the first books on plants around 300 BCE and is often called the father of botany.
- The first plant genome to be fully sequenced, in 2000, was that of the small weed *Arabidopsis thaliana*.
- Kew scientists estimated in 2020 that around two in five plant species may be threatened with extinction.
- The Svalbard Global Seed Vault, opened in 2008 in Arctic permafrost, holds backup copies of seeds from gene banks worldwide.

### Timeline
- **c. 300 BCE · Theophrastus** writes early studies of plants.
- **1665 · Robert Hooke** sees “cells” in cork.
- **1753 · Carl Linnaeus** publishes *Species Plantarum*, naming plants with two words.
- **1779 · Jan Ingenhousz** shows plants need light to purify air.
- **1865 · Gregor Mendel** reports his experiments with pea plants.
- **1880 · Charles and Francis Darwin** publish *The Power of Movement in Plants*.
- **1926 · Nikolai Vavilov** proposes centres of origin of crop plants.
- **1950s · Melvin Calvin's team** maps the carbon fixation cycle.
- **2000 · *Arabidopsis thaliana*** becomes the first plant genome sequenced.
- **2008 · The Svalbard Global Seed Vault** opens.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know plant biology the way a strong undergraduate course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain plant structure:** cells, tissues, roots, stems, leaves and transport.
- **Explain photosynthesis:** light reactions, carbon fixation and adaptations to heat and drought.
- **Explain growth and reproduction:** hormones, senses, flowers, pollination and seeds.
- **Explain diversity:** plant evolution, classification and threats to plants.
- **Connect plants and people:** domestication, food security, disease, medicine and climate.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Botanists and plant scientists from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages makes you a capable student of plant biology. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Plant Biology** certificate, you also complete:

1. **The grand quiz.** 40 game-style questions across all six stages. Pass mark 80%, with unlimited retakes.
2. **Field journal.** Identify and describe 30 plants near you, with photos and notes.
3. **Experiment.** Run a simple plant experiment, such as light or water stress, and report results.
4. **Capstone essay.** 2,500 words on one of the Stage 6 questions. Strong essays are read by an expert reviewer.
5. **Explain it.** Answer three unseen questions as if to a friend. For example: “How does a tree drink water from its roots to its top?”

The certificate needs every stage passed.
