# Energy

How the world is powered: the physics of energy, fossil fuels and their history, electricity grids, renewables and nuclear power, and the economics, politics and justice of the energy transition. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. International agencies publish free data and reports, and a classic free book does the sums. The path presents debates about nuclear power, the speed of the transition and who pays fairly, and includes views from countries still building basic access to energy.

# About energy

Energy studies how societies produce, move and use energy: from coal, oil and gas to sunlight, wind, water and the atom. It combines physics and engineering with economics, politics and the environment, and sits at the centre of the climate challenge.

This path begins with lively books on energy's role in history, teaches the physics and the numbers, explains fossil fuels and electricity, explores renewables and nuclear power, and ends with the economics, politics and fairness of the energy transition.

**Trivia**
- About 80% of the world's primary energy still came from fossil fuels in the early 2020s.
- In 2023, around 685 million people still lived without electricity, most of them in sub-Saharan Africa.
- The price of solar panels fell by about 90% in the 2010s.
- One kilogram of uranium in a nuclear reactor can release roughly as much energy as tens of tonnes of coal.

### Timeline
- **1712 · Newcomen's steam engine** pumps water from mines.
- **1859 · The first commercial oil well** is drilled in Pennsylvania.
- **1882 · Edison's Pearl Street Station** supplies electricity in New York.
- **1954 · The first nuclear power plant** connects to a grid, in the Soviet Union.
- **1954 · The modern silicon solar cell** is invented at Bell Labs.
- **1973 · The oil crisis** shocks the world economy.
- **1986 · Chernobyl** the worst nuclear accident.
- **2011 · Fukushima** a nuclear accident after a tsunami.
- **2015 · The Paris Agreement** on climate change.
- **2023 · COP28** calls for transitioning away from fossil fuels.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know energy the way a strong first year of a university energy studies course expects. You will be able to:

- **Do the sums:** units, power and energy, and the scale of demand.
- **Explain energy sources:** fossil fuels, renewables and nuclear, with their strengths and limits.
- **Explain electricity systems:** grids, storage and balancing supply with demand.
- **Read energy data:** using international statistics and scenarios.
- **Weigh the transition:** cost, speed, security and fairness.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Energy engineers, economists and policy researchers from more than one region will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
