# Risk Management

How organisations and societies understand and handle risk: thinking clearly about chance, identifying and assessing risks, financial and enterprise risk, safety and why systems fail, global and systemic risks from pandemics to cyber attacks, and communicating and deciding under uncertainty. About 120 to 150 hours over five to six months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free standards summaries, official accident reports and global risk reports cover the core. You need school maths; the Probability path helps. The path draws on finance, engineering, health and disaster management, and weighs the limits of risk tools as well as their uses.

# About risk management

Risk management is the practice of identifying what could go wrong (or right), judging how likely and serious it is, and deciding what to do about it. Banks, hospitals, airlines, governments and families all manage risk.

This path starts with clear thinking about risk and uncertainty, teaches tools for identifying and assessing risks, covers financial and enterprise risk, studies accidents and safe organisations, examines global risks from disasters, pandemics and cyber attacks, and ends with communicating risk and deciding under deep uncertainty.

**Trivia**
- Lloyd's of London began around 1688 in Edward Lloyd's coffee house, where merchants met to insure ships.
- In 1921 the economist Frank Knight distinguished measurable “risk” from unmeasurable “uncertainty”.
- A micromort is a one-in-a-million chance of death, a unit used to compare everyday risks.
- James Reason's “Swiss cheese model” shows accidents happen when holes in several layers of defence line up.

### Timeline
- **1654 · Pascal and Fermat** lay the foundations of probability.
- **c. 1688 · Lloyd's** begins in a London coffee house.
- **1921 · Frank Knight** distinguishes risk from uncertainty.
- **1952 · Harry Markowitz** shows how diversification reduces portfolio risk.
- **1984 · Charles Perrow** publishes *Normal Accidents*; the Bhopal disaster occurs.
- **1986 · The Challenger and Chernobyl disasters** expose organisational failures.
- **1988 · The first Basel Accord** sets bank capital rules.
- **2008 · The global financial crisis** reveals hidden systemic risk.
- **2009 · ISO 31000** sets a global standard for risk management.
- **2020 · COVID-19** tests pandemic preparedness worldwide.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will manage risk the way a capable risk professional does. You will be able to:

- **Think clearly about risk:** probability, consequences, uncertainty and bias.
- **Use risk tools:** registers, bowties, FMEA and their limits.
- **Explain financial and enterprise risk:** diversification, capital rules, insurance and frameworks.
- **Explain why systems fail:** normal accidents, high reliability and accident investigations.
- **Handle systemic risk and decisions:** disasters, pandemics, cyber risk, communication and precaution.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Risk professionals and educators from finance, engineering and public health in more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
