# Engineering

The big ideas behind every branch of engineering: how things are designed, why they stand up or fall down, how systems are controlled, and what engineers owe society. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free open textbooks cover the maths and physics, and several classic reports are free. This path teaches knowledge about engineering: **it is not a qualification to practise as an engineer**, which needs an accredited degree and professional registration.

# About engineering

Engineering uses science, mathematics and judgement to design things that work: bridges, engines, circuits, chemical plants, software and medical devices. Unlike pure science, it must deliver something useful within limits of cost, time, safety and materials, often before everything is known.

This path begins with books about what engineers do and how things fail, teaches the shared tools of maths, mechanics and design, tours the main branches, moves to systems and control, weighs engineering's place in society, and ends with today's frontiers: chips, materials and safety in complex systems.

**Trivia**
- The Pont du Gard aqueduct in France, built by the Romans in the first century CE, drops only about 2.5 centimetres in height for every 100 metres of length.
- The 1940 collapse of the Tacoma Narrows Bridge in the United States, filmed as it twisted in the wind, changed how engineers design bridges for wind.
- The first transistor was built at Bell Labs in 1947; a modern phone chip holds billions of them.
- Engineers in the Netherlands manage a country where about a quarter of the land lies below sea level.

### Timeline
- **c. 2560 BCE · The Great Pyramid of Giza** Precise engineering in stone.
- **1st century CE · Roman aqueducts** Water carried over long distances by gentle slopes.
- **1712 · Newcomen's steam engine** pumps water from mines.
- **1825 · The Stockton and Darlington Railway** opens in England.
- **1879 · Edison's lamp** Practical electric lighting.
- **1947 · The transistor** is invented at Bell Labs.
- **1969 · Apollo 11** lands people on the Moon.
- **1986 · The Challenger disaster** and the lessons of engineering ethics.
- **2010 · The Burj Khalifa** opens in Dubai, the world's tallest building.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know engineering the way a strong first year of a general engineering degree expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain the engineering method:** designing under limits, with rules of thumb, models and testing.
- **Use the core ideas:** forces, stress, energy, circuits and feedback, at an introductory level.
- **Compare the branches:** civil, mechanical, electrical, chemical and systems engineering.
- **Learn from failure:** how disasters happen in complex systems and how to prevent them.
- **Weigh the ethics:** safety, honesty and engineering's effects on society.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Engineers and engineering teachers from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published. Knowledge only: never a licence to practise.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages makes you a capable student of engineering. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Engineering** certificate, you also complete:
