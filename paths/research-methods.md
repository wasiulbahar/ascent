# Research Methods

How to find things out reliably, in any field: reading research critically, designing studies, sampling and ethics, quantitative and qualitative methods, the replication crisis and open science, and reviewing and sharing evidence. About 150 to 180 hours over six to eight months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free textbooks, ethics codes, reporting guidelines and open science tools cover nearly everything. You need school maths; the Statistics path helps for Stage 3. The path treats quantitative and qualitative research as equally serious, and you plan a small study of your own along the way.

# About research methods

Research methods are the tools for answering questions with evidence: choosing a design, collecting and analysing data, and drawing honest conclusions. They are shared across the sciences, social sciences, health, education and business.

This path begins with reading research critically, teaches research design, sampling and ethics, covers statistics and causal inference, explores interviews, ethnography and case studies, confronts the replication crisis and open science, and ends with systematic reviews, data management and research integrity.

**Trivia**
- Ronald Fisher's “lady tasting tea” experiment, testing whether a woman could tell if milk was poured first, helped found modern experimental design.
- A 2015 attempt to repeat 100 psychology studies reproduced fewer than half of the original results.
- The Belmont Report on research ethics was written after the Tuskegee syphilis study, in which treatment was withheld from Black men for decades.
- In 2016 the American Statistical Association issued its first-ever statement on a statistical practice: the use of p-values.

### Timeline
- **1620 · Francis Bacon** argues for knowledge built from careful observation.
- **1747 · James Lind** runs a comparative trial on scurvy.
- **1935 · Ronald Fisher** publishes *The Design of Experiments*.
- **1964 · The Declaration of Helsinki** sets ethics for medical research.
- **1979 · The Belmont Report** states principles of respect, beneficence and justice.
- **1993 · The Cochrane Collaboration** is founded to produce systematic reviews.
- **2005 · John Ioannidis** argues most published research findings are false.
- **2010 · The Singapore Statement** sets global principles of research integrity.
- **2015 · The Reproducibility Project** reports on repeating 100 psychology studies.
- **2016 · The FAIR principles** set standards for sharing research data.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will design and judge research the way a strong research methods course expects. You will be able to:

- **Read research critically:** spot strong and weak designs, bias and spin.
- **Design studies:** questions, designs, sampling, measurement and ethics.
- **Analyse data:** descriptive and inferential statistics, effect sizes and causal inference.
- **Use qualitative methods:** interviews, thematic analysis, ethnography and case studies.
- **Practise open, rigorous research:** preregistration, reproducibility, systematic reviews and integrity.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Researchers and methods teachers from several disciplines and countries will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages makes you a capable researcher. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Research Methods** certificate, you also complete:

1. **The grand quiz.** 40 game-style questions across all six stages. Pass mark 80%, with unlimited retakes.
2. **Critical appraisal.** Appraise two published studies, one quantitative and one qualitative.
3. **Study protocol.** Write a preregistered protocol for a small study, with ethics and analysis plan.
4. **Capstone report.** Carry out the small study and report it in 2,500 words. Strong reports are read by an expert reviewer.
5. **Explain it.** Answer three unseen questions as if to a friend. For example: “How do I know if a study is any good?”

The certificate needs every stage passed.
