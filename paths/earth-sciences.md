# Earth sciences

The planet itself: rocks and deep time, plate tectonics, volcanoes and earthquakes, Earth's 4.5-billion-year history and mass extinctions, resources and hazards, and the debate over the Anthropocene. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. A free textbook, free courses and live global data on earthquakes and volcanoes cover much of the path. The rocks outside your door are free too: look at walls, cliffs and riverbeds.

# About earth sciences

Earth sciences study how the planet works and how it came to be: the rocks and minerals it is made of, the moving plates of its surface, the volcanoes and earthquakes they cause, its oceans and atmosphere, and the 4.5-billion-year story written in its layers. They explain where resources come from and how to live with natural hazards.

This path begins with books that make geology vivid, teaches rocks, minerals and deep time, explains plate tectonics, earthquakes and volcanoes, follows Earth's history and its mass extinctions, covers resources and hazards, and ends at the frontier: the Anthropocene debate, critical minerals and the geology of Mars.

**Trivia**
- The oldest known mineral grains, zircons from the Jack Hills in Western Australia, are about 4.4 billion years old.
- The Himalayas are still rising by a few millimetres a year as India pushes into Asia.
- The 1815 eruption of Tambora in Indonesia cooled the world so much that 1816 became “the year without a summer”.
- In 2024 geologists voted not to declare a new “Anthropocene” epoch, though the idea remains widely used.

### Timeline
- **c. 4.54 billion years ago · Earth forms**
- **c. 252 million years ago · The end-Permian extinction** The greatest mass extinction.
- **c. 66 million years ago · The asteroid impact** that ended the age of dinosaurs.
- **1788 · James Hutton** Deep time: “no vestige of a beginning, no prospect of an end”.
- **1815 · William Smith's geological map** of England and Wales.
- **1830–33 · Lyell's *Principles of Geology***
- **1912–15 · Wegener** proposes continental drift.
- **1960s · Plate tectonics** is accepted.
- **1980 · The Alvarez hypothesis** An asteroid killed the dinosaurs.
- **2004 · The Indian Ocean earthquake and tsunami**

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know the earth sciences the way a strong first year of a university geology degree expects. You will be able to:

- **Identify rocks and minerals:** and explain how they form in the rock cycle.
- **Read deep time:** layers, fossils and dating, across 4.5 billion years.
- **Explain plate tectonics:** and the earthquakes, volcanoes and mountains it causes.
- **Explain Earth's history:** ice ages, mass extinctions and how life and rock shaped each other.
- **Weigh resources and hazards:** water, minerals, energy and disaster risk.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Geologists and earth scientists from more than one continent will confirm them before the path is published.
