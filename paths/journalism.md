# Journalism and media

How news is found, checked and told: reporting and verification, investigations and data journalism, media and power, press freedom around the world, and the future of news. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Handbooks, investigations and press-freedom data are free online. The path draws on journalism from many countries and presents both defenders and critics of the media.

# About journalism and media

Journalism gathers, checks and reports information that people need to understand their world and govern themselves. Media studies asks how news and other media are made, owned and consumed, and how they shape what we know and believe.

This path begins with classic accounts of what journalism is for, teaches reporting, verification and writing, covers investigative and data journalism, reads the critics of media power, looks at press freedom around the world, and ends with the future of news in the age of platforms and misinformation.

**Trivia**
- The *Acta Diurna*, daily notices posted in ancient Rome, are sometimes called the first newspapers.
- Maria Ressa of the Philippines and Dmitry Muratov of Russia shared the 2021 Nobel Peace Prize for defending freedom of expression.
- The 2016 Panama Papers investigation involved over 370 journalists from about 80 countries working on 11.5 million leaked documents.
- The “inverted pyramid”, putting the most important facts first, became common in the era of the telegraph.

### Timeline
- **c. 59 BCE · *Acta Diurna*** Daily public notices in Rome.
- **1605 · *Relation*** in Strasbourg, often called the first printed newspaper.
- **1766 · Sweden's Freedom of the Press Act** One of the first laws protecting press freedom.
- **1922 · Lippmann's *Public Opinion***
- **1972–74 · Watergate** Reporting that led to a US president's resignation.
- **1991 · The web** begins to change how news is published.
- **2016 · The Panama Papers** A global collaborative investigation.
- **2021 · Ressa and Muratov** share the Nobel Peace Prize.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know journalism and media the way a strong first year of a university journalism or media degree expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain what journalism is for:** its principles, from verification to independence.
- **Report and verify:** interviews, sources, fact-checking and checking digital content.
- **Understand investigations:** documents, data and collaboration across borders.
- **Analyse media power:** ownership, framing, propaganda and the attention economy.
- **Weigh today's debates:** press freedom, platforms, misinformation and trust.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Journalists and media scholars from more than one continent will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.

# Final mastery check
