# Entrepreneurship

How to start and grow a venture: finding problems worth solving, testing ideas cheaply, business models, customers and sales, money and funding, building a team, and social enterprise and entrepreneurship around the world. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free courses, government small-business guides and startup resources cover the core. The path covers small local businesses, social enterprises and high-growth startups, in many countries, and is honest about failure and risk. **It is knowledge, not financial, legal or tax advice.**

# About entrepreneurship

Entrepreneurship is creating something new, a business, a nonprofit or a product, and taking the risk to make it work. Most businesses in the world are small, and they create most jobs; a few grow into giants.

This path begins with founders' stories and the entrepreneurial mindset, teaches how to find and test ideas, designs business models, covers customers, marketing and sales, explains money and funding, and ends with teams, growth, social enterprise and entrepreneurship worldwide.

**Trivia**
- Small and medium-sized enterprises make up about 90% of businesses and over half of employment worldwide, according to the World Bank.
- Around a fifth of new businesses close within their first year, and about half within five years, in many countries' statistics.
- M-Pesa, launched in Kenya in 2007, brought mobile money to tens of millions of people.
- Muhammad Yunus's Grameen Bank helped popularise microcredit for small entrepreneurs.

### Timeline
- **1776 · Adam Smith's *Wealth of Nations*** describes markets and enterprise.
- **1803 · Jean-Baptiste Say** popularises the word “entrepreneur”.
- **1934 · Joseph Schumpeter** describes entrepreneurs as agents of “creative destruction”.
- **1939 · Hewlett-Packard** starts in a garage.
- **1976 · Grameen's microcredit** begins in Bangladesh.
- **1985 · Peter Drucker's *Innovation and Entrepreneurship*** treats enterprise as a discipline.
- **2005 · Y Combinator** launches the startup accelerator model.
- **2007 · M-Pesa** launches in Kenya.
- **2010 · The Business Model Canvas** is published.
- **2011 · Eric Ries's *The Lean Startup*** popularises build–measure–learn.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know entrepreneurship the way a strong first year of a university entrepreneurship course expects. You will be able to:

- **Find and test ideas:** customer problems, interviews and cheap experiments.
- **Design a business model:** value, customers, channels, costs and revenue.
- **Reach customers:** marketing, sales and pricing.
- **Manage money:** costs, cash flow, break-even and funding options.
- **Build and grow:** teams, legal basics, ethics, social enterprise and global contexts.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Founders and entrepreneurship educators from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published. Knowledge only: not financial, legal or tax advice.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages makes you a capable student of entrepreneurship. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Entrepreneurship** certificate, you also complete:

1. **The grand quiz.** 40 game-style questions across all six stages. Pass mark 80%, with unlimited retakes.
2. **Venture plan.** Write a one-page plan with a Business Model Canvas and evidence from customer interviews.
3. **Financial model.** Build a simple cash-flow and break-even model.
4. **Capstone essay.** 2,500 words on one of the Stage 6 questions. Strong essays are read by an expert reviewer.
5. **Explain it.** Answer three unseen questions as if to a curious friend. For example: “How do I know if my business idea is any good?”

The certificate needs every stage passed, and is knowledge only: not financial, legal or tax advice.
