# Fluid Mechanics

How liquids and gases move: pressure and buoyancy, flow in pipes and over wings, viscosity and boundary layers, turbulence, waves, and fluids in weather, blood, engines and the ocean, plus how engineers simulate them. About 150 to 200 hours over six to eight months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free classic films, textbooks and simulation tools cover the core. You need school physics and calculus. Simple kitchen experiments with water, dye and air are part of every stage.

# About fluid mechanics

Fluid mechanics studies how liquids and gases flow and push on things. It explains flight, weather, ocean currents, blood flow, pipelines, sport and the shape of fast cars and fish.

This path starts with everyday wonders and classic films, teaches fluid statics and Bernoulli's principle, covers viscosity, pipe flow and boundary layers, tackles turbulence and the Navier–Stokes equations, explores fluids in nature and the body, and ends with computational fluid dynamics and research.

**Trivia**
- Archimedes is said to have realised the principle of buoyancy in his bath, crying “Eureka!”.
- Leonardo da Vinci sketched swirling turbulent water around 1500, centuries before turbulence was explained.
- Golf balls have dimples because a turbulent boundary layer reduces drag, letting them fly farther.
- Whether the Navier–Stokes equations always have smooth solutions is a $1 million Millennium Prize Problem.

### Timeline
- **c. 250 BCE · Archimedes** states the principle of buoyancy.
- **1643 · Evangelista Torricelli** measures air pressure with a mercury barometer.
- **1653 · Blaise Pascal** states that pressure in a fluid acts equally in all directions.
- **1738 · Daniel Bernoulli** publishes *Hydrodynamica*.
- **1845 · George Stokes** completes the Navier–Stokes equations.
- **1883 · Osborne Reynolds** shows when pipe flow becomes turbulent.
- **1904 · Ludwig Prandtl** introduces the boundary layer.
- **1941 · Andrey Kolmogorov** describes the energy cascade in turbulence.
- **1960s · The National Committee for Fluid Mechanics Films** records classic teaching films.
- **1970s onwards · Computational fluid dynamics** becomes an engineering tool.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know fluid mechanics the way a strong undergraduate course expects. You will be able to:

- **Analyse fluids at rest:** pressure, buoyancy and stability.
- **Analyse flows:** continuity, Bernoulli's equation and momentum.
- **Explain viscosity and boundary layers:** pipe flow, the Reynolds number, drag and lift.
- **Explain turbulence:** its onset, energy cascade and why it is hard.
- **Apply and simulate:** fluids in nature, medicine and engineering, and CFD basics.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.
