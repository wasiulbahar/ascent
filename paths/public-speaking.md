# Public Speaking

How to speak so people listen and remember: handling nerves, structuring talks and stories, voice and body, slides and data, persuasion and audiences across cultures, and debate, impromptu speaking and short-form talks. About 100 to 130 hours over four to six months, most of it spent practising.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free talks, guides and recordings of great speeches cover the core. Speaking is learned by speaking: every stage asks you to record yourself and, when you can, to speak to a real audience such as a club, class or meeting.

# About public speaking

Public speaking is the craft of communicating to a group, from a two-minute toast to a keynote, a lecture, a pitch or a debate. It combines clear thinking, structure, story, voice and presence, and it can be learned by anyone.

This path begins with watching great speakers and taming nerves, teaches structure and story, then voice and body, then slides and data, moves to persuasion, cultures and answering questions, and ends with debate, speechwriting, short-form talks and building a habit of speaking.

**Trivia**
- Lincoln's Gettysburg Address is only 272 words and took about two minutes; the main speaker before him spoke for about two hours.
- Aristotle named three modes of persuasion: ethos (character), pathos (emotion) and logos (reasoning).
- In the Three Minute Thesis competition, researchers explain their doctoral work in three minutes with one slide.
- Malala Yousafzai addressed the United Nations on her 16th birthday in 2013.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will speak the way confident professionals and good teachers do. You will be able to:

- **Manage nerves:** prepare, practise and use anxiety as energy.
- **Structure talks:** clear messages, stories and memorable openings and endings.
- **Deliver well:** voice, pace, pauses, gestures and eye contact.
- **Use visuals honestly:** simple slides and clear data, or no slides at all.
- **Persuade and respond:** adapt to audiences and cultures, answer questions and speak impromptu.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Speaking coaches and communication teachers from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages makes you a confident speaker. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Public Speaking** certificate, you also complete:

1. **The grand quiz.** 40 game-style questions across all six stages. Pass mark 80%, with unlimited retakes.
2. **Recorded talk.** Record a ten-minute talk with visuals for a real or invited audience.
3. **Impromptu set.** Answer three unseen questions on the spot, recorded.
4. **Reflection.** Write 2,500 words analysing your growth with recordings and feedback. Strong portfolios are read by an expert reviewer.
5. **Explain it.** Coach a friend through preparing their own talk.

The certificate needs every stage passed.
