# Food Science

The science of what we eat and how it is made: the chemistry and physics of cooking, fermentation and microbes, food preservation and safety, food processing, flavour and the senses, and the global food system, culture and the future of food. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Food safety agencies, free courses and recipes-as-experiments cover the core, and your own kitchen is the laboratory. The path covers cuisines and food systems worldwide, separates evidence from food fads, and presents debates about processed food and new technologies fairly. **It is not dietary or medical advice.**

# About food science

Food science applies chemistry, biology, physics and engineering to food: how ingredients behave when cooked, how microbes ferment and spoil food, how food is kept safe and preserved, and how it is processed, packaged and tasted.

This path begins with the science of cooking, studies food chemistry and physics, explores fermentation and microbes, covers preservation and food safety, examines processing, flavour and the senses, and ends with the global food system, culture and the future of food.

**Trivia**
- The Maillard reaction between sugars and amino acids gives browned foods such as bread crust, roasted coffee and seared meat their flavour.
- Louis Pasteur showed in the 1860s that heating wine and milk kills spoilage microbes: pasteurisation.
- Nicolas Appert invented canning (in glass jars) around 1810, winning a prize offered by Napoleon's government.
- The WHO estimates about 600 million people fall ill from contaminated food each year.

### Timeline
- **c. 7000 BCE · Fermented drinks** are made in China.
- **c. 4000 BCE · Cheese-making** is practised in Europe and the Middle East.
- **1810 · Nicolas Appert** publishes his method of preserving food.
- **1860s · Pasteur** develops pasteurisation.
- **1912 · Louis-Camille Maillard** describes the browning reaction.
- **1924 · Clarence Birdseye** develops quick freezing.
- **1963 · The Codex Alimentarius** international food standards, is founded.
- **1960s · HACCP** is developed for NASA astronauts' food.
- **1984 · Harold McGee's *On Food and Cooking*** is published.
- **2020 · Singapore** approves the first sale of cultivated meat.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know food science the way a strong first year of a university food science course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain cooking chemistry:** proteins, starches, fats, sugars and browning.
- **Explain fermentation and microbes:** in bread, cheese, yoghurt, kimchi and more.
- **Explain food safety:** pathogens, preservation and HACCP.
- **Explain processing and flavour:** how foods are made, and how taste and smell work.
- **Weigh the food system:** ultra-processed foods, sustainability and new foods.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Food scientists and food safety experts from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published. Knowledge only: not dietary or medical advice.
