# Real Analysis

The rigorous foundations of calculus: what real numbers are, limits and sequences, continuity and derivatives, integrals and series, and the leap to metric spaces and Lebesgue integration. About 200 to 250 hours over eight to ten months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free textbooks, recorded university courses and proof games cover the core. You need calculus (the Calculus path) and some experience with proofs (the Discrete mathematics path helps). Expect to struggle productively: analysis is learned by writing and rewriting proofs.

# About real analysis

Real analysis rebuilds calculus with complete rigour. It defines real numbers, limits and integrals precisely and proves why the methods of calculus work, and when they fail.

This path begins with puzzles about infinity and an introduction to proofs, constructs the real numbers and studies sequences, proves the great theorems of continuity and differentiation, develops integration and series, moves beyond the real line to metric spaces and Lebesgue integration, and ends with proof assistants, history and current research.

**Trivia**
- The repeating decimal 0.999… is exactly equal to 1.
- Karl Weierstrass presented a function in 1872 that is continuous everywhere but has no derivative anywhere.
- In 1734 Bishop George Berkeley mocked calculus's infinitesimals as “ghosts of departed quantities”.
- The harmonic series 1 + 1/2 + 1/3 + … grows forever, but it needs more than 10⁴³ terms to pass 100.

### Timeline
- **c. 450 BCE · Zeno of Elea** poses paradoxes of motion and infinity.
- **c. 250 BCE · Archimedes** uses the method of exhaustion to find areas.
- **1734 · George Berkeley** attacks the logic of infinitesimals in *The Analyst*.
- **1821 · Augustin-Louis Cauchy** publishes his *Cours d'analyse*.
- **1854 · Bernhard Riemann** defines the Riemann integral.
- **1872 · Richard Dedekind** constructs real numbers with cuts; Weierstrass shows his strange function.
- **1874 · Georg Cantor** proves the real numbers are uncountable.
- **1902 · Henri Lebesgue** introduces a more powerful integral.
- **1906 · Maurice Fréchet** introduces metric spaces.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know real analysis the way a strong first university course expects. You will be able to:

- **Understand the real numbers:** completeness, suprema and the construction of ℝ.
- **Prove results about limits:** sequences, subsequences and Cauchy sequences.
- **Prove the great theorems:** continuity, the intermediate and mean value theorems, and differentiability.
- **Handle integrals and series:** the Riemann integral, convergence tests and uniform convergence.
- **Go beyond:** metric spaces, countability and the idea of Lebesgue integration.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Mathematicians and mathematics educators from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
