# Medicine and health

From how the body works to how diseases spread, how doctors reason, and the hard questions medicine faces next. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Many core sources, such as open textbooks and World Health Organization reports, are free. This path teaches knowledge about medicine and health: **it is not medical advice, and not a qualification to treat anyone**. For a health problem, see a qualified health professional.

# About medicine and health

Medicine is the science and practice of understanding, preventing and treating disease. Health is wider: it depends on bodies and germs, but also on clean water, food, housing, money and the care people can reach. Studying medicine and health means learning how the body works, what goes wrong in disease, how treatments are tested, and how whole populations stay well or fall ill.

This path begins with books that show what medicine means to patients and doctors, teaches how the healthy body works, covers disease and treatment, widens to the health of populations around the world, reaches clinical reasoning and medical ethics, and ends with the questions medicine faces next: gene editing, artificial intelligence and pandemics.

**Trivia**
- Smallpox, declared eradicated in 1980, is the only human disease ever wiped out worldwide.
- Ibn Sina's *Canon of Medicine*, finished around 1025, was used as a medical textbook in Europe and the Islamic world for centuries.
- Alexander Fleming noticed in 1928 that a mould had killed the bacteria on one of his plates: the discovery of penicillin.
- Cells taken from Henrietta Lacks in 1951 were the first human cells to keep dividing in a laboratory, and are still used in research today.

### Timeline
- **c. 400 BCE · The Hippocratic writings** Greek medicine based on observation, not the gods.
- **c. 1025 · Ibn Sina's *Canon of Medicine*** A great summary of medical knowledge.
- **1543 · Vesalius** *On the Fabric of the Human Body* corrects anatomy by dissection.
- **1796 · Jenner** Vaccination against smallpox.
- **1854 · John Snow** Traces a cholera outbreak to a water pump.
- **1928 · Penicillin** The first antibiotic is discovered.
- **1948 · The World Health Organization** is founded.
- **1980 · Smallpox eradicated**
- **2003 · The Human Genome Project** is completed.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know medicine and health the way a strong first year of a university course in health sciences expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain how the body works:** its main systems, and how they keep conditions stable.
- **Explain how disease happens:** infection, inflammation, cancer and the immune response.
- **Judge evidence:** what makes a fair trial of a treatment, and why anecdotes mislead.
- **Explain population health:** why health differs so much between and within countries.
- **Weigh the ethics:** consent, autonomy and justice in medicine and research.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Doctors, nurses and public-health researchers from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published. Knowledge only: never medical advice.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages makes you a capable student of medicine and health. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Medicine and health** certificate, you also complete:

1. **The grand quiz.** 40 game-style questions across all six stages. Pass mark 80%, with unlimited retakes.
2. **Case reasoning.** Read a published teaching case and explain the reasoning: history, signs, possible diagnoses and how they were narrowed.
3. **Evidence appraisal.** Judge one published trial: who was studied, how it was controlled, and how strong its conclusion is.
4. **Capstone essay.** 2,500 words on one of the Stage 6 questions. Strong essays are read by an expert reviewer.
5. **Explain it.** Answer three unseen questions as if to a curious friend. For example: “Why don't antibiotics work on a cold?”

The certificate needs every stage passed, and is knowledge only: never a qualification to treat anyone or a source of medical advice.
