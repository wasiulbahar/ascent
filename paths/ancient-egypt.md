# Ancient Egypt

Three thousand years of pharaohs, pyramids and the Nile: how Egyptian civilisation arose, the Old, Middle and New Kingdoms, hieroglyphs and literature, religion and the afterlife, everyday life, Egypt's neighbours in Africa, and how Egyptology itself was made. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Museums such as the British Museum, the Met and the Egyptian Museum share collections free online, and many translated texts are free. The path places Egypt in Africa, includes Egyptian and African scholars, and examines how colonial-era Egyptology shaped what we know.

# About ancient Egypt

Ancient Egypt was one of the world's earliest and longest-lasting civilisations, flourishing along the Nile for about 3,000 years from around 3100 BCE. It built pyramids and temples, developed hieroglyphic writing, and created rich religious, literary and artistic traditions.

This path begins with lively histories and museum collections, follows the rise of the state and the age of the pyramids, studies hieroglyphs and literature, explores religion and the afterlife, looks at everyday life, Nubia and the wider world, and ends with how Egyptology was made and who owns Egypt's past.

**Trivia**
- Cleopatra VII lived closer in time to the Moon landing than to the building of the Great Pyramid.
- The Rosetta Stone, carved in 196 BCE, carries the same decree in hieroglyphs, Demotic and Greek, which helped Jean-François Champollion decipher hieroglyphs in 1822.
- The workers who built the pyramids were paid labourers housed in nearby villages, not slaves.
- Nubia's kings of the 25th dynasty ruled Egypt for about a century, around 744–656 BCE.

### Timeline
- **c. 3100 BCE · Upper and Lower Egypt** are unified.
- **c. 2560 BCE · The Great Pyramid of Giza** is completed for Khufu.
- **c. 2055–1650 BCE · The Middle Kingdom** a classical age of literature.
- **c. 1550–1069 BCE · The New Kingdom** Egypt's imperial age.
- **c. 1353–1336 BCE · Akhenaten** promotes worship of the Aten.
- **c. 1323 BCE · Tutankhamun** is buried.
- **c. 744–656 BCE · Nubian kings** rule Egypt as the 25th dynasty.
- **332 BCE · Alexander the Great** conquers Egypt; the Ptolemies follow.
- **30 BCE · Cleopatra VII dies;** Egypt becomes a Roman province.
- **1822 · Champollion** deciphers hieroglyphs.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know ancient Egypt the way a strong first year of a university Egyptology course expects. You will be able to:

- **Trace Egypt's history:** from unification to Roman rule.
- **Read a little Middle Egyptian:** simple hieroglyphic signs and phrases.
- **Explain religion and the afterlife:** gods, temples, tombs and mummification.
- **Describe everyday life:** work, family, food and law, from many kinds of evidence.
- **Weigh Egyptology's history:** colonialism, repatriation and Egypt's place in Africa.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Egyptologists from Egypt and other countries will confirm them before the path is published.
