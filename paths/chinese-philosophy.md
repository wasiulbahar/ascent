# Chinese Philosophy

Three thousand years of Chinese thought: Confucius and Mencius, Laozi and Zhuangzi, Mohists and Legalists, the Yijing, Chinese Buddhism, Neo-Confucianism, and modern Chinese philosophy, read in translation and in conversation with philosophy worldwide. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. The Chinese Text Project and many classic translations are free, and the Stanford Encyclopedia covers the field. The path reads the texts on their own terms, compares translations, and presents scholarly debates fairly.

# About Chinese philosophy

Chinese philosophy is one of the world's great philosophical traditions, asking how to live well, govern justly, cultivate virtue and understand the natural order. Its classical age, the “Hundred Schools of Thought”, flourished in the 6th–3rd centuries BCE.

This path begins with accessible introductions, reads Confucius and his followers, studies the Daoist classics, examines Mohists, Legalists and the debates of the Warring States, explores Buddhism and Neo-Confucianism, and ends with modern Chinese philosophy and comparative thought.

**Trivia**
- The *Analects* records sayings of Confucius (551–479 BCE) collected by his followers.
- The *Daodejing*, attributed to Laozi, is one of the most translated books in the world.
- China's imperial civil service exams tested knowledge of the Confucian classics for over 1,300 years, until 1905.
- Zhuangzi's “butterfly dream” asks whether he was a man dreaming he was a butterfly, or a butterfly dreaming he was a man.

### Timeline
- **551–479 BCE · Confucius** teaches.
- **c. 5th century BCE · Mozi** founds Mohism.
- **c. 4th century BCE · The *Daodejing*** takes shape.
- **c. 372–289 BCE · Mencius** argues human nature is good.
- **c. 4th–3rd century BCE · Zhuangzi** writes.
- **c. 310–235 BCE · Xunzi** argues human nature is bad.
- **221 BCE · The Qin** unify China with Legalist methods.
- **c. 400 CE · Buddhist texts** are translated into Chinese.
- **1130–1200 · Zhu Xi** systematises Neo-Confucianism.
- **1472–1529 · Wang Yangming** teaches the unity of knowledge and action.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know Chinese philosophy the way a strong first year of a university course expects. You will be able to:

- **Read the classics:** the *Analects*, *Mencius*, *Daodejing* and *Zhuangzi*.
- **Explain key ideas:** ren, li, de, dao, wu wei and qi.
- **Compare schools:** Confucian, Daoist, Mohist and Legalist.
- **Trace later thought:** Chinese Buddhism and Neo-Confucianism.
- **Bring Chinese philosophy into dialogue:** with ethics and philosophy worldwide.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Scholars of Chinese philosophy from China and elsewhere will confirm them before the path is published.
