# Translation Studies

How meaning crosses languages: the history of translation from scripture to subtitles, theories of equivalence and foreignisation, literary and poetry translation, interpreting, translation and power, and machine translation and AI. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Translators' associations, free courses and parallel texts cover the core, and you can practise with any two languages you know. The path draws on translation traditions from China, the Arab world, India, Africa and Europe, and presents debates about AI fairly. **It is knowledge, not a professional qualification.**

# About translation studies

Translation studies examines how texts and speech are carried between languages and cultures: in literature, religion, science, law, business, film and everyday life. Interpreting does the same for spoken and signed language in real time.

This path begins with lively books on what translation is, follows its history worldwide, studies the main theories, explores literary and poetry translation, examines interpreting and translation in society, and ends with machine translation, AI and the future of the profession.

**Trivia**
- The Bible has been fully translated into over 700 languages, and parts of it into more than 3,000.
- Baghdad's House of Wisdom translated Greek, Persian and Indian works into Arabic in the 9th century.
- The Buddhist monk Kumārajīva translated sutras from Sanskrit into Chinese around 400 CE.
- The Nuremberg trials of 1945–46 pioneered simultaneous interpreting with headsets.

### Timeline
- **c. 250 BCE · The Septuagint** translates Hebrew scriptures into Greek.
- **c. 400 CE · Kumārajīva** translates Buddhist sutras into Chinese.
- **c. 400 CE · Jerome** translates the Bible into Latin (the Vulgate).
- **9th century · The House of Wisdom** translates Greek science into Arabic.
- **12th century · Toledo's translators** turn Arabic works into Latin.
- **1813 · Friedrich Schleiermacher** describes two methods of translation.
- **1945–46 · Nuremberg** pioneers simultaneous interpreting.
- **1964 · Eugene Nida** proposes dynamic equivalence.
- **1995 · Lawrence Venuti's *The Translator's Invisibility*** calls for foreignising translation.
- **2016 · Neural machine translation** transforms Google Translate.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know translation studies the way a strong first year of a university course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain what translation involves:** meaning, form, culture and purpose.
- **Trace translation history:** across religions, empires and sciences.
- **Use translation theory:** equivalence, skopos, domestication and foreignisation.
- **Translate and compare translations:** of literature, poetry and practical texts.
- **Weigh machine translation and ethics:** AI, interpreting in crises, and power.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Translators, interpreters and translation scholars from more than one language tradition will confirm them before the path is published. Knowledge only: not a professional qualification.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages makes you a capable student of translation. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Translation Studies** certificate, you also complete:
