# Ethics

How to think about right and wrong: the great moral theories from several traditions, life-and-death questions, animals and the environment, justice, the psychology of morality, and the ethics of new technology. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Most classic texts are free, and the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy is free. The path presents competing views fairly on hard questions such as abortion, euthanasia and animals, and asks you to reason for yourself. (The Philosophy path covers ethics alongside other branches; this one goes deeper.)

# About ethics

Ethics, or moral philosophy, asks how we ought to live and act: what makes actions right or wrong, what we owe each other, other animals and future generations, and what a just society looks like. Applied ethics brings these questions to real problems in medicine, business, the environment and technology.

This path begins with lively introductions, reads the great moral theories, tackles questions of life and death, widens to animals, nature and the far future, studies theories of justice, and ends with the psychology of morality, the ethics of machines, and current research.

**Trivia**
- The “trolley problem”, now famous worldwide, was introduced by the philosopher Philippa Foot in 1967.
- Peter Singer's “drowning child” argument (1972) helped inspire the effective altruism movement.
- John Rawls asked what rules for society you would choose behind a “veil of ignorance”, not knowing who you would be.
- An online experiment, the Moral Machine, gathered about 40 million moral decisions from people in over 200 countries and territories about self-driving cars.

### Timeline
- **c. 5th century BCE · Confucius and Socrates** ask how to live well.
- **c. 340 BCE · Aristotle's *Nicomachean Ethics***
- **1785 · Kant's *Groundwork***
- **1861 · Mill's *Utilitarianism***
- **1949 · Leopold's “land ethic”**
- **1967 · Foot's trolley problem**
- **1971 · Rawls's *A Theory of Justice***
- **1975 · Singer's *Animal Liberation***
- **1984 · Noddings's ethics of care**
- **2018 · The Moral Machine experiment** is published.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know ethics the way a strong first year of a university moral philosophy course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain the main theories:** consequentialism, deontology, virtue ethics, care ethics and others.
- **Reason about hard cases:** abortion, euthanasia, poverty and the trolley problem, fairly.
- **Widen the moral circle:** animals, the environment and future generations.
- **Compare theories of justice:** Rawls, Nozick, the capabilities approach and their critics.
- **Weigh new questions:** moral psychology and the ethics of AI and technology.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Moral philosophers from more than one tradition will confirm them before the path is published.
