# History of Science

How humans came to understand nature: ancient astronomy and mathematics across civilisations, science in the Islamic world, India and China, the Scientific Revolution, Darwin, chemistry and germs, relativity, atoms and big science, and the links between science, empire, gender and society. About 150 to 180 hours over six to eight months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free museum collections, digitised original works and scholarly archives cover the core. No science background is needed. The path tells a global story, giving full weight to contributions from Mesopotamia, China, India, the Islamic world, Africa and the Americas.

# About the history of science

The history of science studies how knowledge of nature was made, who made it, and how it shaped and was shaped by societies, from Babylonian astronomers to modern laboratories.

This path begins with how historians see the past of science, travels through ancient and medieval sciences across the world, studies the Scientific Revolution, the 19th century's great transformations and 20th-century big science, and ends with science's ties to empire, gender and trust.

**Trivia**
- The Babylonian clay tablet Plimpton 322, about 3,800 years old, lists sets of numbers that fit Pythagoras's theorem, more than a thousand years before Pythagoras.
- Marie Curie is the only person to win Nobel Prizes in two different sciences, physics in 1903 and chemistry in 1911.
- The Royal Society's motto, *Nullius in verba*, means “take nobody's word for it”.
- The Piltdown Man “fossil”, presented in 1912, was exposed as a hoax only in 1953.

### Timeline
- **c. 1800 BCE · Babylonian scribes** record Pythagorean triples on Plimpton 322.
- **c. 300 BCE · Euclid** compiles the *Elements* in Alexandria.
- **628 · Brahmagupta** sets out rules for calculating with zero.
- **c. 830 · The House of Wisdom** flourishes in Baghdad.
- **1543 · Copernicus and Vesalius** publish works on the cosmos and the human body.
- **1660 · The Royal Society** is founded in London.
- **1687 · Isaac Newton** publishes the *Principia*.
- **1859 · Charles Darwin** publishes *On the Origin of Species*.
- **1869 · Dmitri Mendeleev** publishes his periodic table.
- **1905 · Albert Einstein** publishes special relativity.
- **1945 · The Manhattan Project** produces the first atomic bombs.
- **1954 · CERN** is founded.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will understand the history of science the way a strong university course expects. You will be able to:

- **Trace science across civilisations:** Mesopotamia, Greece, China, India, the Islamic world and beyond.
- **Explain the Scientific Revolution:** Copernicus, Vesalius, Newton and new institutions.
- **Explain modern transformations:** evolution, chemistry, germ theory, relativity and big science.
- **Analyse science and society:** empire, gender, race, fraud and public trust.
- **Read historically:** use primary sources and avoid judging the past by today's knowledge alone.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Historians of science from more than one region will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
