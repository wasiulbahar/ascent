# Genetics

How inheritance works: Mendel's peas to the double helix, genes and genomes, human ancestry and disease, the dark history of eugenics and today's ethics, and gene editing and ancient DNA. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free textbooks, lessons and genome databases cover the core. The path confronts genetics' misuse, from eugenics to “race science”, and presents current ethical debates fairly. **It is not medical or genetic-counselling advice.**

# About genetics

Genetics studies genes, the units of inheritance made of DNA, and how they pass from parents to offspring, vary between individuals and shape living things. Genomics studies whole genomes. Together they explain inheritance, evolution and many diseases, and now allow genes to be read and edited.

This path begins with gripping histories of the gene and gene editing, teaches the foundations of inheritance, explains molecular genetics and the human genome, explores human ancestry and disease, confronts the ethics and history of genetics, and ends with gene therapies and ancient DNA.

**Trivia**
- Gregor Mendel worked out the basic laws of inheritance by breeding about 28,000 pea plants in the 1850s and 60s; his work was ignored until 1900.
- The human genome has about 3 billion DNA letters and around 20,000 protein-coding genes.
- The first complete, gap-free human genome sequence was published in 2022, nineteen years after the Human Genome Project was declared finished.
- In 2023 Casgevy became the first approved therapy using CRISPR gene editing, for sickle-cell disease and beta thalassaemia.

### Timeline
- **1866 · Mendel** publishes his pea experiments.
- **1900 · Mendel's work** is rediscovered.
- **1944 · Avery, MacLeod and McCarty** show DNA carries genetic information.
- **1953 · The double helix** Watson, Crick, Franklin and Wilkins.
- **1977 · DNA sequencing** methods are invented.
- **2003 · The Human Genome Project** is declared complete.
- **2012 · CRISPR gene editing** is described by Doudna and Charpentier.
- **2018 · Gene-edited babies** are announced in China, to worldwide condemnation.
- **2022 · The first complete human genome** is published.
- **2023 · The first CRISPR therapy** is approved.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know genetics the way a strong first year of a university genetics course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain inheritance:** Mendel's laws, dominance, and how traits pass on.
- **Explain molecular genetics:** DNA, genes, the genetic code and how genes are expressed.
- **Read genomes:** sequencing, variation and what genomes reveal about ancestry.
- **Explain genetics and disease:** single-gene and complex conditions, and gene therapy.
- **Weigh the ethics:** eugenics' history, race, gene editing and genetic privacy.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Geneticists and bioethicists from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published. Knowledge only: not medical or genetic-counselling advice.
