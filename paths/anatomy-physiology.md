# Anatomy and Physiology

How the human body is built and how it works: cells and tissues, the skeleton and muscles, the nervous and endocrine systems, heart, blood and lungs, digestion, kidneys and immunity, and reproduction, growth and ageing. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free textbooks, 3D anatomy tools and video courses cover the core. The path covers bodies of all sexes, ages and backgrounds, and notes where medicine's research has historically under-studied some groups. **It is knowledge only, not medical advice.**

# About anatomy and physiology

Anatomy is the study of the body's structure; physiology is the study of how it works. Together they underpin medicine, nursing, sport science and much of biology, and explain everyday experiences, from a racing heart to a full stomach.

This path begins with lively books on the body, studies cells and tissues, moves through the skeleton and muscles, the nervous and endocrine systems, the heart, blood and lungs, digestion, kidneys and immunity, and ends with reproduction, development and ageing.

**Trivia**
- An adult human body has 206 bones; a newborn has around 300, some of which fuse as they grow.
- The heart beats about 100,000 times a day.
- If laid end to end, the blood vessels of an adult would stretch tens of thousands of kilometres.
- The small intestine is around 6 metres long in an adult.

### Timeline
- **c. 1600 BCE · The Edwin Smith Papyrus** describes injuries and the brain.
- **c. 300 BCE · Herophilus** dissects human bodies in Alexandria.
- **c. 170 CE · Galen** writes anatomy that dominates for 1,300 years.
- **1242 · Ibn al-Nafis** describes the pulmonary circulation.
- **1543 · Vesalius's *Fabrica*** corrects Galen through dissection.
- **1628 · William Harvey** shows blood circulates.
- **1858 · *Gray's Anatomy*** is first published.
- **1895 · X-rays** let doctors see inside living bodies.
- **1921–22 · Insulin** is discovered, explaining part of the endocrine system.
- **1970s · CT and MRI scans** transform anatomical imaging.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know anatomy and physiology the way a strong first year of a university course for health sciences expects. You will be able to:

- **Describe the body's organisation:** cells, tissues, organs and systems.
- **Explain each major system:** skeletal, muscular, nervous, endocrine, cardiovascular, respiratory, digestive, urinary, immune and reproductive.
- **Explain homeostasis:** how the body keeps conditions stable, and what happens when it fails.
- **Use anatomical language:** directions, planes and regions.
- **Connect structure and function:** and apply them to health, exercise and disease.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Anatomists, physiologists and health educators from more than one country will review the path before it is published. Knowledge only: not medical advice.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
