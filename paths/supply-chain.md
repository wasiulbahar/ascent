# Supply Chain Management

How goods get from raw materials to your hands: forecasting and inventory, lean operations, shipping and logistics, risk and disruption, responsible sourcing, and sustainable, digital supply chains. About 150 to 180 hours over six to eight months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free university courses, a free forecasting textbook and reports from the UN, OECD and others cover the core. You need school maths and basic spreadsheet skills. The path covers both efficiency and the human and environmental costs of global supply chains.

# About supply chain management

A supply chain is the network of organisations, people and activities that moves a product from raw materials to the customer. Supply chain management plans, sources, makes, moves and returns goods as reliably, cheaply and responsibly as possible.

This path starts with the shipping container and a classic simulation game, teaches forecasting and inventory, covers lean operations and flow, studies shipping and logistics, examines disruption and resilience, looks at labour rights and due diligence, and ends with emissions, circularity and digital tools.

**Trivia**
- The first product scanned with a barcode at a shop checkout, in 1974, was a pack of chewing gum.
- In 2021 the container ship *Ever Given* blocked the Suez Canal for six days, holding up hundreds of ships.
- Around 80% of world trade by volume travels by sea.
- The term “supply chain management” was popularised by the consultant Keith Oliver in 1982.

### Timeline
- **1913 · Ford Harris** publishes the economic order quantity formula.
- **1956 · Malcom McLean's *Ideal X*** carries the first modern shipping containers.
- **1961 · John Little** proves Little's law; Jay Forrester publishes *Industrial Dynamics*.
- **1970s · Toyota's just-in-time production** spreads beyond Japan.
- **1974 · The first barcode** is scanned at a shop checkout.
- **1982 · Keith Oliver** popularises the term “supply chain management”.
- **1990 · *The Machine That Changed the World*** introduces “lean production”.
- **2011 · The Tōhoku earthquake** disrupts car and electronics supply chains worldwide.
- **2013 · The Rana Plaza collapse** kills more than 1,100 garment workers.
- **2021 · The *Ever Given*** blocks the Suez Canal.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will understand supply chains the way a strong university course and early professional work expect. You will be able to:

- **Forecast and plan:** demand forecasting, inventory policies and the bullwhip effect.
- **Improve operations:** lean, flow, bottlenecks and Little's law.
- **Manage logistics:** shipping, ports, warehousing and the last mile.
- **Build resilience:** map risks and plan for disruption.
- **Source responsibly:** labour rights, due diligence, emissions and circular design.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Supply chain professionals and educators from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
