# Computer science

From how a computer really works to algorithms, the limits of computation and the arguments about what computers do to society. About 300 to 360 hours over twelve to fifteen months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Computer science has some of the best free material of any subject: whole university courses, and classic textbooks free online. You'll write code from Stage 2; any computer, even an old one, is enough. Quizzes include short problems; you can type the number alone.

# About computer science

Computer science is the study of computation: what can be computed, how to compute it efficiently, and how to build the machines and programs that do it. It is part mathematics (algorithms and their limits), part engineering (hardware, systems and software), and part social science, because software now shapes how billions of people work, talk and are judged.

This path starts with books that open up the machine, teaches you to program, covers the core of algorithms and systems, explores the theory of what computers can and can't do, reaches the university core, and ends with the arguments about software's power over people.

**Trivia**
- Ada Lovelace's notes on Charles Babbage's Analytical Engine, published in 1843, include what is often called the first computer program.
- In 1936 Alan Turing proved that some problems can never be solved by any computer, before electronic computers existed.
- The first computer "bug" to be logged was a real moth, found in a relay of the Harvard Mark II computer in 1947.
- Whether every problem whose answer can be checked quickly can also be solved quickly, "P versus NP", is one of the Millennium Prize problems, worth a million dollars.

### Timeline
- **1843 · Lovelace** Notes on the Analytical Engine, with an algorithm for a machine.
- **1936 · Turing** "On Computable Numbers": the Turing machine and the limits of computation.
- **1945 · Stored programs** The design of computers that hold their programs in memory.
- **1947 · The transistor** Invented at Bell Labs, it makes modern electronics possible.
- **1969 · ARPANET** The first message travels over the network that became the internet.
- **1971 · The microprocessor** A whole processor on one chip.
- **1989–91 · The World Wide Web** Tim Berners-Lee proposes and builds the web at CERN.
- **2012 · Deep learning** Neural networks win at image recognition, starting the current wave of AI.

# Before you start

You need only school arithmetic and a computer with a web browser. The path teaches the rest:

- **Stage 2:** programming from scratch, and the discrete mathematics computer scientists use.
- **Stages 3 and 4:** comfort with proofs helps; the Mathematics path's Stage 2 teaches them.
- **Stage 5:** some probability and linear algebra for the AI textbook.

Already know some computer science? Take the **placement test** on this path's page to skip the stages you have mastered.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know computer science the way a strong first and second year of a university degree expects. You will be able to:

- **Write working programs** in Python and C, and read code in other languages.
- **Design and analyse algorithms,** and explain their efficiency with big-O notation.
- **Explain how a computer works** from logic gates to operating systems and networks.
- **Explain the limits of computation:** undecidable problems and P versus NP.
- **Weigh the debates:** what software and AI do to people, work and fairness.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Computer scientists from more than one field will confirm them before the path is published.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages makes you a capable student of computer science. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Computer science** certificate, you also complete:
