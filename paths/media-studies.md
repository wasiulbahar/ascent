# Media Studies

How media shape what we know, feel and do: the history of media from print to platforms, media theory, effects and audiences, ownership and political economy, representation, propaganda and misinformation, and media literacy for the digital age. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free research centres, regulators' reports and media literacy courses cover the core. The path studies media in many countries and political systems, and presents debates about media effects, platforms and regulation fairly. Journalism itself is covered in the Journalism path.

# About media studies

Media studies examines the technologies, institutions, content and audiences of communication: books, newspapers, radio, film, television, the internet, social media and games. It asks how media shape society, culture and politics, and how people use and resist them.

This path begins with the big ideas, follows the history of media, studies media theory, examines effects and audiences, explores ownership, power and representation, and ends with platforms, misinformation, regulation and media literacy.

**Trivia**
- Marshall McLuhan coined “the medium is the message” in 1964.
- Over 5 billion people used social media in 2024, more than 60% of the world's population.
- The Payne Fund studies of the 1930s were among the first large research projects on how films affected children.
- Reuters Institute surveys find trust in news varies hugely between countries, from over 60% in Finland to around 30% or less in some others.

### Timeline
- **c. 1450 · Gutenberg's press** begins the age of print.
- **1830s · The penny press** brings cheap newspapers.
- **1920s · Radio broadcasting** spreads.
- **1930s · Propaganda** is studied after its use in war and by dictators.
- **1950s · Television** becomes a mass medium.
- **1964 · McLuhan's *Understanding Media*** says “the medium is the message”.
- **1973 · Stuart Hall's “Encoding/Decoding”** argues audiences actively interpret.
- **1988 · Herman and Chomsky's *Manufacturing Consent*** proposes the propaganda model.
- **2004–2010 · Facebook, YouTube and Twitter** launch platform media.
- **2022 · The EU's Digital Services Act** regulates large platforms.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know media studies the way a strong first year of a university course expects. You will be able to:

- **Trace media history:** from print to platforms.
- **Apply media theory:** medium theory, encoding/decoding, political economy, agenda-setting.
- **Weigh evidence on effects:** violence, persuasion, wellbeing and polarisation.
- **Analyse representation:** how media portray groups, places and events.
- **Practise media literacy:** verifying, questioning and making media responsibly.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Media scholars from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
