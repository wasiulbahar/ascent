# South Asian Studies

The history, cultures and societies of India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Nepal, Bhutan, the Maldives and Afghanistan's borderlands: ancient civilisations, empires, religions and languages, colonial rule and partition, and the region today. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Many classic texts are free in translation, and archives and museums share collections online. The path covers the whole region, not one country, presents contested histories from several national and scholarly viewpoints, and avoids taking sides in present-day disputes.

# About South Asian studies

South Asia is home to about a quarter of humanity, with ancient civilisations, many of the world's major religions, hundreds of languages, and some of the world's largest democracies. Its history includes the Indus civilisation, great empires, British colonial rule, the partition of 1947 and the creation of new nations.

This path begins with lively histories and literature, studies early South Asia and its religions, follows empires from the Mauryas to the Mughals, examines colonial rule, follows independence, partition and new nations, and ends with the region today.

**Trivia**
- The Indus Valley civilisation, around 2600–1900 BCE, built planned cities such as Harappa and Mohenjo-daro with drainage systems; its script is still undeciphered.
- Buddhism, Jainism and Sikhism all began in South Asia, and Hinduism is one of the world's oldest living religious traditions.
- The 1947 partition of British India displaced around 15 million people and killed perhaps up to a million or more.
- Sri Lanka's Sirimavo Bandaranaike became the world's first woman prime minister in 1960.

### Timeline
- **c. 2600–1900 BCE · The Indus civilisation** flourishes.
- **c. 1500–500 BCE · The Vedic period** produces the Vedas.
- **c. 5th century BCE · The Buddha and Mahavira** teach.
- **c. 268–232 BCE · Ashoka** rules the Mauryan Empire.
- **c. 320–550 CE · The Gupta period** sees advances in mathematics and the arts.
- **1206 · The Delhi Sultanate** is founded.
- **1526 · Babur** founds the Mughal Empire.
- **1857 · The great uprising** against East India Company rule.
- **1947 · Independence and partition** create India and Pakistan.
- **1971 · Bangladesh** becomes independent after war.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know South Asia the way a strong first year of a university area studies course expects. You will be able to:

- **Trace South Asian history:** from the Indus to the present.
- **Explain religions and languages:** their diversity, interaction and change.
- **Explain colonial rule:** how it worked and how it was resisted.
- **Explain partition and new nations:** and why their histories are contested.
- **Discuss the region today:** democracy, development, migration and culture.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Scholars from several South Asian countries and from outside the region will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
