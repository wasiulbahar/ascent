# Development Studies

Why some countries are rich and others poor, and what can change it: poverty and its measurement, growth and institutions, aid and its critics, health, education and gender, and the debates over what development should mean. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. International agencies publish free data and reports, and several key books are free. The path gives real weight to scholars from Africa, Asia and Latin America, and presents the sharp debates over aid, markets and the meaning of development fairly.

# About development studies

Development studies asks how societies escape poverty and improve people's lives. It draws on economics, politics, sociology, geography and history, and studies questions such as why growth happens, whether aid works, and what “development” should even mean.

This path begins with books that make global poverty and progress vivid, teaches how poverty is measured, explains theories of growth and institutions, examines aid and its critics, explores health, education and gender, and ends with today's debates.

**Trivia**
- The share of the world's people living in extreme poverty fell from about 38% in 1990 to under 10% before the COVID-19 pandemic.
- The Human Development Index, created in 1990, ranks countries by health, education and income, not income alone.
- South Korea was poorer than Ghana in the late 1950s by some measures; by the 2000s it was one of the world's richest countries.
- Microcredit pioneer Muhammad Yunus and the Grameen Bank shared the 2006 Nobel Peace Prize.

### Timeline
- **1944 · Bretton Woods** creates the IMF and World Bank.
- **1949 · Truman's Point Four** launches modern development aid.
- **1960s · Dependency theory** argues poor countries are kept poor by rich ones.
- **1980s · Structural adjustment** and the debt crisis.
- **1990 · The Human Development Index** is created by Mahbub ul Haq and Amartya Sen.
- **1999 · *Development as Freedom*** Amartya Sen.
- **2000 · The Millennium Development Goals** are agreed.
- **2015 · The Sustainable Development Goals** replace them.
- **2019 · Banerjee, Duflo and Kremer** win the Nobel for experiments against poverty.
- **2020 · The COVID-19 pandemic** pushes extreme poverty up for the first time in a generation.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know development studies the way a strong first year of a university course expects. You will be able to:

- **Measure poverty and well-being:** income, the poverty line and the Human Development Index.
- **Explain theories of development:** growth, institutions, dependency and the developmental state.
- **Weigh aid:** its successes, failures and critics.
- **Explain human development:** health, education, gender and capabilities.
- **Join the debates:** markets and states, growth and sustainability, and who decides.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Development scholars and practitioners from more than one region, including the Global South, will confirm them before the path is published.
