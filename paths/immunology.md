# Immunology

How the body defends itself: innate and adaptive immunity, antibodies and T cells, vaccines from smallpox to mRNA, allergy, autoimmunity and immunodeficiency, and immunotherapy for cancer. About 200 to 250 hours over eight to ten months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free open textbooks, Nobel Prize materials and health agency guides cover the core. You need school biology; the Biology path is a good start. This path is for understanding, not medical advice: talk to a health professional about your own health and vaccinations.

# About immunology

Immunology studies the immune system: the cells and molecules that recognise and destroy invaders, remember them, and normally leave our own tissues alone. It explains infection, vaccination, allergy, autoimmune disease and transplant rejection.

This path begins with lively introductions, explains the fast innate defences and the precise adaptive system, follows vaccines from Jenner to mRNA, studies what happens when immunity fails or attacks the body, and ends with immunotherapy and current research.

**Trivia**
- The word “vaccine” comes from the Latin *vacca*, cow, because Edward Jenner used cowpox to protect against smallpox.
- Ilya Metchnikoff discovered phagocytes after pushing rose thorns into starfish larvae and watching cells swarm around them.
- Before vaccination, people in China, India and the Ottoman Empire practised variolation, deliberately infecting people with mild smallpox.
- The 2025 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine honoured the discovery of regulatory T cells, which stop the immune system attacking the body.

### Timeline
- **1721 · Lady Mary Wortley Montagu** promotes variolation in England, learned in the Ottoman Empire.
- **1796 · Edward Jenner** vaccinates with cowpox against smallpox.
- **1882 · Ilya Metchnikoff** discovers phagocytosis.
- **1885 · Louis Pasteur** uses a rabies vaccine on a human.
- **1890 · Emil von Behring and Kitasato Shibasaburō** discover antitoxins, later called antibodies.
- **1957 · Frank Macfarlane Burnet** proposes clonal selection.
- **1975 · Köhler and Milstein** make monoclonal antibodies.
- **1980 · The WHO** declares smallpox eradicated.
- **2018 · Allison and Honjo** win the Nobel Prize for checkpoint immunotherapy.
- **2020 · mRNA vaccines** are authorised against COVID-19.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know immunology the way a strong undergraduate course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain innate immunity:** barriers, inflammation, phagocytes and pattern recognition.
- **Explain adaptive immunity:** B cells, antibodies, T cells, MHC and immune memory.
- **Explain vaccines:** how they work, their types, their history and their safety monitoring.
- **Explain immune disorders:** allergy, autoimmunity, tolerance and immunodeficiency.
- **Discuss immunotherapy:** checkpoint inhibitors, CAR-T cells and current research.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Immunologists and biomedical educators from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.
