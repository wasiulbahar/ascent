# Project Management

How to deliver projects on time, on budget and worth doing: scoping and planning, scheduling and estimating, agile methods, people and risk, lessons from famous megaprojects, and tools, certifications and practice. About 120 to 150 hours over five to six months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free open textbooks, guides and the agile classics cover the core. Professional certificates cost money and are optional; this path prepares you for them without requiring them. Manage a real project as you learn, however small.

# About project management

Project management is the discipline of planning, organising and leading work towards a specific goal with limited time, money and people. It is used everywhere, from building bridges and software to running events and research.

This path starts with why projects fail and the basics, teaches scope, schedules, estimates and budgets, covers agile methods, deals with people, stakeholders and risk, learns from famous successes and disasters, and ends with tools, certifications and your own project.

**Trivia**
- The Sydney Opera House opened ten years late, at roughly 14 times its original budget, yet became a beloved landmark.
- In Bent Flyvbjerg's database of thousands of big projects, only about 0.5% were delivered on budget, on time and with the promised benefits.
- Brooks's law states that adding people to a late software project makes it later.
- The critical path method was developed in the late 1950s at DuPont, and PERT for the US Navy's Polaris missile programme.

### Timeline
- **c. 2560 BCE · The Great Pyramid of Giza** is completed by a huge organised workforce.
- **1910s · Henry Gantt** develops bar charts for scheduling.
- **1957 · The critical path method** is developed at DuPont.
- **1958 · PERT** is created for the Polaris programme.
- **1965 · Bruce Tuckman** describes forming, storming, norming and performing.
- **1969 · The Project Management Institute** is founded; Apollo 11 lands on the Moon.
- **1973 · The Sydney Opera House** opens after years of delays.
- **1975 · Fred Brooks** publishes *The Mythical Man-Month*.
- **1996 · PRINCE2** is published.
- **2001 · The Agile Manifesto** is written.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will manage projects the way a capable professional project manager does. You will be able to:

- **Define projects:** goals, scope, stakeholders and a work breakdown structure.
- **Plan and control:** schedules, critical paths, estimates, budgets and earned value.
- **Work agile:** Scrum and Kanban, and when to use them.
- **Lead people and risk:** teams, stakeholders, communication and risk registers.
- **Learn from experience:** megaproject lessons, retrospectives and continuous improvement.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Project managers and management educators from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages makes you a capable project manager. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Project Management** certificate, you also complete:

1. **The grand quiz.** 40 game-style questions across all six stages. Pass mark 80%, with unlimited retakes.
2. **Project plan.** Submit a full plan: scope, schedule, budget, stakeholders and risks.
3. **Real project.** Deliver a small real project and report results against your plan.
4. **Capstone report.** 2,500 words on one of the Stage 6 questions. Strong reports are read by an expert reviewer.
5. **Explain it.** Answer three unseen questions as if to a new team member. For example: “Why is the project late if everyone is busy?”

The certificate needs every stage passed. It is not a PMP or PRINCE2 certification.
