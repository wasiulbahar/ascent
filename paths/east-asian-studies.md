# East Asian studies

China, Japan and Korea: their long histories, classical philosophies, great literature, modern upheavals and economic transformations, and East Asia today. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. The classical texts are free in older translations. The path treats China, Japan and Korea as distinct civilisations with deep connections, includes writers from each, and presents debated history (such as wars and famines) with care.

# About East Asian studies

East Asian studies explores the histories, cultures, languages, philosophies, politics and economies of China, Japan and Korea, which have shared writing systems, Confucian and Buddhist traditions, and long interactions, while developing in very different ways. Today the region is home to about a fifth of humanity and some of the world's largest economies.

This path begins with gripping memoirs, novels and histories, reads the classical philosophers, explores great works of literature, follows the region's turbulent modern history, examines its economic transformations and societies, and ends with East Asia today.

**Trivia**
- Chinese characters were also used to write Japanese and Korean; Korea's own alphabet, Hangul, was created under King Sejong in the 1440s.
- *The Tale of Genji*, written by Murasaki Shikibu around 1000, is often called the world's first novel.
- Paper, printing, the compass and gunpowder, the “Four Great Inventions”, all came from China.
- South Korea went from one of the world's poorest countries in the 1950s to one of the richest within two generations.

### Timeline
- **c. 1600–1046 BCE · The Shang dynasty** The earliest Chinese writing on oracle bones.
- **551–479 BCE · Confucius** by tradition.
- **221 BCE · Qin Shi Huang** unifies China.
- **c. 1000 · *The Tale of Genji*** is written in Japan.
- **1443–46 · Hangul** is created in Korea.
- **1603–1868 · The Tokugawa shogunate** in Japan.
- **1868 · The Meiji Restoration** begins Japan's rapid modernisation.
- **1911 · The Qing dynasty falls**
- **1945 · The end of the Second World War** and the division of Korea.
- **1949 · The People's Republic of China** is founded.
- **1978 · China's reform and opening** begins under Deng Xiaoping.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know East Asian studies the way a strong first year of a university East Asian studies course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain each civilisation's history:** China, Japan and Korea, and how they interacted.
- **Read classical thought:** Confucianism, Daoism, Buddhism and strategic thinking.
- **Read great literature:** from *Genji* and the *Story of the Stone* to modern fiction.
- **Explain the modern century:** revolution, war, occupation, division and recovery.
- **Analyse today:** economic growth, politics, culture and the region's place in the world.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Scholars of China, Japan and Korea, including scholars from the region, will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
