# Geography

How places and people fit together: landscapes and climates, cities and migration, maps and GIS, the big ideas of geography, and the world's changing map. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free textbooks, map software and world data cover much of the path, and the best fieldwork is your own neighbourhood. The path draws on examples from every continent.

# About geography

Geography studies the Earth as the home of people: where things are, why they are there, and how places connect. Physical geography looks at landforms, rivers, climate and ecosystems; human geography looks at population, cities, economies, cultures and politics. Maps and geographic information systems (GIS) tie them together.

This path begins with books on why place matters, covers physical geography, then human geography, teaches maps and GIS, reads the field's big ideas, and ends with the world's changing geography: climate migration, cities and power.

**Trivia**
- More than half the world's people have lived in cities since about 2007, according to the United Nations.
- The Nile and the Amazon compete for the title of the world's longest river, depending on how their sources are measured.
- Waldo Tobler's “first law of geography” (1970): everything is related to everything else, but near things are more related than distant things.
- OpenStreetMap, a free map of the world, is made by millions of volunteers.

### Timeline
- **c. 600 BCE · The Babylonian world map** One of the oldest known maps of the world.
- **c. 150 CE · Ptolemy's *Geography*** Coordinates for thousands of places.
- **1154 · Al-Idrisi's world map** made for Roger II of Sicily.
- **1569 · Mercator's projection**
- **1854 · John Snow's cholera map**
- **1884 · Köppen's climate zones** first proposed.
- **1960s · The first GIS** Computer mapping begins.
- **2004 · OpenStreetMap** is founded.
- **2007 · The world becomes mostly urban**

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know geography the way a strong first year of a university geography degree expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain physical patterns:** landforms, rivers, climates and biomes.
- **Explain human patterns:** population, migration, cities and development.
- **Read and make maps:** projections, scale, and the basics of GIS.
- **Use geographical ideas:** place, space, scale and connection.
- **Weigh today's debates:** climate migration, urbanisation and the politics of territory.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Geographers from more than one continent will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages makes you a capable student of geography. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Geography** certificate, you also complete:

1. **The grand quiz.** 40 game-style questions across all six stages. Pass mark 80%, with unlimited retakes.
2. **Map task.** Make a thematic map with free GIS software and explain what it shows.
3. **Fieldwork.** Observe and record one local place, with notes, sketches or photos, and explain its geography.
4. **Capstone essay.** 2,500 words on one of the Stage 6 questions. Strong essays are read by an expert reviewer.
