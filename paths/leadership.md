# Leadership

How people lead and follow: classic and modern theories, leadership across cultures, self-awareness and teams, decisions and change, power and ethics, and leading through crisis. About 150 to 180 hours over six to eight months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free research guides and courses cover the core, and several classic books are worth buying or borrowing. The path separates research evidence from popular advice and includes leaders and ideas from many cultures.

# About leadership

Leadership is the process of influencing a group towards shared goals. It happens in governments, companies, schools, teams, movements and families, and it can be learned.

This path starts with what leadership is and some remarkable leaders, studies the main theories and how cultures differ, works on self-awareness and team climate, covers decisions, change and negotiation, examines power, ethics and bad leadership, and ends with leading through crisis and building your own practice.

**Trivia**
- The *Tao Te Ching* says the best leader is one people barely know exists.
- All 28 men of Shackleton's *Endurance* expedition survived after their ship was crushed by Antarctic ice in 1915.
- Google's Project Aristotle found that psychological safety mattered most for its teams' effectiveness.
- The GLOBE study surveyed about 17,000 managers in 62 societies about leadership.

### Timeline
- **c. 400 BCE · The *Tao Te Ching*** describes the leader who barely seems to lead.
- **1513 · Niccolò Machiavelli** writes *The Prince*.
- **1840 · Thomas Carlyle** lectures on heroes, inspiring “great man” theory.
- **1939 · Lewin, Lippitt and White** study autocratic, democratic and laissez-faire styles.
- **1967 · Fred Fiedler** proposes a contingency theory of leadership.
- **1970 · Robert Greenleaf** writes “The Servant as Leader”.
- **1978 · James MacGregor Burns** describes transforming leadership.
- **2004 · The GLOBE study** publishes its findings on culture and leadership.
- **2015 · Google** publishes Project Aristotle's findings on teams.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will understand leadership the way a strong university course in leadership and organisational behaviour expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain the theories:** traits, styles, contingency, transformational, servant and adaptive leadership.
- **Lead people and teams:** with motivation, feedback and psychological safety.
- **Lead decisions and change:** avoiding groupthink, negotiating and managing change.
- **Use power ethically:** and recognise bad leadership and its followers.
- **Lead across cultures and crises:** and build your own development plan.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Leadership researchers and practitioners from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
