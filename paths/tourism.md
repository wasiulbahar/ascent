# Tourism and Hospitality

How travel shapes economies, cultures and places: the tourism system and its businesses, heritage and authenticity, overtourism, climate and ecotourism, crises and resilience, accessible and sustainable tourism, and careers and research. About 120 to 150 hours over five to six months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free UN Tourism, UNESCO and industry data and reports cover the core. No background is needed. The path looks at tourism from the side of travellers, workers, host communities and the environment, across every region.

# About tourism

Tourism is one of the world's largest industries, moving well over a billion international travellers a year and employing millions in hotels, transport, food, guiding and attractions.

This path begins with the scale and story of tourism, studies how the tourism system and hospitality businesses work, explores heritage, authenticity and community tourism, examines overtourism, climate and ecotourism, covers crises, small islands and accessibility, and ends with digital platforms, sustainability standards, careers and research.

**Trivia**
- Thomas Cook's first organised trip in 1841 took about 500 people 11 miles by train to a temperance meeting.
- International tourist arrivals reached about 1.5 billion in 2019, then fell by about 73% in 2020.
- Yellowstone, created in 1872, is usually called the world's first national park.
- In 2024 Venice became the first city to charge day-trippers an entry fee.

### Timeline
- **1841 · Thomas Cook** organises his first excursion.
- **1872 · Yellowstone** becomes the first national park.
- **1958 · Jet airliners** begin regular transatlantic service, opening mass air travel.
- **1972 · UNESCO** adopts the World Heritage Convention.
- **1975 · The World Tourism Organization** is established.
- **2008 · Airbnb** is founded.
- **2019 · International arrivals** reach about 1.5 billion.
- **2020 · COVID-19** collapses international tourism.
- **2024 · Venice** introduces a day-tripper fee.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will understand tourism the way a strong university course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain the tourism system:** demand, transport, accommodation, attractions and distribution.
- **Understand hospitality business:** service, revenue management and destination marketing.
- **Analyse culture and heritage:** authenticity, pilgrimage and community-based tourism.
- **Weigh impacts:** overtourism, climate, ecotourism, exploitation and resilience.
- **Plan sustainably and fairly:** accessibility, standards, platforms and local benefit.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Tourism researchers and professionals from more than one region will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
