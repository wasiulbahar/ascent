# Hinduism

The study of Hindu traditions as history, texts, philosophy and lived practice: the Vedas and Upaniṣads, the epics and the Bhagavad Gītā, gods and devotion, schools of philosophy, temples, festivals and rituals, caste and reform, and Hinduism in the modern world and diaspora. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Many Hindu texts are free in translation, and museums share temple art online. This is an academic path, neither promoting nor criticising Hinduism. It presents traditions in their own words alongside scholarship, reflects the great diversity within Hindu traditions, and covers contested history and politics through several perspectives.

# About Hinduism

Hinduism is a family of religious traditions originating in South Asia, with over a billion followers. It has no single founder or creed, and includes many gods, texts, philosophies, practices and communities, from the Vedas to village festivals.

This path begins with accessible introductions, studies the Vedas and Upaniṣads, reads the great epics and the Bhagavad Gītā, explores gods, devotion and temples, examines the philosophical schools, and ends with caste, reform, politics and Hinduism worldwide.

**Trivia**
- There are about 1.2 billion Hindus, around 15% of the world's population, most in India and Nepal.
- The Ṛgveda, composed around 1500–1200 BCE, is among the oldest religious texts still used in worship.
- Angkor Wat in Cambodia was built in the 12th century as a Hindu temple to Viṣṇu before becoming Buddhist.
- The 2025 Kumbh Mela at Prayagraj recorded hundreds of millions of pilgrim visits over six weeks, by official counts.

### Timeline
- **c. 1500–1200 BCE · The Ṛgveda** is composed.
- **c. 800–200 BCE · The principal Upaniṣads** explore brahman and ātman.
- **c. 400 BCE–400 CE · The *Mahābhārata* and *Rāmāyaṇa*** take shape.
- **c. 200 BCE–200 CE · The Bhagavad Gītā** is composed.
- **c. 300–600 CE · The Purāṇas** develop devotion to Viṣṇu, Śiva and the Goddess.
- **c. 600–900 CE · Tamil bhakti poets** spread devotional movements.
- **c. 8th century · Śaṅkara** teaches Advaita Vedānta.
- **1828 · Ram Mohan Roy** founds the Brahmo Samaj.
- **1893 · Swami Vivekananda** speaks at the World's Parliament of Religions.
- **1950 · India's constitution** abolishes untouchability.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know Hinduism the way a strong first year of a university religious studies course expects. You will be able to:

- **Describe Hindu diversity:** traditions, gods, practices and communities.
- **Read key texts:** Vedas, Upaniṣads, epics, the Gītā and devotional poetry.
- **Explain core concepts:** dharma, karma, saṃsāra, mokṣa, bhakti and brahman.
- **Compare philosophical schools:** Advaita, Viśiṣṭādvaita, Sāṃkhya, Yoga and more.
- **Discuss Hinduism in society:** caste, reform, gender, politics and diaspora.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Scholars of Hinduism and practitioners from several traditions and countries will review the path before it is published.

5. **Explain it.** Answer three unseen questions as if to a curious friend. For example: “Do Hindus believe in one God or many?”

The certificate needs every stage passed.
