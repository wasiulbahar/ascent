# Comics and Graphic Novels

How words and pictures tell stories together: how comics work, their history from newspaper strips to superheroes, manga and bande dessinée, the graphic novel and comics journalism, comics from Africa, South Asia and the Arab world, and how comics are made. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Libraries lend graphic novels widely, many webcomics are free, and museums and archives share historical comics online. The path covers comics from Japan, Europe, the Americas, Africa and Asia, includes work for adults and young readers, and notes when material is mature.

# About comics and graphic novels

Comics tell stories through sequences of images, usually with words, arranged in panels. They range from newspaper strips and superhero series to Japanese manga, Franco-Belgian bande dessinée, webcomics and serious graphic memoirs and reportage.

This path begins with how comics work, follows their history in the US, Japan and Europe, studies the graphic novel and memoir, explores comics journalism and comics from around the world, and ends with making comics and the industry today.

**Trivia**
- Art Spiegelman's *Maus* won a special Pulitzer Prize in 1992, the first comic to do so.
- Osamu Tezuka, the “god of manga”, created *Astro Boy* and drew around 150,000 pages in his lifetime.
- Hergé's *Tintin* albums have sold over 200 million copies and been translated into more than 100 languages.
- Webtoons, vertical-scrolling comics from South Korea, now reach hundreds of millions of readers on phones.

### Timeline
- **1830s · Rodolphe Töpffer** publishes picture stories in Switzerland.
- **1895 · *The Yellow Kid*** appears in US newspapers.
- **1929 · *Tintin*** first appears in Belgium.
- **1938 · Superman** launches the superhero comic book.
- **1947 · Osamu Tezuka's *New Treasure Island*** transforms manga.
- **1954 · The US Comics Code** restricts comic content.
- **1978 · Will Eisner's *A Contract with God*** popularises the term “graphic novel”.
- **1986–91 · *Maus*** is published in book form.
- **2000 · *Persepolis*** appears in France.
- **2000s · Korean webtoons** spread worldwide.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know comics the way a strong first year of a university comics studies course expects. You will be able to:

- **Read comics closely:** panels, gutters, layout, lettering and colour.
- **Trace comics history:** in the US, Japan, Europe and beyond.
- **Analyse graphic novels:** memoir, fiction and journalism.
- **Compare traditions:** manga, bande dessinée, superhero and webcomics.
- **Make a short comic:** script, thumbnail, draw and letter.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Comics scholars and artists from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages makes you a capable reader and student of comics. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Comics and Graphic Novels** certificate, you also complete:

1. **The grand quiz.** 40 game-style questions across all six stages. Pass mark 80%, with unlimited retakes.
2. **Page analysis.** Analyse an unseen comics page: panels, layout, lettering and meaning.
3. **Short comic.** Create a short comic and explain your choices.
4. **Capstone essay.** 2,500 words on one of the Stage 6 questions. Strong essays are read by an expert reviewer.
5. **Explain it.** Answer three unseen questions as if to a curious friend. For example: “Why read comics if you're an adult?”

The certificate needs every stage passed.
