# Maternal and Newborn Health

How to keep mothers and babies alive and well: the global picture, healthy pregnancy, safe and respectful birth, newborn survival, midwives, family planning and mental health, and how countries cut deaths dramatically. About 120 to 150 hours over five to six months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free WHO, UNICEF and research resources cover nearly everything. No background is needed. This path is for understanding, not for giving care: anyone pregnant should see a midwife, nurse or doctor. It covers care in every kind of setting, from home births to hospitals.

# About maternal and newborn health

Maternal and newborn health covers pregnancy, childbirth, the weeks after birth and the first month of a baby's life. Most maternal and newborn deaths are preventable with known, often simple, care.

This path begins with the global picture and the history of midwifery, studies healthy pregnancy and its complications, covers safe and respectful birth, newborn survival and breastfeeding, examines midwives, family planning, mental health and equity, and ends with country success stories, history and research.

**Trivia**
- About 260,000 women died from pregnancy and childbirth in 2023, most from preventable causes, according to the WHO.
- The Apgar score for newborns was created in 1952 by the anaesthetist Virginia Apgar.
- Kangaroo mother care, holding small babies skin-to-skin, began in Bogotá, Colombia, in 1978.
- The SDGs aim to cut the global maternal death rate to below 70 per 100,000 live births by 2030.

### Timeline
- **1847 · Ignaz Semmelweis** shows handwashing prevents deadly childbed fever.
- **1935 · Sulfonamide drugs** begin to cut deaths from childbed fever.
- **1952 · Virginia Apgar** introduces the newborn Apgar score.
- **1960 · The contraceptive pill** is approved in the United States.
- **1978 · Kangaroo mother care** begins in Bogotá.
- **1987 · The Safe Motherhood Initiative** is launched in Nairobi.
- **2010 · Helping Babies Breathe** starts training birth attendants worldwide.
- **2015 · The SDGs** set a global target for maternal deaths.
- **2023 · The E-MOTIVE trial** shows a bundle of care sharply cuts severe bleeding after birth.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will understand maternal and newborn health the way a strong global health course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain the global picture:** causes of maternal and newborn deaths and who is most at risk.
- **Explain pregnancy care:** antenatal care, nutrition and complications such as pre-eclampsia.
- **Explain safe birth:** skilled attendance, bleeding, infection, caesareans and respectful care.
- **Explain newborn care:** resuscitation, warmth, kangaroo care, breastfeeding and infection.
- **Analyse systems and equity:** midwives, family planning, mental health and what works at scale.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Midwives, obstetricians, paediatricians and global health experts from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
