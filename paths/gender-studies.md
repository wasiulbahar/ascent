# Gender studies

How ideas of gender shape lives and societies: the classic feminist texts, gender at work and at home, men and masculinities, perspectives from around the world, the major theories, and today's debates. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Several classics and all UN reports are free. Gender is debated, so the path presents different positions, including critics, and asks you to weigh evidence and arguments rather than adopt one view.

# About gender studies

Gender studies examines how societies understand being women, men and other genders, and how those ideas shape work, family, health, law, politics, culture and identity. It studies how gender interacts with class, race, sexuality and nationality, and draws on sociology, history, biology, psychology, law and literature.

This path begins with short, influential books, reads the classic feminist texts, studies gender in work, the home and masculinity, widens to perspectives from the Global South and the UN, reads the major theories, and ends with today's debates and data.

**Trivia**
- Mary Wollstonecraft's *A Vindication of the Rights of Woman* (1792) is one of the earliest works of feminist philosophy.
- New Zealand in 1893 was the first self-governing country to give women the vote in national elections.
- The legal scholar Kimberlé Crenshaw coined the term “intersectionality” in 1989.
- The 1995 Beijing World Conference on Women adopted a Platform for Action covering twelve critical areas.

### Timeline
- **1792 · Wollstonecraft's *Vindication***
- **1848 · The Seneca Falls Convention** on women's rights in the US.
- **1893 · New Zealand** Women win the national vote.
- **1929 · Woolf's *A Room of One's Own***
- **1949 · Beauvoir's *The Second Sex***
- **1979 · CEDAW** the UN women's rights treaty.
- **1989 · Crenshaw** coins “intersectionality”.
- **1990 · Butler's *Gender Trouble***
- **1995 · The Beijing Platform for Action**
- **2017 · #MeToo** spreads worldwide.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know gender studies the way a strong first year of a university gender-studies course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain key concepts:** sex and gender, patriarchy, masculinities, intersectionality.
- **Read the classics:** Wollstonecraft, Woolf, Beauvoir, hooks and others.
- **Analyse gender in society:** work, care, health, violence, politics and the media.
- **Include the world:** perspectives from the Global South and international data.
- **Weigh the debates:** about biology and culture, boys and men, and identity, using evidence.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Scholars of gender from more than one continent, with differing views, will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
