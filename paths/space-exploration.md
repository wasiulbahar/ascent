# Space Exploration

How humans and machines reach space: rockets and orbits, the space race and the Moon, robotic explorers across the Solar System, living in space, and the debates over who space belongs to. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Space agencies publish huge amounts of free material, and NASA images and data are free to use. The path tells space history from more than one country, and presents debates about cost, commercialisation and space law fairly.

# About space exploration

Space exploration is the use of rockets, spacecraft and people to explore beyond Earth. It combines physics and engineering with politics, money and human courage, and has sent robots to every planet and people to the Moon.

This path begins with gripping stories of spaceflight, teaches the physics of rockets and orbits, tells the history of the space race, follows robotic missions across the Solar System, explores human spaceflight and its risks, and ends with the commercial space age and the law and ethics of space.

**Trivia**
- Sputnik 1, launched by the Soviet Union in 1957, was the first artificial satellite.
- Twelve people walked on the Moon between 1969 and 1972.
- Voyager 1, launched in 1977, crossed into interstellar space in 2012.
- In 2023 India's Chandrayaan-3 became the first mission to land near the Moon's south pole.

### Timeline
- **1903 · Konstantin Tsiolkovsky** publishes the rocket equation.
- **1926 · Robert Goddard** launches the first liquid-fuelled rocket.
- **1957 · Sputnik 1** the first satellite.
- **1961 · Yuri Gagarin** becomes the first person in space.
- **1969 · Apollo 11** lands people on the Moon.
- **1977 · Voyager 1 and 2** are launched.
- **1998 · The International Space Station** begins assembly.
- **2003 · China's Yang Liwei** becomes the country's first astronaut.
- **2015 · SpaceX** lands an orbital rocket booster for the first time.
- **2021 · The James Webb Space Telescope** is launched.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know space exploration the way a strong first year of a university space science course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain rockets and orbits:** thrust, the rocket equation and how orbits work.
- **Tell the history:** the space race and spaceflight from many countries.
- **Explain robotic exploration:** probes, rovers and telescopes, and what they found.
- **Explain human spaceflight:** life support, risks and the effects of space on the body.
- **Weigh the debates:** cost, commercial space, space law and space debris.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Space scientists, engineers and historians from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.
