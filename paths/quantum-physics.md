# Quantum Physics

The physics of the very small: light as particles and atoms as waves, uncertainty and superposition, the Schrödinger equation, spin and entanglement, the interpretation debates, and quantum technology from lasers to quantum computers. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free lectures, textbooks and simulations cover the core. Early stages need little maths; later stages use algebra, complex numbers, vectors and some calculus, which the path points you to. It presents the competing interpretations fairly and separates real quantum physics from “quantum” hype.

# About quantum physics

Quantum physics describes the behaviour of atoms, light and subatomic particles, where energy comes in packets, particles act like waves, and outcomes are probabilistic. It is the most precisely tested theory in science and underlies chemistry, lasers, transistors and MRI.

This path begins with accessible books and the experiments that started it, teaches the core ideas, introduces the mathematics, explores spin and entanglement, examines the interpretation debates, and ends with quantum technologies and research.

**Trivia**
- Max Planck introduced energy “quanta” in 1900 to explain the light given off by hot objects.
- Einstein won his Nobel Prize for explaining the photoelectric effect with light quanta, not for relativity.
- Experiments testing Bell's inequality, which rule out local hidden variables, won the 2022 Nobel Prize in Physics.
- Quantum electrodynamics predicts the electron's magnetic moment to about 10 decimal places, matching experiment.

### Timeline
- **1900 · Max Planck** introduces energy quanta.
- **1905 · Einstein** explains the photoelectric effect with light quanta.
- **1913 · Niels Bohr** proposes a quantum model of the atom.
- **1924 · Louis de Broglie** proposes that particles have waves.
- **1925–26 · Heisenberg and Schrödinger** create quantum mechanics.
- **1927 · The Solvay Conference** hosts the Einstein–Bohr debates.
- **1935 · Einstein, Podolsky and Rosen** question quantum completeness; Schrödinger names entanglement.
- **1964 · John Bell** derives his inequality.
- **1982 · Alain Aspect's experiments** support quantum predictions.
- **2019 · Quantum computers** claim to outperform classical ones on a narrow task.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know quantum physics the way a strong first year of a university quantum course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain the experiments:** blackbody radiation, the photoelectric effect, atomic spectra and the double slit.
- **Explain core ideas:** quantisation, wave–particle duality, superposition, uncertainty and measurement.
- **Use basic quantum mathematics:** wavefunctions, probabilities and simple systems.
- **Explain spin and entanglement:** and what Bell tests show.
- **Weigh interpretations and technologies:** Copenhagen, many-worlds, pilot wave, and quantum computing claims.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.
