# Agriculture and food

How humanity feeds itself: the origins of farming, soils, crops and animals, the Green Revolution and its critics, farmers and food systems around the world, and how to feed ten billion people without wrecking the planet. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. FAO reports, open textbooks and global data are free. The path gives equal attention to large commercial farms and the hundreds of millions of smallholders who grow much of the world's food, and presents debates (such as genetic modification) from several sides.

# About agriculture and food

Agriculture is the cultivation of plants and animals for food, fibre and fuel. It began about 12,000 years ago and remains the world's largest employer and its biggest user of land and fresh water. Agricultural science studies soils, crops, livestock, pests and water; food-systems research follows food from farm to fork, including trade, waste, hunger and the environment.

This path begins with histories of food and farming, teaches the science of soils, plants and animals, studies the Green Revolution and the debates about technology, looks at farmers and food systems around the world, examines sustainability and climate, and ends with the future of food.

**Trivia**
- Farming began independently in several places, including the Fertile Crescent, China, Mesoamerica, the Andes, New Guinea and parts of Africa.
- Three crops, rice, wheat and maize, provide about half of the calories people eat worldwide.
- Roughly a third of food produced is lost or wasted, according to FAO estimates.
- Norman Borlaug, a plant breeder, won the 1970 Nobel Peace Prize for high-yield wheat that helped avert famine in South Asia and Mexico.

### Timeline
- **c. 10,000 BCE · Farming begins** in the Fertile Crescent, and later independently elsewhere.
- **c. 7000 BCE · Rice** is domesticated in China.
- **c. 7000 BCE · Maize's ancestor** is domesticated in Mexico.
- **18th century · The British Agricultural Revolution**
- **1909 · The Haber–Bosch process** makes synthetic nitrogen fertiliser possible.
- **1945 · The FAO** is founded.
- **1960s–70s · The Green Revolution** High-yield crops spread across Asia and Latin America.
- **1996 · GM crops** are first grown widely.
- **2019 · The EAT–Lancet Commission** proposes a planetary health diet.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know agriculture and food systems the way a strong first year of a university agriculture or food-systems course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain the origins of farming:** and how it changed human societies.
- **Explain the science:** soils, plant growth, breeding, pests and livestock.
- **Weigh the technology debates:** the Green Revolution, fertilisers, pesticides and genetic modification.
- **Analyse food systems:** smallholders, markets, trade, hunger and waste.
- **Plan for the future:** sustainable intensification, agroecology, climate and diets.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Agricultural scientists, farmers and food-systems researchers from more than one continent will confirm them before the path is published.
