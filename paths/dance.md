# Dance

How humans move to make meaning: dance across the world's cultures, classical traditions from ballet to Bharatanatyam, modern and contemporary dance, social and street dance, choreography, and how to watch and write about dance. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Archives such as Jacob's Pillow Dance Interactive and many recorded performances are free online. The path gives equal weight to dance traditions from Africa, Asia, the Americas and Europe, and to social and popular dance as well as the stage. Watching is central: most works include recordings.

# About dance

Dance is the art of moving the body in time, usually to music, to express, celebrate, worship, tell stories or simply enjoy. Every culture dances, and dance studies examines it as art, ritual, social life and history.

This path begins with ways into watching dance, studies dance as culture and ritual, explores classical traditions across the world, follows modern and contemporary dance, celebrates social and street dance, and ends with choreography, criticism and dance today.

**Trivia**
- Bharatanatyam, from Tamil Nadu in southern India, draws on the *Natya Shastra*, an ancient Sanskrit treatise on performance.
- Ballet developed in the Italian and French courts; Louis XIV founded the Royal Academy of Dance in 1661.
- Hip hop dance styles such as breaking began in the Bronx, New York, in the 1970s; breaking debuted at the Olympics in Paris in 2024.
- Labanotation, a system for writing down movement, was devised by Rudolf Laban in 1928.

### Timeline
- **c. 200 BCE–200 CE · The *Natya Shastra*** sets out Indian performance theory.
- **1661 · Louis XIV** founds the Royal Academy of Dance.
- **1892 · *The Nutcracker*** premieres in St Petersburg.
- **1913 · *The Rite of Spring*** causes a riot in Paris.
- **1926 · Martha Graham** founds her dance company.
- **1928 · Labanotation** is devised.
- **1958 · Alvin Ailey** founds his American Dance Theater.
- **1970s · Breaking** emerges in the Bronx.
- **1973 · Pina Bausch** takes over the Wuppertal company, developing dance theatre.
- **2024 · Breaking** appears at the Paris Olympics.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know dance the way a strong first year of a university dance studies course expects. You will be able to:

- **Watch dance closely:** describing movement, space, time and energy.
- **Explain dance as culture:** ritual, community and identity.
- **Compare traditions:** classical, folk, modern, contemporary and street dance.
- **Explain choreography:** how dances are made and recorded.
- **Write about dance:** a clear, evidence-based response to a performance.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Dance scholars and artists from more than one tradition will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages makes you a capable student of dance. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Dance** certificate, you also complete:

1. **The grand quiz.** 40 game-style questions across all six stages. Pass mark 80%, with unlimited retakes.
2. **Movement analysis.** Analyse an unseen dance video using body, effort, shape and space.
3. **Tradition profile.** Profile one dance tradition: origins, meaning and change.
4. **Capstone essay.** 2,500 words on one of the Stage 6 questions. Strong essays are read by an expert reviewer.
5. **Explain it.** Answer three unseen questions as if to a curious friend. For example: “Why does every culture dance?”

The certificate needs every stage passed.
