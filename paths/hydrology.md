# Hydrology

Where water goes on land: rain, evaporation and runoff, groundwater and aquifers, floods and droughts, sharing rivers and managing scarce water, and how hydrologists model and measure it from the ground and from space. About 120 to 160 hours over five to seven months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free manuals, open textbooks, satellite data and modelling software cover the core. You need school science and some algebra. Measuring rain and watching a local stream are part of every stage.

# About hydrology

Hydrology studies the movement, distribution and quality of water on land, from raindrops and rivers to aquifers deep underground.

This path starts with the water cycle and the global water crisis, learns how rain becomes runoff, explores groundwater, studies floods and droughts, examines how water is shared and managed, and ends with models, satellites and current research.

**Trivia**
- Only about 2.5% of Earth's water is fresh, and most of that is locked in ice or underground.
- Darcy's law of groundwater flow came from designing fountains for the city of Dijon.
- The Aral Sea was once one of the world's largest lakes before rivers feeding it were diverted for irrigation.
- Twin satellites measured groundwater loss by sensing tiny changes in Earth's gravity.

### Timeline
- **1674 · Pierre Perrault** shows rainfall is enough to feed the River Seine.
- **1856 · Henry Darcy** publishes his law of flow through sand.
- **1932 · LeRoy Sherman** introduces the unit hydrograph.
- **1933 · Robert Horton** explains infiltration and overland flow.
- **1960 · The Indus Waters Treaty** divides rivers between India and Pakistan.
- **1960s · Soviet irrigation schemes** begin draining the Aral Sea.
- **1979 · Freeze and Cherry** publish their classic groundwater textbook.
- **1998 · FAO** publishes the standard method for crop evaporation.
- **2002 · The GRACE satellites** begin measuring water through gravity.
- **2018 · Cape Town** narrowly avoids running out of municipal water.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know hydrology the way a strong undergraduate course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain the water cycle on land:** precipitation, evaporation, infiltration and runoff.
- **Explain groundwater:** aquifers, Darcy's law and depletion.
- **Analyse floods and droughts:** hydrographs, frequency and warning systems.
- **Discuss water management:** allocation, shared rivers and governance.
- **Use models and data:** from field gauges to satellites.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Hydrologists from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
