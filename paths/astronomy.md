# Astronomy

From the night sky and the planets to stars, galaxies, the origin of the universe, the sky as other cultures read it, and the discoveries of today's telescopes. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free textbooks, planetarium software and space-agency sites cover much of the path, and the sky itself is free. Astronomy is told as a story from many cultures, not only the European one.

# About astronomy

Astronomy studies everything beyond Earth: the Sun and planets, stars and how they live and die, galaxies, and the universe as a whole. Astrophysics uses physics to explain what telescopes see; cosmology asks how the universe began and how it will end.

This path begins with books and tools that make the sky wonderful, explores the solar system, explains stars and galaxies, reaches modern cosmology, looks at astronomy across history and cultures, and ends at the frontier: gravitational waves, the James Webb Space Telescope, and the search for life.

**Trivia**
- Light from the Sun takes about 8 minutes and 20 seconds to reach Earth.
- Some Aboriginal Australian peoples see an emu in the sky, traced not by stars but by dark dust clouds in the Milky Way.
- In 1924 Edwin Hubble showed that the Andromeda “nebula” is a separate galaxy, far outside our own.
- Thousands of planets have been found orbiting other stars since the first around a Sun-like star in 1995.

### Timeline
- **c. 1600 BCE · The Nebra sky disc** One of the oldest known pictures of the sky.
- **c. 150 CE · Ptolemy's *Almagest*** An Earth-centred model of the heavens.
- **1259 · The Maragha observatory** founded in Persia.
- **1543 · Copernicus** puts the Sun at the centre.
- **1610 · Galileo** sees Jupiter's moons through a telescope.
- **1924–29 · Hubble** Other galaxies, and an expanding universe.
- **1965 · The cosmic microwave background** is discovered.
- **1995 · The first planet around a Sun-like star** is found.
- **2015 · Gravitational waves** are detected by LIGO.
- **2021 · The James Webb Space Telescope** is launched.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know astronomy the way a strong first year of a university astronomy course expects. You will be able to:

- **Find your way around the sky:** constellations, planets, the Moon's phases and seasons.
- **Explain the solar system:** how planets form, what they are made of, and how we explore them.
- **Explain stars and galaxies:** how stars live and die, and how galaxies are arranged.
- **Explain cosmology:** the big bang, the expanding universe, dark matter and dark energy.
- **Read astronomy in history:** how many cultures watched the sky, and how science changed.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Astronomers and science educators from more than one continent will confirm them before the path is published.
