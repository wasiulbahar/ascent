# Bioethics

The ethics of medicine, life sciences and health: core principles, lessons from research scandals, consent and end-of-life care, reproduction and genetics, justice in public health and AI, and diverse ethical traditions. About 120 to 150 hours over five to six months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free reports from the Nuffield Council, the Hastings Center, the WHO and international codes cover the core. No background is needed; the Ethics path helps. The path presents several sides of hard questions and draws on ethical traditions beyond the Western canon.

# About bioethics

Bioethics examines moral questions raised by medicine, biology and health care: who decides, what we owe patients and research participants, how to share scarce resources, and how far to change human life.

This path begins with the four principles and leading bioethics bodies, studies the research scandals that created modern rules, covers consent, end-of-life care, organ donation and cultural difference, examines reproduction and genetics, explores justice in pandemics, global health and AI, and ends with ethics committees, non-Western traditions, animal research and current debates.

**Trivia**
- The Tuskegee study withheld treatment from Black men with syphilis for 40 years, from 1932 to 1972.
- Louise Brown, the first baby conceived by IVF, was born in England in 1978.
- In 1966 the anaesthetist Henry Beecher exposed 22 published studies that had been unethical.
- The four classic principles of biomedical ethics are autonomy, beneficence, non-maleficence and justice.

### Timeline
- **1947 · The Nuremberg Code** sets rules for human experiments after Nazi atrocities.
- **1964 · The Declaration of Helsinki** sets ethics for medical research.
- **1966 · Henry Beecher** exposes unethical research in the US.
- **1972 · The Tuskegee study** is exposed and ended.
- **1978 · Louise Brown** is born after IVF.
- **1979 · Beauchamp and Childress** publish *Principles of Biomedical Ethics*; the Belmont Report appears.
- **1984 · The Warnock Report** sets limits on embryo research in the UK.
- **2002 · The Netherlands** legalises euthanasia under strict conditions.
- **2015 · The UK** allows mitochondrial donation.
- **2018 · He Jiankui** announces gene-edited babies, provoking global condemnation.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will reason about bioethics the way a strong university course expects. You will be able to:

- **Use ethical frameworks:** the four principles, rights, care and other approaches.
- **Explain research ethics:** consent, risk, vulnerable groups and review committees.
- **Reason about clinical dilemmas:** capacity, end of life, organs and cultural difference.
- **Weigh reproductive and genetic questions:** IVF, embryos, editing and disability critiques.
- **Analyse justice:** pandemics, global access to medicines, vaccines and AI in health.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Bioethicists, clinicians and patient advocates from more than one region will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages gives you a strong grounding in bioethics. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Bioethics** certificate, you also complete:

1. **The grand quiz.** 40 game-style questions across all six stages. Pass mark 80%, with unlimited retakes.
2. **Case analysis.** Analyse a real clinical or research case using two ethical frameworks.
3. **Debate brief.** Write balanced briefs for both sides of a current bioethics debate.
4. **Capstone essay.** 2,500 words on one of the Stage 6 questions. Strong essays are read by an expert reviewer.
5. **Explain it.** Answer three unseen questions as if to a friend. For example: “Should we edit embryos' genes?”

The certificate needs every stage passed. It is not a clinical ethics qualification.
