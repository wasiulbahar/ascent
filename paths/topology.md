# Topology

The mathematics of shape without measurement, where a mug is a doughnut: open sets and continuity, surfaces and knots, algebraic topology, the Poincaré conjecture and other great results, and applications from data science to materials and DNA. About 180 to 220 hours over seven to nine months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free videos, a free classic textbook (Hatcher), MIT notes and prize materials cover the core. You need comfort with proofs (the Discrete mathematics and Real analysis paths help). Play with paper, string and clay: topology is very hands-on.

# About topology

Topology studies properties of shapes that survive stretching and bending but not tearing. It explains why a coffee mug and a doughnut are the same to a topologist, and why you can't comb a hairy ball flat.

This path begins with playful shapes and the idea of space, builds point-set topology, studies surfaces, the Euler characteristic and knots, develops algebraic topology, explores landmark results such as the Poincaré conjecture, and ends with applications in data, physics and biology.

**Trivia**
- To a topologist a coffee mug and a doughnut are the same shape, because each has exactly one hole.
- Grigori Perelman proved the Poincaré conjecture in 2003 and declined both a Fields Medal and a $1 million prize.
- The hairy ball theorem implies that at any moment there is somewhere on Earth with no horizontal wind.
- The word “topology” was coined by Johann Benedict Listing in 1847.

### Timeline
- **1736 · Leonhard Euler** solves the Königsberg bridges problem.
- **1750s · Euler** states his formula for polyhedra: V − E + F = 2.
- **1847 · Johann Listing** coins the word “topology”.
- **1858 · Möbius and Listing** discover the one-sided Möbius strip.
- **1882 · Felix Klein** describes the Klein bottle.
- **1895 · Henri Poincaré** founds algebraic topology in *Analysis Situs*.
- **1912 · L. E. J. Brouwer** proves his fixed-point theorem.
- **1984 · Vaughan Jones** discovers a new knot invariant.
- **2003 · Grigori Perelman** proves the Poincaré conjecture.
- **2016 · The Nobel Prize in Physics** honours topological phases of matter.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know topology the way a strong first university course expects. You will be able to:

- **Work with topological spaces:** open sets, continuity, compactness and connectedness.
- **Classify surfaces:** using orientability and the Euler characteristic.
- **Study knots:** diagrams, moves and invariants.
- **Use algebraic topology:** the fundamental group and homology.
- **Apply and appreciate:** fixed points, the Poincaré conjecture and applications in data and physics.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Mathematicians and mathematics educators from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
