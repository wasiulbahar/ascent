# Social Psychology

How other people shape what we think, feel and do: attribution and attitudes, conformity and groups, prejudice and identity, the replication crisis, and how findings are used to change behaviour. About 120 to 160 hours over five to seven months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free textbooks, original papers and podcasts cover the core. No background is needed. You will meet famous studies alongside the critiques and failed replications that came later.

# About social psychology

Social psychology studies how the real or imagined presence of others affects people's thoughts, feelings and behaviour.

This path starts with the power of situations, studies how we explain behaviour and hold attitudes, explores conformity and groups, examines prejudice and identity, faces the replication crisis honestly, and ends with applying findings and reading research.

**Trivia**
- In Asch's studies, most people gave at least one wrong answer about line lengths to match a group.
- People paid $1 to tell a lie later rated a dull task as more enjoyable than people paid $20.
- Tajfel found people favour their own group even when groups are formed by trivial criteria.
- A large share of famous social psychology findings failed to replicate in repeated large studies.

### Timeline
- **1898 · Norman Triplett** studies how cyclists ride faster with others present.
- **1951 · Solomon Asch** begins his line-judgement conformity studies.
- **1954 · Gordon Allport** publishes *The Nature of Prejudice*.
- **1954 · Muzafer Sherif** runs the Robbers Cave summer camp study.
- **1959 · Festinger and Carlsmith** demonstrate cognitive dissonance.
- **1967 · Jones and Harris** show people over-attribute behaviour to personality.
- **1971 · Henri Tajfel** publishes the minimal group experiments.
- **1979 · Latané, Williams and Harkins** measure social loafing.
- **1998 · Greenwald and colleagues** introduce the Implicit Association Test.
- **2010s · The replication crisis** prompts large multi-lab replications.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know social psychology the way a strong undergraduate course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain social cognition:** attribution, attitudes and dissonance.
- **Explain social influence:** conformity, groups and social loafing.
- **Explain prejudice:** identity, contact and implicit bias, with critiques.
- **Judge evidence:** replication, effect sizes and open science.
- **Apply findings:** carefully and ethically to real problems.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Social psychologists from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages makes you a capable student of social psychology. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Social Psychology** certificate, you also complete:

1. **The grand quiz.** 40 game-style questions across all six stages. Pass mark 80%, with unlimited retakes.
2. **Study critique.** Critique an unseen study's design and evidence.
3. **Study design.** Design and preregister a small study.
4. **Capstone essay.** 2,500 words on one of the Stage 6 questions. Strong essays are read by an expert reviewer.
5. **Explain it.** Answer three unseen questions as if to a friend. For example: “Why do people go along with a group they disagree with?”

The certificate needs every stage passed.
