# History

From gripping stories of the past to the arguments historians have about them. About 220 to 260 hours over nine to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. This is a path through world history, not one country's: it moves between China, the Islamic world, South Asia, Africa, Europe and the Americas, and gives a special thread to Bengal and the making of Bangladesh. It also teaches the historian's craft: how to read a source, and why historians disagree.

# About history

History is the study of the human past, built from evidence: documents, objects, buildings, oral memories, data. It tells us how the world we live in came to be. But history is not just a list of what happened. It is an argument about what the evidence means, written by people who are themselves part of history. Two honest historians can read the same sources and tell very different stories.

This path begins with the great storytellers, then teaches you how historians actually work, then travels through the ancient, medieval and modern worlds, the twentieth century, and finally the arguments about how history should be written at all, and by whom.

**Trivia**
- Herodotus has been called both "the father of history" and "the father of lies", for including fabulous tales alongside careful research.
- The great Chinese historian Sima Qian was castrated as punishment for defending a disgraced general, and chose to live on to finish his history.
- The traveller Ibn Battuta reached Bengal in the 1340s and visited the Sufi saint Shah Jalal in Sylhet.
- The word "loot" came into English from Hindustani, through the East India Company's conquests in India.
- Fernand Braudel wrote much of his masterpiece on the Mediterranean from memory, as a prisoner of war in Germany.

### Timeline
- **c. 3000 BCE · The first states** Writing and cities in Mesopotamia and Egypt; soon after, the Indus Valley and early China.
- **c. 430 BCE · Herodotus** The first Greek history; in China, Sima Qian follows around 100 BCE.
- **27 BCE – 476 CE · Rome** The Roman Empire in the West; the Han dynasty in China rules a comparable empire at the same time.
- **7th–15th century · The medieval world** Islam spreads from Arabia to Spain and South Asia; Song China flourishes; Ibn Battuta crosses the known world.
- **1492 onwards · Global encounters** European voyages, the conquest of the Americas, and the Atlantic slave trade.
- **1757 · Plassey** The East India Company wins Bengal; British rule in India begins.
- **1789–1848 · The age of revolution** The French and Industrial Revolutions transform politics and work.
- **1914–1945 · The world wars** Two world wars, the Great Depression and the Holocaust.
- **1947 · Partition** British India is divided into India and Pakistan.
- **1971 · Bangladesh** East Pakistan becomes Bangladesh after a war of liberation.
- **1989–1991 · The end of the Cold War** The Berlin Wall falls and the Soviet Union breaks up.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know history the way a good university introductory and second-year sequence expects. You will be able to:

- **Place events in time and place** across five thousand years and every continent.
- **Read a primary source critically:** who wrote it, when, for whom, and what they left out.
- **Explain how historians work,** and the main schools: narrative history, Marxist history, the Annales school, microhistory, global history and postcolonial history.
- **Know the history of South Asia and Bengal,** from the Persianate world to Plassey, Partition and 1971.
- **Have read at least five major historians,** including one whose views most scholars reject (Ferguson on empire) and one who challenges the field's centre (Chakrabarty).
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Expert reviewers from more than one region and specialism will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.

## Sapiens
- id: h1 · type: read · creator: Yuval Noah Harari · year: 2011 (English 2014) · length: about 15 hours
- access: paid | buy, or borrow from a library

### Introduction
How did one species of ape come to rule the planet? Harari's answer runs through three revolutions: the cognitive revolution, when humans learned to believe shared stories; the agricultural revolution; and the scientific revolution. It is a thrilling, provocative read, and also a book many specialists criticise. Enjoy it, and keep a list of claims you'd want to check.

**Trivia**
- Harari teaches history at the Hebrew University of Jerusalem.
- He calls the agricultural revolution "history's biggest fraud", because farming made most people's lives harder.

### Analyse
- **Debate:** anthropologists and historians have criticised the book for sweeping claims and thin evidence. Part of this path's point is to learn to tell a great story from a well-evidenced one.

## What Is History?
- id: h5 · type: read · creator: E. H. Carr · year: 1961 · length: about 5 hours

### Introduction

# Stage 6: How History Is Written
level: Expert
intent: The great schools, the challenge from beyond Europe, and a defence of empire to argue against.

The last stage turns to historiography: the history of how history is written. You'll read a masterpiece of the Annales school, a classic of microhistory, a challenge to Europe-centred history from a historian of Bengal, a provocative defence of the British Empire that most historians reject, and current research.

## The Mediterranean and the Mediterranean World in the Age of Philip II (part 1)
- id: h21 · type: read · creator: Fernand Braudel · year: 1949 · length: about 15 hours for part 1
- access: paid | buy, or borrow from a library

### Introduction
Braudel's masterpiece looks at the sixteenth-century Mediterranean on three timescales: the almost unchanging *longue durée* of mountains, seas and climate; the slower rhythms of economies and societies; and the fast surface of events and battles. It changed how historians think about time.

**Trivia**
- He wrote much of it from memory while a prisoner of war in Germany during the Second World War.
