# Jazz

America's great improvised art form and its journeys around the world: roots in New Orleans and African American culture, swing, bebop, cool, modal, free and fusion, the great musicians and singers, how improvisation works, and jazz in Africa, Europe, Latin America and Asia. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Most recordings stream free, and oral histories and archives are online. The path centres listening, the African American roots and Black musicians who created jazz, women in jazz, and jazz's global life, and presents debates about jazz's boundaries fairly.

# About jazz

Jazz emerged in the early 20th century in New Orleans from African American musical traditions, blues, ragtime and brass bands. Built on improvisation, swing rhythm and the blues, it grew into many styles and became a global music.

This path begins with ways into listening, follows jazz's roots and the swing era, studies bebop and the modern jazz revolution, explores modal, free and fusion jazz, learns how improvisation works, and ends with jazz around the world and today.

**Trivia**
- The first jazz recording, by the Original Dixieland Jass Band, was made in 1917.
- Miles Davis's *Kind of Blue* (1959) is often called the best-selling jazz album of all time.
- Ella Fitzgerald recorded over 200 albums and won over a dozen Grammy Awards.
- Ethio-jazz, created by Mulatu Astatke in the 1960s–70s, blends Ethiopian scales with jazz.

### Timeline
- **1890s–1910s · New Orleans** blends blues, ragtime and brass bands.
- **1917 · The first jazz recording** is made.
- **1925–28 · Louis Armstrong's Hot Five and Hot Seven** record.
- **1930s · The swing era** big bands of Ellington, Basie and Goodman.
- **1940s · Bebop** Charlie Parker and Dizzy Gillespie.
- **1959 · *Kind of Blue* and *The Shape of Jazz to Come*** modal and free jazz.
- **1964 · John Coltrane's *A Love Supreme*** is recorded.
- **1970 · Miles Davis's *Bitches Brew*** launches fusion.
- **1970s · Ethio-jazz and South African jazz** flourish.
- **2015 · Kamasi Washington's *The Epic*** brings jazz to new audiences.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know jazz the way a strong first year of a university jazz studies course expects. You will be able to:

- **Listen to jazz:** form, swing, improvisation and interaction.
- **Trace jazz history:** from New Orleans to today's global scenes.
- **Recognise styles:** swing, bebop, cool, hard bop, modal, free, fusion.
- **Explain improvisation:** blues, chord changes, scales and the role of tradition.
- **Discuss jazz in society:** race, civil rights, women and globalisation.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Jazz musicians and scholars from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.
