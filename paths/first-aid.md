# First Aid and Emergency Care

How to help in the first minutes of an emergency: staying safe and calling for help, CPR and defibrillators, choking and severe bleeding, strokes, heart attacks and allergic reactions, burns, injuries and the environment, crises of mind and poisoning, and the systems that save lives. About 60 to 80 hours over two to three months, plus a hands-on course.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free international guidelines, Red Cross and Red Crescent materials and short videos cover the knowledge. Reading is not enough: practise on a manikin in a certified hands-on course, which may cost money. Always call your local emergency number first; guidance can differ slightly between countries, so follow your local first aid organisation.

# About first aid

First aid is the help given to an ill or injured person until professional care arrives. Simple actions by ordinary people, such as starting CPR, stopping severe bleeding or recognising a stroke, save many lives every day.

This path starts with staying safe and the basics, teaches CPR, defibrillators, choking and bleeding, then medical emergencies, injuries and environmental dangers, mental health crises, poisoning and children, and ends with emergency systems, disasters and getting certified.

**Trivia**
- Bystander CPR can double or even triple a person's chance of surviving a cardiac arrest.
- Chest compressions should be about 100 to 120 per minute, roughly the beat of the song “Stayin' Alive”.
- The Red Cross began after Henry Dunant saw thousands of wounded soldiers left without care at the Battle of Solferino in 1859.
- For burns, the advice is to cool the burn with cool running water for 20 minutes.

### Timeline
- **1859 · Henry Dunant** witnesses the Battle of Solferino.
- **1863 · The Red Cross** is founded in Geneva.
- **1877 · St John Ambulance** begins teaching first aid to the public.
- **1960 · Kouwenhoven, Jude and Knickerbocker** describe closed-chest CPR.
- **1966 · Frank Pantridge** starts a mobile coronary care unit in Belfast.
- **1970s · Portable defibrillators** begin to spread beyond hospitals.
- **2005 · Resuscitation guidelines** stress more and better chest compressions.
- **2015 · The Stop the Bleed campaign** teaches the public to control severe bleeding.
- **2020 · The IFRC** publishes international first aid guidelines.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path, with a hands-on course, you will be able to act as a confident first aider. You will be able to:

- **Act safely:** check for danger, call for help and use emergency numbers well.
- **Save life:** CPR, defibrillators, choking, severe bleeding and the recovery position.
- **Recognise emergencies:** stroke, heart attack, anaphylaxis, asthma, seizures and diabetes.
- **Treat injuries:** burns, fractures, head injuries, heat and cold, and drowning.
- **Support people:** in mental health crises, poisoning and with children, and understand emergency systems.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Emergency physicians, paramedics and first aid trainers from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages and a certified hands-on course makes you a confident first aider. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: First Aid and Emergency Care** certificate, you also complete:

1. **The grand quiz.** 40 game-style questions across all six stages. Pass mark 80%, with unlimited retakes.
2. **Hands-on course.** Complete a certified first aid course with practical assessment.
3. **Scenario plans.** Write step-by-step responses to five realistic emergency scenarios.
4. **Capstone report.** 2,500 words on one of the Stage 6 questions. Strong reports are read by an expert reviewer.
5. **Explain it.** Teach three people hands-only CPR and the FAST stroke signs.

The certificate needs every stage passed. It is not a substitute for a certified first aid qualification.
