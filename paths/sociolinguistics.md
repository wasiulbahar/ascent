# Sociolinguistics

How language lives in society: accents, dialects and why people judge them, language and identity, gender and class, multilingualism and code-switching, endangered languages, and language policy, power and the internet. About 120 to 150 hours over five to six months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free courses, atlases, podcasts and language databases cover the core. No background is needed; the Linguistics path helps. The path draws on languages and communities from every continent and treats all varieties of language as equally systematic.

# About sociolinguistics

Sociolinguistics studies how language varies and changes with who is speaking, to whom, where and why. It shows that every dialect has rules and that language is bound up with identity, power and belonging.

This path begins with accents, dialects and language attitudes, studies how sociolinguists measure variation, explores identity, gender, class and politeness, covers multilingualism, code-switching, pidgins and creoles, examines endangered languages and revitalisation, and ends with language policy, the internet and research.

**Trivia**
- About 7,000 languages are spoken today, and around 40% of them are endangered.
- In William Labov's 1960s study, New York department store staff pronounced the “r” in “fourth floor” more often in more upmarket stores.
- More than half of the world's people are thought to use two or more languages in daily life.
- Hebrew was revived as an everyday spoken language in the late 19th and 20th centuries after centuries of mainly religious use.

### Timeline
- **1786 · William Jones** suggests Sanskrit, Greek and Latin share a common source.
- **1959 · Charles Ferguson** describes diglossia.
- **1963 · William Labov** studies sound change on Martha's Vineyard.
- **1966 · Labov** publishes his New York City study.
- **1972 · Dell Hymes** introduces communicative competence.
- **1975 · Robin Lakoff** publishes *Language and Woman's Place*.
- **1996 · The Oakland school board** sparks debate over African American English.
- **2003 · UNESCO** sets criteria for language endangerment.
- **2019 · The UN** marks the International Year of Indigenous Languages.
- **2022 · The UN** begins the International Decade of Indigenous Languages.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will understand sociolinguistics the way a strong university course expects. You will be able to:

- **Analyse variation:** accents, dialects, style and how language changes.
- **Explain language attitudes:** prejudice, prestige and standard language ideology.
- **Explain language and identity:** gender, class, ethnicity and politeness.
- **Explain multilingualism:** code-switching, diglossia, pidgins and creoles.
- **Discuss policy and survival:** endangered languages, revitalisation, education and the internet.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Linguists and language educators from more than one region will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
