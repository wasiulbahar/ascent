# Public health

How whole populations stay healthy: measuring health and disease, the social causes of ill health, infectious disease and vaccines, health systems and policy, and new threats from pandemics to drug-resistant microbes and climate change. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Nearly all the key reports and data are free from the WHO, the UN and research groups. The path presents debates fairly. **It is not medical advice**: for your own health, see a qualified professional. (The Medicine and health path covers the individual patient; this one covers populations.)

# About public health

Public health is the science and art of preventing disease, prolonging life and promoting health through the organised efforts of society. Where medicine treats individual patients, public health looks at whole populations: clean water and sanitation, vaccination, tobacco control, safe food, healthy cities, and the social conditions, like income and education, that shape who gets sick.

This path begins with stories of epidemics and inequality, teaches how health is measured, studies the social and commercial causes of ill health, examines infectious disease and vaccines, looks at health systems and policy, and ends with new frontiers: One Health, drug resistance and climate change.

**Trivia**
- Smallpox, which killed hundreds of millions in the twentieth century, was declared eradicated in 1980 after a global vaccination campaign.
- The WHO Framework Convention on Tobacco Control (2003) was the first global public-health treaty.
- Clean water and sanitation in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries did more to extend city-dwellers' lives than most medical treatments of the time.
- Life expectancy can differ by more than ten years between neighbourhoods of the same city.

### Timeline
- **1842 · Edwin Chadwick's sanitary report** on the conditions of the labouring poor.
- **1854 · John Snow** and the Broad Street pump.
- **1948 · The World Health Organization** is founded.
- **1978 · The Alma-Ata Declaration** “Health for all” through primary health care.
- **1980 · Smallpox eradicated**
- **2003 · The Framework Convention on Tobacco Control**
- **2008 · The WHO Commission on Social Determinants of Health** reports.
- **2015 · The Sustainable Development Goals** include health for all.
- **2020–23 · The COVID-19 pandemic**

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know public health the way a strong first year of a public-health degree expects. You will be able to:

- **Measure population health:** rates, life expectancy, DALYs and the burden of disease.
- **Explain the determinants of health:** social, economic, environmental and commercial.
- **Explain how infectious disease is controlled:** surveillance, vaccines and outbreak response.
- **Analyse health systems and policy:** universal health coverage, taxes, regulation and nudges.
- **Weigh new threats:** pandemics, antimicrobial resistance and climate change.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Public-health scientists and practitioners from more than one continent will confirm them before the path is published. Knowledge only: not medical advice.
