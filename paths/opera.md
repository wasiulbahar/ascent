# Opera

Drama sung: how opera began in Italy, Baroque and Mozart, bel canto and Verdi, Wagner's music dramas, Puccini and verismo, opera in the 20th and 21st centuries, the voice and staging, and music theatre traditions such as Chinese opera, Noh and Kabuki. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Many opera houses stream performances free, librettos and scores are free online, and recordings stream widely. The path includes music theatre from China, Japan and elsewhere, women and composers of colour, and debates about opera's staging and relevance.

# About opera

Opera tells stories through singing, with orchestra, staging, costumes and sometimes dance. Born in Italy around 1600, it spread across Europe and the world, and remains one of the most ambitious art forms.

This path begins with ways into opera, follows its history from Monteverdi through Mozart, Verdi, Wagner and Puccini to today, studies the voice and production, explores music theatre traditions from Asia, and ends with opera's present and future.

**Trivia**
- Jacopo Peri's *Dafne* (1598) is considered the first opera; Monteverdi's *L'Orfeo* (1607) is the earliest still regularly performed.
- Wagner's *Ring* cycle takes about 15 hours across four evenings.
- Peking opera (jingju) and Japanese Kabuki are UNESCO-listed intangible heritage.
- The Metropolitan Opera's *Live in HD* cinema broadcasts, begun in 2006, have reached millions of viewers in dozens of countries.

### Timeline
- **1598 · Peri's *Dafne*** the first opera.
- **1607 · Monteverdi's *L'Orfeo*** premieres in Mantua.
- **1637 · The first public opera house** opens in Venice.
- **1786–91 · Mozart's** great operas.
- **1831–35 · Bellini and Donizetti** lead bel canto.
- **1853 · Verdi's *La traviata*** premieres in Venice.
- **1876 · Wagner's *Ring*** premieres at Bayreuth.
- **1896–1904 · Puccini's *La bohème*, *Tosca* and *Madama Butterfly*** premiere.
- **1935 · Gershwin's *Porgy and Bess*** premieres.
- **1987 · John Adams's *Nixon in China*** brings current events to opera.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know opera the way a strong first year of a university opera course expects. You will be able to:

- **Listen to opera:** recitative, aria, ensemble and orchestra.
- **Trace opera's history:** from Monteverdi to living composers.
- **Explain the voice:** voice types and singing techniques.
- **Discuss staging:** directors, design and modern productions.
- **Compare traditions:** Western opera, Peking opera, Noh, Kabuki and more.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Singers, directors and opera scholars from more than one tradition will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
