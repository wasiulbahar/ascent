# Ageing

How and why we age, and how to live well for longer: the biology of ageing, health in later life from dementia to falls, the psychology and social side of growing older, care systems and pensions, end of life, and building age-friendly societies. About 120 to 150 hours over five to six months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free WHO, UN, OECD and research resources cover the core. No background is needed. The path separates solid evidence from anti-ageing hype, and looks at ageing in rich and poor countries alike. It is for understanding, not medical advice.

# About ageing

The world is ageing fast: people live longer than ever, and by 2030 one in six people will be 60 or older. Ageing raises questions in biology, medicine, psychology, economics, care and how societies treat older people.

This path starts with the big picture and personal stories, explains why bodies age, covers dementia, frailty, exercise and medicines, explores emotional wellbeing, ageism and loneliness, studies care, carers and pensions, and ends with palliative care, age-friendly cities and research.

**Trivia**
- Jeanne Calment of France lived 122 years and 164 days, the longest confirmed human lifespan.
- By 2030, one in six people in the world will be aged 60 or over, according to the WHO.
- Leonard Hayflick found in 1961 that normal human cells divide only about 50 times before stopping.
- The 2024 Lancet Commission estimated nearly half of dementia cases might be prevented or delayed by tackling 14 risk factors.

### Timeline
- **1825 · Benjamin Gompertz** describes how death rates rise with age.
- **1909 · Ignatz Nascher** coins the word “geriatrics”.
- **1952 · Peter Medawar** proposes an evolutionary explanation of ageing.
- **1961 · Leonard Hayflick** finds cells have a division limit.
- **1982 · The UN** holds the first World Assembly on Ageing in Vienna.
- **1997 · Jeanne Calment** dies aged 122.
- **2000 · Japan** introduces long-term care insurance.
- **2007 · The WHO** publishes its guide to age-friendly cities.
- **2021 · The UN Decade of Healthy Ageing** begins.
- **2024 · The Lancet Commission** identifies 14 modifiable dementia risk factors.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will understand ageing the way a strong introductory gerontology course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain the biology of ageing:** cellular hallmarks, telomeres and evolutionary theories.
- **Explain health in later life:** dementia, frailty, falls, exercise and medicines.
- **Understand the social and psychological side:** wellbeing, ageism and loneliness.
- **Analyse care and economics:** long-term care, carers, pensions and policy.
- **Support good later lives:** palliative care, advance planning and age-friendly communities.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Gerontologists, geriatricians and care experts from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
