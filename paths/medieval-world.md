# The Medieval World

A thousand years of world history, roughly 500 to 1500: Byzantium and the Islamic caliphates, Tang and Song China, African empires, Christian Europe, the Mongols, the Black Death, and the travellers and traders who connected them. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Many medieval texts are free in translation, and museums and manuscript libraries put collections online. The path is global: Europe is one region among many, and it challenges the myth of a backward “Dark Ages”.

# About the medieval world

The medieval period, roughly 500 to 1500 CE, is the age between the ancient world and the modern one. It saw great empires and faiths spread, cities, universities and trade networks grow, and connections from West Africa to China via the Silk Roads and the Indian Ocean.

This path begins with lively global histories, studies Byzantium and the Islamic world, travels through China, India and Africa, explores Christian Europe, follows the Mongols and the Black Death, and ends with how historians study and imagine the Middle Ages.

**Trivia**
- The University of al-Qarawiyyin in Fez, Morocco, founded in 859 by Fatima al-Fihri, is often called the oldest continuously operating university.
- Mansa Musa of Mali travelled to Mecca in 1324 with so much gold that he reportedly depressed its value in Cairo.
- Song dynasty China issued the world's first government paper money in the 11th century.
- The Black Death of 1347–51 killed perhaps a third to a half of Europe's population.

### Timeline
- **527–565 · Justinian** rules the Byzantine Empire.
- **610–632 · Muhammad** founds Islam.
- **618–907 · The Tang dynasty** rules China.
- **750 · The Abbasid caliphate** begins; Baghdad becomes a centre of learning.
- **800 · Charlemagne** is crowned emperor.
- **1066 · The Norman conquest** of England.
- **1096–99 · The First Crusade** captures Jerusalem.
- **1206 · Genghis Khan** unites the Mongols.
- **1324 · Mansa Musa** makes his pilgrimage to Mecca.
- **1347–51 · The Black Death** spreads across Eurasia and North Africa.
- **1453 · Constantinople** falls to the Ottomans.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know the medieval world the way a strong first year of a university history course expects. You will be able to:

- **Compare medieval societies:** across Europe, Asia, Africa and the Middle East.
- **Explain religion and power:** Christianity, Islam, Buddhism and the states they shaped.
- **Trace connections:** trade, travel, conquest and disease across continents.
- **Read medieval sources:** chronicles, travel accounts and objects.
- **Challenge myths:** about the “Dark Ages” and medieval people.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Medieval historians from more than one region will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
