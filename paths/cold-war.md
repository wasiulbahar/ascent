# The Cold War

The global rivalry between the United States and the Soviet Union from 1945 to 1991: its origins, the nuclear arms race, hot wars in Asia, Africa and Latin America, spies and propaganda, culture and everyday life, and how it ended. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. The Wilson Center Digital Archive and national archives publish many declassified documents free. The path tells the Cold War from Washington and Moscow, but also from Beijing, Havana, Hanoi, Luanda, Jakarta and Delhi, and presents historians' debates fairly.

# About the Cold War

The Cold War was a rivalry between the United States and the Soviet Union, and their allies, from the end of the Second World War to the Soviet collapse in 1991. The superpowers never fought each other directly, but they raced to build nuclear weapons and backed wars that killed millions across the world.

This path begins with gripping accounts of the era, explains how the rivalry began, studies the nuclear arms race and its near misses, follows the Cold War into Asia, Africa and Latin America, explores spies, propaganda and culture, and ends with how it ended and what it left behind.

**Trivia**
- During the 1962 Cuban Missile Crisis, a Soviet submarine officer, Vasili Arkhipov, reportedly refused to agree to launch a nuclear torpedo.
- At their peak in 1986, the world's nuclear arsenals held about 70,000 warheads.
- The Berlin Wall stood from 1961 to 1989.
- The Non-Aligned Movement, founded in 1961, gathered countries that refused to join either bloc.

### Timeline
- **1945 · Yalta and Potsdam** divide postwar Europe.
- **1947 · The Truman Doctrine and Marshall Plan** launch containment.
- **1949 · NATO is founded;** the Soviet Union tests an atomic bomb; the People's Republic of China is proclaimed.
- **1950–53 · The Korean War** is fought; it ends in an armistice.
- **1955 · The Bandung Conference** of Asian and African states.
- **1961 · The Berlin Wall** is built.
- **1962 · The Cuban Missile Crisis** brings the world close to nuclear war.
- **1965–75 · The United States** fights a major war in Vietnam.
- **1989 · The Berlin Wall falls;** revolutions sweep Eastern Europe.
- **1991 · The Soviet Union** dissolves.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know the Cold War the way a strong first year of a university history course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain its origins:** ideology, security and the postwar settlement.
- **Explain the nuclear age:** deterrence, the arms race and the crises that nearly caused war.
- **Follow the global Cold War:** in Asia, Africa, Latin America and the Middle East.
- **Explain culture and intelligence:** propaganda, spies and everyday life on both sides.
- **Weigh how it ended:** and its legacies today.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Historians of the Cold War from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.
