# Criminology

Why crime happens and how societies respond: the main theories, punishment and prisons, policing and alternatives, white-collar and organised crime, victims, and the evidence on what reduces harm. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Global data from the UN and research reviews are free. The path presents several schools of thought, from control theory to critical criminology, and compares countries. **It is not legal advice.**

# About criminology

Criminology studies crime and the responses to it: what counts as a crime, why some people offend and most stop, how victims are affected, how police, courts and prisons work, and which policies actually reduce harm. It draws on sociology, psychology, law, economics and statistics.

This path begins with books that show crime and justice up close, teaches the main theories, studies punishment and prisons, then policing and alternatives such as restorative justice, widens to white-collar crime, organised crime and victims, and ends with today's frontier: big data, cybercrime and how people leave crime behind.

**Trivia**
- More than 11 million people are held in prisons worldwide, according to the World Prison Brief.
- Crime fell sharply in the United States, Canada and much of Europe from the early 1990s, and criminologists still debate why.
- Edwin Sutherland coined the term “white-collar crime” in a 1939 speech.
- Crime victim surveys show that many crimes are never reported to the police.

### Timeline
- **1764 · Beccaria's *On Crimes and Punishments***
- **1791 · Bentham's Panopticon** design for a prison.
- **1876 · Lombroso's *Criminal Man*** A biological theory now discredited.
- **1938 · Merton's strain theory**
- **1939 · Sutherland** coins “white-collar crime”.
- **1963 · Becker's *Outsiders*** and labelling theory.
- **1975 · Foucault's *Discipline and Punish***
- **1990 · Zehr's *Changing Lenses*** and restorative justice.
- **1990s · The great crime decline** begins in many countries.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know criminology the way a strong first year of a university criminology degree expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain the main theories:** strain, control, labelling, learning and critical criminology.
- **Read crime data critically:** recorded crime, victim surveys and their limits.
- **Analyse punishment and policing:** purposes, evidence and alternatives.
- **Compare countries:** prison rates, homicide and justice systems around the world.
- **Weigh the debates:** race and justice, big-data policing, and what reduces harm.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Criminologists from more than one continent will confirm them before the path is published. Knowledge only: not legal advice.
