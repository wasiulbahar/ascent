# Thermodynamics

The science of energy, heat and disorder: the four laws, engines and the limits of efficiency, entropy and the second law, statistical mechanics from molecules up, engineering cycles, refrigerators and heat pumps, and frontiers from information to black holes. About 200 to 250 hours over eight to ten months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free textbooks, simulations, historical sources and lectures cover the core. You need school physics and algebra; calculus is needed from Stage 2. The path serves physicists, chemists and engineers alike.

# About thermodynamics

Thermodynamics studies energy, heat and work, and why some processes happen on their own while others never do. Its laws limit every engine, power station, refrigerator, battery and living cell.

This path begins with gentle introductions to the laws and entropy, studies energy and heat engines, explores entropy and free energy, derives thermodynamics from molecules with statistical mechanics, applies it to power cycles, heat pumps and energy, and ends with information, black holes and the arrow of time.

**Trivia**
- Absolute zero, −273.15 °C, can be approached but never reached, according to the third law.
- Ludwig Boltzmann's formula for entropy, S = k log W, is carved on his tombstone in Vienna.
- Sadi Carnot founded the theory of heat engines in a single short book in 1824 and died of cholera at 36.
- The “zeroth law” was named after the first three, because physicists realised it was more basic than them.

### Timeline
- **1712 · Thomas Newcomen** builds a practical steam engine.
- **1769 · James Watt** patents a separate condenser, greatly improving efficiency.
- **1824 · Sadi Carnot** publishes *Reflections on the Motive Power of Fire*.
- **1845 · James Joule** measures the mechanical equivalent of heat.
- **1850 · Rudolf Clausius** states the second law.
- **1865 · Clausius** names entropy.
- **1877 · Ludwig Boltzmann** links entropy to counting molecular arrangements.
- **1876–1878 · Josiah Willard Gibbs** develops chemical thermodynamics.
- **1912 · Walther Nernst** completes the third law.
- **1974 · Stephen Hawking** predicts that black holes radiate heat.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know thermodynamics the way a strong university course expects. You will be able to:

- **State and apply the laws:** energy conservation, entropy and absolute zero.
- **Analyse engines:** heat engines, the Carnot limit, and power and refrigeration cycles.
- **Use entropy and free energy:** to predict which processes and reactions happen.
- **Explain statistical mechanics:** temperature and entropy from molecules and probability.
- **Apply it widely:** to energy systems, living things, information and the arrow of time.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Physicists, chemists and engineering educators from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.
