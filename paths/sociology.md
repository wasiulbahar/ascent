# Sociology

From seeing your own life as part of society to the theories, methods and arguments of a social science. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Several founding classics are free online. The path takes in social theory from beyond Europe and North America, and it pairs classic thinkers with their critics.

# About sociology

Sociology studies how societies work: how groups, institutions, inequalities and everyday interactions shape people's lives, and how people in turn change society. Its central skill, the "sociological imagination", is seeing private troubles, such as losing a job or a home, as part of public patterns.

This path begins with books that change how you see everyday life, teaches the basics and the founding classics, covers the great classical theorists, explores class, culture, work and globalisation, reaches the methods taught at university, and ends with the arguments about what sociology should study and how.

**Trivia**
- W. E. B. Du Bois, the first African American to earn a doctorate at Harvard (in 1895), wrote in 1903 that "the problem of the twentieth century is the problem of the color-line".
- Émile Durkheim showed in 1897 that suicide rates, which seem the most private of acts, follow social patterns.
- The word "sociology" was coined by the French thinker Auguste Comte in the 1830s.
- Pierre Bourdieu's surveys of French tastes showed that preferences in music, food and art map closely onto social class.

### Timeline
- **1838 · Comte** The word "sociology" is coined.
- **1848 · Marx and Engels** *The Communist Manifesto*.
- **1897 · Durkheim** *Suicide* shows the social patterns in the most private act.
- **1903 · Du Bois** *The Souls of Black Folk*.
- **1905 · Weber** *The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism*.
- **1959 · Mills** *The Sociological Imagination*.
- **1979 · Bourdieu** *Distinction* and cultural capital.
- **2007 · Connell** *Southern Theory* asks why social theory comes mostly from the global North.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know sociology the way a strong first and second year of a university sociology degree expects. You will be able to:

- **Use the sociological imagination:** connect individual lives to social structures.
- **Explain the classical theorists:** Marx, Durkheim, Weber, Du Bois and Simmel.
- **Analyse inequality** of class, race, gender and their intersections.
- **Judge research methods:** surveys, interviews, ethnography and statistics.
- **Weigh the debates:** power, globalisation, and what "the social" even is.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Sociologists from more than one region will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages makes you a capable student of sociology. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Sociology** certificate, you also complete:
