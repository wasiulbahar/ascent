# Microeconomics

How people and firms make choices and how markets work: supply and demand, consumers and behaviour, firms and costs, competition and strategy, market failures and information, and market design in the real world. About 180 to 220 hours over seven to nine months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. A free textbook, free courses, interactive graphs and Nobel materials cover the core. You need school algebra; some calculus helps from Stage 3. The path presents evidence from many countries and shows where simple models work and where they fail.

# About microeconomics

Microeconomics studies how individuals, households and firms make decisions with limited resources, and how their choices interact in markets to set prices, wages and what gets produced.

This path starts with friendly introductions and interactive graphs, teaches supply, demand and price policy, covers consumers, behavioural economics and firms, explores competition and game theory, studies market failures and information problems, and ends with market design and research.

**Trivia**
- Alfred Marshall compared supply and demand to the two blades of a pair of scissors: both are needed to cut.
- George Akerlof's “Market for Lemons” paper, which helped win him a Nobel Prize, was rejected by several journals before publication.
- Market design by Alvin Roth and colleagues helps match kidney donors and patients, saving lives.
- Card and Krueger's study of fast-food restaurants in New Jersey and Pennsylvania changed debates about minimum wages.

### Timeline
- **1776 · Adam Smith** describes markets guided by an “invisible hand”.
- **1838 · Antoine Augustin Cournot** models competition between firms.
- **1871–1874 · Jevons, Menger and Walras** launch the marginal revolution.
- **1890 · Alfred Marshall** publishes *Principles of Economics*.
- **1920 · Arthur Pigou** analyses externalities and corrective taxes.
- **1937 · Ronald Coase** asks why firms exist.
- **1950 · John Nash** defines the Nash equilibrium.
- **1970 · George Akerlof** publishes “The Market for Lemons”.
- **1994 · Card and Krueger** publish their minimum wage study.
- **2012 · Roth and Shapley** win the Nobel Prize for market design.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will understand microeconomics the way a strong intermediate university course expects. You will be able to:

- **Analyse markets:** supply, demand, elasticity and the effects of taxes and price controls.
- **Model choices:** consumers, firms and the predictable mistakes behavioural economics finds.
- **Analyse competition:** monopoly, oligopoly, game theory and platforms.
- **Explain market failures:** externalities, public goods and asymmetric information.
- **Apply it:** to market design, health, policy and field evidence.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Economists and economics educators from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages gives you a strong command of microeconomics. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Microeconomics** certificate, you also complete:

1. **The grand quiz.** 40 game-style questions across all six stages. Pass mark 80%, with unlimited retakes.
2. **Problem set.** Solve unseen problems on markets, firms and games.
3. **Policy analysis.** Analyse a real policy, such as a tax, price control or regulation, with evidence.
4. **Capstone essay.** 2,500 words on one of the Stage 6 questions. Strong essays are read by an expert reviewer.
5. **Explain it.** Answer three unseen questions as if to a friend. For example: “Why are concert tickets so hard to get at face value?”
