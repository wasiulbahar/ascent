# Chemistry

From the stories of the elements to the reactions, structures and debates of modern chemistry. About 300 to 360 hours over twelve to fifteen months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Excellent free material exists, including a complete university textbook and whole courses. From Stage 2 some quizzes include short problems; you can type the number alone.

# About chemistry

Chemistry is the science of matter and how it changes: what substances are made of, how atoms join into molecules, and how those molecules react. It sits between physics and biology, and it makes almost everything around you, from medicines and fertilisers to plastics, batteries and the colours on a screen.

This path starts with writers who fell in love with the elements, builds the basics with free courses, covers the three classic branches (organic, inorganic and physical chemistry), shows how chemistry changed history, reaches the university core, and ends with the arguments about what chemistry is.

**Trivia**
- Dmitri Mendeleev's 1869 periodic table left gaps for elements nobody had found yet, and predicted their properties. Gallium, scandium and germanium soon filled them.
- In 1828 Friedrich Wöhler made urea, a substance from living things, out of non-living chemicals, undermining the idea that life's chemistry was special.
- Marie Curie is the only person to have won Nobel prizes in two different sciences: physics in 1903 and chemistry in 1911.
- Roughly half of the world's food depends on fertiliser made by the Haber–Bosch process, invented in Germany before the First World War.

### Timeline
- **c. 800 · Alchemy** Writings attributed to Jabir ibn Hayyan describe distillation and other laboratory methods.
- **1789 · Lavoisier** A modern list of chemical elements, and the law that mass is conserved in reactions.
- **1803–08 · Dalton** Atomic theory: elements are made of atoms with characteristic weights.
- **1869 · Mendeleev** The periodic table.
- **1898 · The Curies** The discovery of polonium and radium.
- **1909 · Haber** Ammonia made from the nitrogen in the air.
- **1939 · Pauling** *The Nature of the Chemical Bond* explains bonding with quantum mechanics.
- **1985 · Fullerenes** A new form of carbon, the football-shaped C₆₀ molecule, is discovered.

# Before you start

You need only school arithmetic to begin. The path adds the rest:

- **Stages 1 and 2:** no prerequisites; Stage 2 teaches atoms, moles and equations from the start.
- **Stage 3:** basic algebra and logarithms (for pH). Khan Academy fills gaps.
- **Stage 5:** some calculus for physical and quantum chemistry. The Mathematics path covers it.

Already know some chemistry? Take the **placement test** on this path's page to skip the stages you have mastered.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know chemistry the way a strong first and second year of a university chemistry degree expects. You will be able to:

- **Read the periodic table** and explain the patterns in it.
- **Balance equations and do the standard calculations:** moles, concentrations, pH and energy changes.
- **Explain bonding and structure,** and predict how simple organic molecules react.
- **Describe how chemistry changed history,** from fertilisers and dyes to plastics and medicines.
- **Weigh the debates:** whether chemistry reduces to physics, and how to make chemistry greener.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Chemists from more than one field will confirm them before the path is published.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages makes you a capable student of chemistry. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Chemistry** certificate, you also complete:

1. **The grand quiz.** 40 game-style questions across all six stages, some of them calculations. Pass mark 80%, with unlimited retakes.
2. **Problem set.** Ten unseen problems on moles, equilibrium, pH, energy and structure, with full working.
3. **Mechanism test.** Explain three organic reactions with curly arrows.
4. **Capstone essay.** 2,500 words on one of the Stage 6 questions. Strong essays are read by an expert reviewer.
5. **Explain it.** Answer three unseen questions as if to a curious friend. For example: "Why does ice float?"

The certificate needs every stage passed. Stages you continued past with a warning must be completed first.

# Bridges to other subjects

- **Physics:** quantum mechanics and thermodynamics, the physics under chemistry.
- **Biology:** biochemistry, the chemistry of life.
- **History:** fertiliser, dyes, plastics and chemical weapons in the modern world.
- **Philosophy:** whether chemistry reduces to physics.
