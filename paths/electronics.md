# Electronics

How electronic circuits work and how to build them: voltage, current and resistance, components and circuit analysis, semiconductors and transistors, analogue and digital electronics, microcontrollers and embedded systems, and the global chip industry. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free textbooks, circuit simulators and university courses cover the core, and you can learn without buying parts. When you build real circuits, use low voltages only: **never work on mains electricity**.

# About electronics

Electronics is the science and engineering of circuits that control electricity to process signals and information: from radios and amplifiers to computers, phones and sensors. It rests on physics, maths and careful practical skill.

This path begins with how electricity works and simple circuits, teaches circuit analysis, explains semiconductors and transistors, studies analogue electronics, follows digital logic to microcontrollers, and ends with embedded systems and the chip industry.

**Trivia**
- The first transistor was built at Bell Labs in 1947 by John Bardeen, Walter Brattain and William Shockley.
- A modern smartphone chip can contain over 15 billion transistors.
- Moore's law, from 1965, predicted the number of transistors on a chip would double roughly every two years.
- Taiwan's TSMC makes most of the world's most advanced chips.

### Timeline
- **1827 · Georg Ohm** publishes his law of current and resistance.
- **1904 · John Ambrose Fleming** invents the vacuum tube diode.
- **1947 · The transistor** is invented at Bell Labs.
- **1958–59 · Jack Kilby and Robert Noyce** invent the integrated circuit.
- **1965 · Gordon Moore** makes his famous prediction.
- **1971 · The Intel 4004** the first commercial microprocessor.
- **1987 · TSMC** is founded in Taiwan.
- **2005 · Arduino** makes microcontrollers easy for beginners.
- **2012 · Raspberry Pi** launches a cheap computer for learning.
- **2020s · Chips** become a focus of geopolitics.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know electronics the way a strong first year of a university electronics course expects. You will be able to:

- **Analyse circuits:** Ohm's law, Kirchhoff's laws, series and parallel.
- **Explain components:** resistors, capacitors, inductors, diodes and transistors.
- **Design simple analogue circuits:** dividers, filters and amplifiers.
- **Build digital logic:** gates, binary, flip-flops and microcontrollers.
- **Discuss the chip industry:** manufacturing, supply chains, e-waste and geopolitics.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Electronics engineers and educators from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published. Low-voltage practice only.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
