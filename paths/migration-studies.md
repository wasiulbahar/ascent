# Migration Studies

Why people move and what happens when they do: the long history of human migration, theories of why people move, labour migration and remittances, refugees and asylum, borders and policy, integration and diaspora, and climate and future migration. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. The UN, IOM, UNHCR and research centres publish free data and reports. The path covers migration within and from the Global South, which is most migration, centres migrants' own voices, and presents debates about borders and immigration policy fairly.

# About migration studies

Migration studies examines why people move within and between countries, how they are received, and how migration changes both origin and destination societies. It draws on economics, sociology, geography, law, history and politics.

This path begins with migrants' stories and the scale of migration, studies its history, examines theories and economics, explores refugees and asylum, follows borders and policy, and ends with integration, diaspora and climate-related migration.

**Trivia**
- About 281 million people, around 3.6% of the world's population, were international migrants in 2020.
- Remittances to low- and middle-income countries reached about 656 billion US dollars in 2023, more than foreign aid in many countries.
- Over 117 million people were forcibly displaced worldwide by the end of 2023.
- Most refugees are hosted in low- and middle-income countries, often neighbouring their own.

### Timeline
- **c. 60,000 years ago · Modern humans** spread out of Africa.
- **1500s–1860s · The transatlantic slave trade** forcibly moves over 12 million Africans.
- **1834–1920 · Indentured labour** takes Indian and Chinese workers across empires.
- **1880s–1920s · Mass migration** from Europe to the Americas.
- **1947 · Partition** displaces around 15 million people.
- **1951 · The Refugee Convention** is adopted.
- **1960s–70s · Labour migration** to Europe and the Gulf grows.
- **2015 · Over a million people** seek asylum in Europe.
- **2018 · The Global Compact for Migration** is adopted.
- **2022 · War in Ukraine** displaces millions.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know migration studies the way a strong first year of a university course expects. You will be able to:

- **Describe migration's scale and patterns:** using UN and IOM data.
- **Explain why people move:** push–pull, networks, aspirations and structures.
- **Explain refugee and asylum law:** and how protection works in practice.
- **Analyse policy:** borders, labour migration, detention and integration.
- **Discuss migration's effects:** on migrants, origin and destination societies.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Migration scholars and practitioners from more than one region, and people with lived experience of migration, will review the path before it is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
