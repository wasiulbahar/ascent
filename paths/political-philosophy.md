# Political Philosophy

The big questions of how we should live together: authority and the state, liberty and rights, justice and equality, democracy and its critics, power, race, gender and empire, and global justice, read through thinkers from many traditions. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Classic texts are free online, as are the Stanford Encyclopedia and many lectures. The path presents liberal, conservative, socialist, libertarian, feminist, anti-colonial and non-Western views fairly, so you can argue with all of them.

# About political philosophy

Political philosophy asks what makes political power legitimate, what we owe each other, what freedom and equality mean, and how institutions should be designed. It connects ethics, law, economics and history.

This path begins with lively introductions, studies classical and non-Western foundations, the social contract, liberty and rights, justice and equality, democracy and its critics, and ends with power, race, gender, empire and global justice.

**Trivia**
- Plato's *Republic*, written around 375 BCE, imagines a city ruled by philosopher-kings.
- Thomas Hobbes wrote that life without government would be “solitary, poor, nasty, brutish, and short”.
- John Rawls's *A Theory of Justice* (1971) asked what principles people would choose behind a “veil of ignorance” about their own place in society.
- Mary Wollstonecraft's *A Vindication of the Rights of Woman* (1792) answered political thinkers who excluded women.

### Timeline
- **c. 500 BCE · Confucius** teaches government by virtue.
- **c. 375 BCE · Plato's *Republic*** imagines philosopher-kings.
- **c. 350 BCE · Aristotle's *Politics*** compares constitutions.
- **1377 · Ibn Khaldun's *Muqaddimah*** analyses states and group solidarity.
- **1513 · Machiavelli's *The Prince*** advises rulers on power.
- **1651 · Hobbes's *Leviathan*** argues for an absolute sovereign.
- **1689 · Locke's *Two Treatises of Government*** defends natural rights.
- **1762 · Rousseau's *The Social Contract*** appeals to the general will.
- **1859 · Mill's *On Liberty*** sets out the harm principle.
- **1971 · Rawls's *A Theory of Justice*** revives political philosophy.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know political philosophy the way a strong first year of a university course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain the major traditions:** liberal, conservative, socialist, libertarian, feminist, anti-colonial and more.
- **Analyse core concepts:** authority, liberty, rights, equality, justice and democracy.
- **Read classic texts:** from Plato and Confucius to Rawls and Fanon.
- **Apply theory to cases:** taxation, free speech, borders, climate and inequality.
- **Write a clear argument:** stating your view and answering objections.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Political philosophers from more than one tradition and country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
