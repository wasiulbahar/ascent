# Marketing

How organisations understand customers and win their attention: the marketing mix, consumer behaviour, brands, research and data, digital and social media marketing, and the ethics of persuasion, privacy and advertising to children. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free textbooks, platform courses and regulators' guidance cover the core. The path uses examples from many countries and from small businesses as well as global brands, and presents critiques of consumer culture and advertising fairly. **It is knowledge, not a professional qualification.**

# About marketing

Marketing is how organisations find out what people need and want, create offers, and communicate and deliver them. It combines psychology, economics, data and creativity, and is used by businesses, charities and governments alike.

This path begins with lively books on why people buy, teaches the foundations of the marketing mix and strategy, explores consumer behaviour and research, studies brands and advertising, covers digital and social media marketing, and ends with ethics, regulation and the future.

**Trivia**
- The “4 Ps” of the marketing mix (product, price, place, promotion) were popularised by E. Jerome McCarthy in 1960.
- Global advertising spending passed about 1 trillion US dollars a year in the 2020s, more than half of it digital.
- Many countries restrict advertising to children; Sweden and Norway ban TV advertising aimed at under-12s.
- Sales of “ugly” fruit and vegetables rose when supermarkets in France rebranded them with humour in 2014.

### Timeline
- **1450s · Printing** makes mass advertising possible.
- **1880s–90s · Branded packaged goods** such as Coca-Cola and Sunlight soap spread.
- **1920s · Edward Bernays** applies psychology to public relations.
- **1960 · McCarthy's 4 Ps** frame the marketing mix.
- **1960 · Theodore Levitt's “Marketing Myopia”** urges a focus on customer needs.
- **1970s · Social marketing** applies marketing to public health.
- **1994 · The first banner ads** appear online.
- **2000 · Google AdWords** launches search advertising.
- **2018 · The EU's GDPR** tightens rules on personal data.
- **2020s · Influencer and AI-driven marketing** grow rapidly.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know marketing the way a strong first year of a university marketing course expects. You will be able to:

- **Use the marketing mix:** product, price, place and promotion.
- **Explain consumer behaviour:** decisions, habits and influence.
- **Plan research:** surveys, interviews, experiments and data.
- **Explain brands and digital marketing:** positioning, content, search and social.
- **Weigh ethics and regulation:** privacy, manipulation, children and sustainability claims.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Marketing academics and practitioners from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published. Knowledge only: not a professional qualification.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
