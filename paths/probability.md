# Probability

The mathematics of chance: intuition and famous puzzles, counting and conditional probability, random variables and distributions, the laws of large numbers, risk in real decisions, Markov chains and rigorous foundations, and probability in information, forecasting and machine learning. About 200 to 250 hours over eight to ten months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free Harvard and MIT courses and free textbooks cover the core. You need school algebra; calculus is needed from Stage 3. Simulating problems in code is one of the best ways to check your intuition.

# About probability

Probability measures uncertainty. It began with gamblers' questions and now underlies statistics, physics, finance, insurance, medicine, genetics and machine learning.

This path starts with intuition and paradoxes, teaches counting, conditional probability and Bayes' rule, develops random variables, distributions and limit theorems, applies them to real risks and simulation, covers Markov chains and rigorous foundations, and ends with information theory, machine learning and forecasting.

**Trivia**
- In a room of just 23 people, the chance that two share a birthday is just over 50%.
- After Marilyn vos Savant explained the Monty Hall problem in 1990, thousands of readers, many with PhDs, wrote to say she was wrong. She was right.
- Probability theory grew out of letters between Blaise Pascal and Pierre de Fermat in 1654 about dividing the stakes of an unfinished game.
- Thomas Bayes's famous essay was published only after his death, by his friend Richard Price, in 1763.

### Timeline
- **c. 1564 · Gerolamo Cardano** writes the first book on games of chance (published 1663).
- **1654 · Pascal and Fermat** solve the problem of points in letters.
- **1713 · Jacob Bernoulli** proves the first law of large numbers in *Ars Conjectandi*.
- **1733 · Abraham de Moivre** approximates the binomial with the normal curve.
- **1763 · Thomas Bayes's essay** is published by Richard Price.
- **1812 · Pierre-Simon Laplace** publishes his analytical theory of probability.
- **1906 · Andrey Markov** introduces Markov chains.
- **1933 · Andrey Kolmogorov** gives probability its modern axioms.
- **1946–1949 · Ulam, von Neumann and Metropolis** develop the Monte Carlo method.
- **1948 · Claude Shannon** founds information theory.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know probability the way a strong first university course, and the start of a second, expects. You will be able to:

- **Reason about chance:** counting, conditional probability, independence and Bayes' rule.
- **Use random variables:** expectation, variance and the common distributions.
- **Explain limit theorems:** the law of large numbers and the central limit theorem.
- **Apply probability:** to risk, medicine, law and simulation, avoiding classic fallacies.
- **Go further:** Markov chains, measure-theoretic foundations, information and forecasting.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Probabilists, statisticians and teachers from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.
