# Logic and critical thinking

How to reason well and spot bad reasoning: arguments and fallacies, formal logic, probability and evidence, paradoxes and the limits of logic, logic traditions beyond Europe, and thinking clearly online. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free textbooks, courses and videos cover most of the path. You need no maths beyond school; the formal stage builds up step by step. Logic traditions from India and China are included alongside Greek and modern logic.

# About logic and critical thinking

Logic studies what makes reasoning good: when conclusions really follow from premises. Critical thinking applies it to everyday life: weighing evidence, spotting fallacies and manipulation, and changing your mind when you should. Formal logic turns arguments into symbols so they can be checked precisely, and underlies mathematics and computing.

This path begins with lively books on clear thinking, teaches arguments and fallacies, builds formal logic, studies probability and evidence, explores paradoxes and the limits of logic, and ends with how people really reason and how to think clearly about information online.

**Trivia**
- Aristotle's syllogisms, such as “All humans are mortal; Socrates is human; so Socrates is mortal”, were the core of logic for two thousand years.
- George Boole's algebra of logic (1854) became the basis of every digital computer.
- Kurt Gödel proved in 1931 that any consistent formal system rich enough for arithmetic contains true statements it cannot prove.
- Indian Nyaya logicians developed a five-step form of argument, including an example, around two thousand years ago.

### Timeline
- **c. 350 BCE · Aristotle's syllogistic logic**
- **c. 300 BCE · The Mohists in China** study argument and naming.
- **c. 2nd century CE · The *Nyaya Sutras*** in India.
- **c. 6th century · Dignaga** develops Buddhist logic.
- **1854 · Boole's *Laws of Thought***
- **1879 · Frege's *Begriffsschrift*** Modern formal logic begins.
- **1910–13 · Russell and Whitehead's *Principia Mathematica***
- **1931 · Gödel's incompleteness theorems**
- **1936 · Turing** on computability.
- **1970s · Kahneman and Tversky** study reasoning errors.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will reason with the skill a strong first-year university logic or critical-thinking course expects. You will be able to:

- **Analyse arguments:** identify premises and conclusions, and test validity and soundness.
- **Spot fallacies and manipulation:** in politics, advertising and online.
- **Use formal logic:** truth tables, propositional and predicate logic.
- **Reason with evidence:** probability, Bayes's rule and calibrated belief.
- **Understand logic's limits:** paradoxes, Gödel, and logic traditions across the world.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Logicians and teachers of critical thinking from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.
