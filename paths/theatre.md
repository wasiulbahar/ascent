# Theatre

Theatre as an art made live: how actors and directors work, the world's great theatre traditions, great plays from Greece to Beckett, the theories that changed the stage, and theatre today. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Many classic plays and treatises are free, and recorded productions are often free or cheap. Above all, see live theatre near you, from school plays to street performance. Traditions from Greece, India, Japan, Africa and beyond are included.

# About theatre

Theatre is the art of live performance in front of an audience: actors, a space, and a story or action shared in the same moment. Theatre studies looks at plays and how they are staged, at acting, directing and design, at the theatre traditions of the world, and at what performance does in society.

This path begins with books that capture what theatre is, teaches how actors and directors work, travels through the great traditions of Greece, India, Japan and Africa, reads great plays, studies the theories that changed modern theatre, and ends with theatre today.

**Trivia**
- The Theatre of Dionysus in Athens, where Greek tragedies were first performed in the fifth century BCE, could seat many thousands of spectators.
- Noh theatre in Japan, developed by Kan'ami and Zeami in the fourteenth century, is still performed today in much the same way.
- Wole Soyinka, a Nigerian playwright, became the first African to win the Nobel Prize in Literature, in 1986.
- Agatha Christie's *The Mousetrap* has been running in London's West End since 1952.

### Timeline
- **5th century BCE · Athenian tragedy** Aeschylus, Sophocles and Euripides.
- **c. 335 BCE · Aristotle's *Poetics***
- **c. 200 BCE–200 CE · The *Natyashastra*** India's great treatise on drama.
- **14th century · Noh** Kan'ami and Zeami in Japan.
- **c. 1600 · Shakespeare's *Hamlet***
- **17th century · Kabuki** develops in Japan.
- **1879 · Ibsen's *A Doll's House***
- **1898 · The Moscow Art Theatre** founded; Stanislavski's work begins.
- **1953 · *Waiting for Godot*** premieres in Paris.
- **1975 · Soyinka's *Death and the King's Horseman***
- **2015 · *Hamilton*** opens on Broadway.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know theatre the way a strong first year of a university drama degree expects. You will be able to:

- **Analyse a performance:** acting, directing, space, design and audience.
- **Explain how actors and directors work:** from Stanislavski to improvisation.
- **Range across traditions:** Greek tragedy, Sanskrit drama, Noh, Yoruba theatre and more.
- **Read great plays:** as texts for performance, not just literature.
- **Use theatre theory:** Aristotle, Brecht, Artaud, Grotowski, Boal and performance studies.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Theatre scholars and practitioners from more than one continent will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
