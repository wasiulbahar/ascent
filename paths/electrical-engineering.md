# Electrical Engineering

How electricity is generated, moved, controlled and used: circuits and electromagnetism, transformers and motors, power electronics, the grid, signals and control, and the energy transition. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free university courses and textbooks cover the core. You need school physics and algebra; calculus helps from Stage 2. The Electronics path covers small circuits and chips; this path focuses on power, machines, signals and control. Never work on mains electricity without training and supervision.

# About electrical engineering

Electrical engineering applies electricity and electromagnetism to generate, transmit and use energy and to process signals and control systems. Electrical engineers design power stations, grids, motors, electric vehicles and the control systems that keep them stable.

This path starts with the history and basics of electricity and safety, builds circuit theory and electromagnetism, explains transformers, motors and power electronics, studies how grids work and fail, teaches signals and control, and ends with renewables, energy access, standards and the profession.

**Trivia**
- Japan's grid runs at two frequencies: 50 Hz in the east and 60 Hz in the west, a legacy of generators bought from Germany and the United States.
- In 1891 three-phase power was sent about 175 km from Lauffen to Frankfurt, showing long-distance AC transmission worked.
- The 2012 blackout in northern India affected more than 600 million people, one of the largest in history.
- The first commercial high-voltage direct current link connected the Swedish island of Gotland to the mainland in 1954.

### Timeline
- **1800 · Alessandro Volta** builds the first battery.
- **1820 · Hans Christian Ørsted** finds that currents create magnetic fields.
- **1831 · Michael Faraday** discovers electromagnetic induction.
- **1865 · James Clerk Maxwell** unifies electricity, magnetism and light.
- **1882 · Thomas Edison** opens a central power station in New York.
- **1888 · Nikola Tesla** patents AC induction motors.
- **1891 · The Lauffen–Frankfurt line** shows long-distance three-phase transmission.
- **1954 · The Gotland link** becomes the first commercial HVDC connection.
- **2012 · Northern India** suffers one of history's largest blackouts.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know electrical engineering the way a strong first two years of a university degree expect. You will be able to:

- **Analyse circuits:** DC and AC, with phasors and three-phase power.
- **Explain electromagnetism:** Maxwell's equations, induction, transformers and machines.
- **Explain power systems:** generation, transmission, distribution, stability and blackouts.
- **Use signals and control:** frequency analysis, feedback and PID control.
- **Discuss the energy transition:** renewables, storage, energy access and engineering safety.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Electrical engineers and engineering educators from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages makes you a capable student of electrical engineering. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Electrical Engineering** certificate, you also complete:

1. **The grand quiz.** 40 game-style questions across all six stages. Pass mark 80%, with unlimited retakes.
2. **Problem set.** Solve unseen AC, three-phase and transformer problems.
3. **Simulation.** Simulate a control system or a small power system and report the results.
4. **Capstone report.** 2,500 words on one of the Stage 6 questions. Strong reports are read by an expert reviewer.
5. **Explain it.** Answer three unseen questions as if to a friend. For example: “Why do power lines use such high voltages?”

The certificate needs every stage passed. It does not qualify you to do electrical work.
