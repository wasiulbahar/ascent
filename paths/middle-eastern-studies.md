# Middle Eastern Studies

The history, cultures and politics of the Middle East and North Africa: ancient empires, the Ottoman and Safavid worlds, colonial borders, oil and nationalism, revolutions and wars, literature and film, and the region today. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Many primary sources, reports and archives are free online. The path covers Arab, Iranian, Turkish, Kurdish, Israeli, Amazigh and other peoples, includes writers from the region, and presents contested histories, including the Israeli–Palestinian conflict, through several perspectives without taking sides.

# About Middle Eastern studies

The Middle East and North Africa stretch from Morocco to Iran and from Turkey to Yemen. The region is home to some of the world's oldest cities, the birthplace of Judaism, Christianity and Islam, great empires, and peoples speaking Arabic, Persian, Turkish, Kurdish, Hebrew, Amazigh languages and more.

This path begins with accessible histories and voices from the region, studies the Ottoman and Safavid eras, examines colonial rule and the drawing of borders, follows nationalism, oil and the Cold War, studies revolutions, wars and the Arab uprisings, and ends with culture and the region today.

**Trivia**
- The Sykes–Picot Agreement of 1916 secretly divided Ottoman Arab lands between Britain and France.
- The Ottoman Empire lasted over 600 years, from around 1299 to 1922.
- Naguib Mahfouz of Egypt became the first Arabic-language writer to win the Nobel Prize in Literature, in 1988.
- About half of the world's proven oil reserves are in the Middle East.

### Timeline
- **1299 · The Ottoman Empire** is founded.
- **1501 · The Safavid dynasty** makes Shi'a Islam Iran's state religion.
- **1798 · Napoleon** invades Egypt.
- **1916 · Sykes–Picot** and the Arab Revolt.
- **1923 · The Republic of Turkey** is founded.
- **1948 · The State of Israel** is founded; the Nakba displaces some 700,000 Palestinians.
- **1952 · Egypt's revolution** brings Nasser to power.
- **1979 · The Iranian Revolution** creates an Islamic Republic.
- **1991 · The Gulf War** reverses Iraq's invasion of Kuwait.
- **2011 · The Arab uprisings** spread across the region.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know the Middle East the way a strong first year of a university area studies course expects. You will be able to:

- **Trace the region's modern history:** from the Ottomans to today.
- **Explain how borders and states were made:** and the legacy of colonial rule.
- **Explain politics:** nationalism, Islamism, oil, authoritarianism and revolution.
- **Engage with the region's cultures:** literature, film and music.
- **Weigh contested histories:** using sources and scholars from several sides.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Scholars from across the region and outside it, with different perspectives, will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
