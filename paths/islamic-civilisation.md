# Islamic civilisation

Fourteen centuries of a world civilisation: its foundations, its scholars and scientists, its art, poetry and cities, its great empires, and the debates of Muslims and scholars today. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Many classic texts are free in older translations. The path studies Islamic civilisation as historians and scholars do: it describes beliefs and debates fairly, includes Muslim and non-Muslim scholars, and **does not argue for or against the faith**.

# About Islamic civilisation

Islamic civilisation is the culture and history shaped by Islam and by the peoples who lived within Muslim societies, from Spain and West Africa to Central Asia, India and Indonesia, since the seventh century. It includes religion and law, but also science and medicine, philosophy, poetry, art, architecture, trade and great empires, and Jews, Christians and others who lived and worked within it.

This path begins with lively histories, studies the foundations of the faith, explores the scholars and scientists of its golden ages, reads its art, literature and cities, follows its great empires into modern times, and ends with today's debates among Muslims and scholars.

**Trivia**
- The word “algebra” comes from the Arabic *al-jabr*, in the title of a book by the ninth-century mathematician al-Khwarizmi, whose name gave us “algorithm”.
- The University of al-Qarawiyyin in Fez, founded by Fatima al-Fihri in 859, is often cited as the oldest university still operating.
- Indonesia, not any Arab country, has the largest Muslim population in the world.
- Ibn al-Haytham's *Book of Optics* (around 1011–1021) correctly explained that we see because light enters the eye.

### Timeline
- **610–632 · The Prophet Muhammad** receives the Qur'an, in Muslim belief.
- **661–750 · The Umayyad caliphate** stretches from Spain to Central Asia.
- **762 · Baghdad** is founded by the Abbasids.
- **9th–10th century · The House of Wisdom** and the translation movement.
- **c. 1025 · Ibn Sina's *Canon of Medicine***
- **1258 · The Mongols sack Baghdad**
- **1377 · Ibn Khaldun's *Muqaddimah***
- **1453 · The Ottomans take Constantinople**
- **1632–1653 · The Taj Mahal** is built by the Mughals.
- **1924 · The Ottoman caliphate** is abolished.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know Islamic civilisation the way a strong first year of a university Islamic-studies or history course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain the foundations:** the Qur'an, the Prophet, the Five Pillars, and the main traditions, fairly.
- **Describe scholarly achievements:** in mathematics, medicine, optics, philosophy and history.
- **Read art and literature:** architecture, calligraphy, poetry and story.
- **Follow the empires:** from the caliphates to the Ottomans, Safavids and Mughals.
- **Weigh modern debates:** interpretation, gender, diversity, and how Islam is studied.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Scholars of Islam and Islamic history, Muslim and non-Muslim, will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
