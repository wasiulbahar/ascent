# Photography

Photography as craft, art and evidence: light and exposure, the history of the medium across the world, documentary and photojournalism, art photography and theory, and images in the age of phones and AI. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months, with plenty of shooting.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free tutorials and museum collections cover much of the path, and any phone camera is enough to start. Every stage asks you to make photographs. Photographers from Africa, Asia, Latin America and elsewhere are included alongside Europe and North America.

# About photography

Photography is the making of images with light. It is a craft, with its own technique of exposure, focus, light and composition; an art, shown in galleries and books; and a kind of evidence, used in journalism, science, law and family memory. Photography studies asks how photographs are made, how they mean, and how they are used.

This path begins with the classic writers on photography, teaches exposure, light and composition, follows the history of photography across the world, studies documentary photography and photojournalism, reads art photography and theory, and ends with photography today: phones, social media, manipulation and AI.

**Trivia**
- The oldest surviving camera photograph, a view from a window taken by Nicéphore Niépce in about 1826–27, needed an exposure of hours or even days.
- The daguerreotype was announced in Paris in 1839, the year often given as photography's birth.
- Dorothea Lange's 1936 photograph *Migrant Mother* is in the US Library of Congress and free for anyone to use.
- More photographs are now taken every two minutes than were taken in the whole nineteenth century, by some estimates.

### Timeline
- **c. 1826–27 · Niépce** The oldest surviving camera photograph.
- **1839 · The daguerreotype** is announced; Talbot's negative process follows.
- **1888 · The Kodak camera** “You press the button, we do the rest.”
- **1925 · The Leica** A small 35 mm camera changes street photography.
- **1936 · Lange's *Migrant Mother***
- **1947 · Magnum Photos** is founded by photographers.
- **1950s · Seydou Keïta's studio portraits** in Bamako, Mali.
- **1975 · The first digital camera** is built at Kodak.
- **2000s · Camera phones** make everyone a photographer.
- **2020s · AI-generated images** challenge what a photograph is.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know photography the way a strong first year of a university photography course expects. You will be able to:

- **Control a camera:** exposure, focus, light and composition, with a phone or any camera.
- **Read photographs:** what they show, how they were made and how they mean.
- **Explain photography's history:** across several continents, from Niépce to the phone.
- **Think about documentary ethics:** consent, representation and images of suffering.
- **Weigh today's debates:** manipulation, AI images, authenticity and who owns images.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Photographers and historians of photography from more than one continent will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages makes you a capable student of photography. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Photography** certificate, you also complete:

1. **The grand quiz.** 40 game-style questions across all six stages. Pass mark 80%, with unlimited retakes.
2. **Portfolio.** A portfolio of twenty photographs showing control of light, exposure and composition.
3. **Image analysis.** Analyse an unseen photograph: its making, meaning and context.
4. **Capstone essay.** 2,500 words on one of the Stage 6 questions. Strong essays are read by an expert reviewer.
5. **Explain it.** Answer three unseen questions as if to a curious friend. For example: “Why do some photos look blurry?”

The certificate needs every stage passed.
