# Architecture

How to look at buildings, how they are designed, the great architectures of the world, modernism and its critics, cities and climate, and architecture today. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free online resources and classic texts cover much of the path, and the best teacher is the street outside: walk, look and sketch. Architecture from every continent is included. This path is not a professional qualification.

# About architecture

Architecture is the art and science of designing buildings and the spaces around them. It balances what the Roman writer Vitruvius called firmness, usefulness and delight: buildings must stand up, work for the people in them, and move us. It is shaped by climate, materials, money, technology and culture.

This path begins with books on why buildings matter, teaches how to see and describe architecture, travels through the world's building traditions, follows modern architecture and its critics, looks at cities, climate and housing, and ends with architecture today.

**Trivia**
- The Pantheon in Rome, finished around 125 CE, still has the world's largest unreinforced concrete dome.
- Buildings and construction account for about a third of the world's energy-related carbon dioxide emissions.
- Diébédo Francis Kéré, from Burkina Faso, became the first African architect to win the Pritzker Prize, in 2022.
- Hassan Fathy built the village of New Gourna in Egypt in the 1940s from mud brick, reviving ancient Nubian vaulting.

### Timeline
- **c. 2560 BCE · The Great Pyramid of Giza**
- **447–432 BCE · The Parthenon** in Athens.
- **c. 125 CE · The Pantheon** in Rome.
- **537 · Hagia Sophia** in Constantinople.
- **12th century · Angkor Wat** in Cambodia.
- **1632–1653 · The Taj Mahal** in Agra.
- **1851 · The Crystal Palace** in London: iron and glass.
- **1919 · The Bauhaus** is founded.
- **1966 · Venturi's *Complexity and Contradiction*** challenges modernism.
- **1997 · The Guggenheim Bilbao** by Frank Gehry.
- **2022 · Kéré** First African winner of the Pritzker Prize.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know architecture the way a strong first year of a university architecture or architectural-history course expects. You will be able to:

- **See and describe buildings:** form, space, light, structure, materials and use.
- **Range across traditions:** African, Asian, Islamic, European and American architecture.
- **Explain modern architecture:** its ideas, its achievements and its critics.
- **Connect buildings and cities:** streets, housing, climate and sustainability.
- **Weigh today's debates:** carbon, housing, heritage and who architecture is for.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Architects and architectural historians from more than one continent will confirm them before the path is published. Knowledge only: not a professional qualification.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages makes you a capable student of architecture. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Architecture** certificate, you also complete:

1. **The grand quiz.** 40 game-style questions across all six stages. Pass mark 80%, with unlimited retakes.
2. **Building study.** Visit and analyse one building: its site, form, space, structure, materials, use and meaning, with sketches or photographs.
3. **Comparison.** Compare two buildings from different traditions that serve the same purpose.
4. **Capstone essay.** 2,500 words on one of the Stage 6 questions. Strong essays are read by an expert reviewer.
5. **Explain it.** Answer three unseen questions as if to a curious friend. For example: “Why do old buildings in hot countries have thick walls?”

The certificate needs every stage passed, and is not a professional qualification.
