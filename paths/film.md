# Film

How films work and how to read them: shots, editing and sound, the great cinemas of the world, film theory, the industry, and the future of moving images. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Many early films are free and out of copyright, and libraries often lend films and streaming access. The path gives the cinemas of India, Japan, Africa, Latin America and Europe the same weight as Hollywood.

# About film

Film studies looks at cinema as an art, an industry and a part of culture. It asks how films create meaning through framing, movement, editing, sound and performance; how cinema developed in different countries; what theorists have said about images and audiences; and how money, technology and politics shape what reaches the screen.

This path begins with a world history of cinema and filmmakers on their craft, teaches film form, travels through great national cinemas, reads the key theories, looks at the industries from Hollywood to Bollywood and Nollywood, and ends with today's questions about streaming, preservation and the canon.

**Trivia**
- The Lumière brothers held one of the first public film screenings for a paying audience in Paris on 28 December 1895.
- India produces more feature films each year than any other country, in many languages.
- Nigeria's film industry, Nollywood, grew from low-budget video films in the 1990s into one of the largest in the world by number of films.
- In the 2022 *Sight and Sound* critics' poll, Chantal Akerman's *Jeanne Dielman* (1975) was voted the greatest film of all time, the first film by a woman to top it.

### Timeline
- **1895 · The Lumière brothers** Public screenings in Paris.
- **1902 · Méliès's *A Trip to the Moon***
- **1913 · *Raja Harishchandra*** India's first full-length feature film.
- **1925 · Eisenstein's *Battleship Potemkin*** and Soviet montage.
- **1927 · *The Jazz Singer*** Synchronised dialogue arrives.
- **1948 · *Bicycle Thieves*** and Italian neorealism.
- **1953 · Ozu's *Tokyo Story***
- **1955 · Satyajit Ray's *Pather Panchali***
- **1966 · Sembène's *Black Girl*** An early feature by a sub-Saharan African director.
- **1990s · Nollywood** grows in Nigeria.
- **2010s · Streaming** changes how films are made and watched.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know film the way a strong first year of a university film-studies degree expects. You will be able to:

- **Analyse film form:** shots, mise-en-scène, camera movement, editing and sound.
- **Place films in history:** from silent cinema to streaming, across many countries.
- **Use film theory:** montage, realism, the gaze and political cinema.
- **Explain the industries:** Hollywood, Bollywood, Nollywood and independent film.
- **Write about film:** clear, evidence-based analysis of scenes and whole films.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Film scholars and filmmakers from several countries will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages makes you a capable student of film. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Film** certificate, you also complete:
