# Archaeology

How we learn about the human past from what people left behind: excavation and dating, the first farmers and cities, civilisations on every continent, the ethics of who owns the past, and new technologies. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free courses, museum collections, a famous radio series and an open-access journal cover much of the path. Archaeology from every continent is included, and the path takes seriously the views of the descendant communities whose past is studied.

# About archaeology

Archaeology studies the human past through material remains: tools, buildings, bones, pottery, seeds, rubbish and whole landscapes. It reaches back long before writing, and adds the lives of ordinary people to the history written by the powerful. It uses excavation, survey, dating methods, DNA, satellites and laser scanning.

This path begins with books that make the past vivid, teaches archaeological methods, explores human origins and the first farmers, visits ancient cities and civilisations across the world, weighs theory and the ethics of who owns the past, and ends with today's technologies and threats to heritage.

**Trivia**
- Göbekli Tepe in Turkey, with carved stone pillars built about 11,500 years ago, is older than farming towns and Stonehenge.
- Radiocarbon dating, developed by Willard Libby in the late 1940s, earned him the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1960.
- The Indus script, used in cities such as Harappa and Mohenjo-daro over 4,000 years ago, has still not been deciphered.
- Laser scanning from aircraft (lidar) has revealed thousands of hidden Maya structures beneath the Guatemalan forest.

### Timeline
- **c. 9500 BCE · Göbekli Tepe** Monumental carved pillars.
- **c. 7100 BCE · Çatalhöyük** A large early farming town in Anatolia.
- **c. 2600–1900 BCE · The Indus cities** flourish.
- **79 CE · Vesuvius** buries Pompeii.
- **c. 1100–1450 · Great Zimbabwe** a stone city in southern Africa.
- **1748 · Excavations at Pompeii** begin.
- **1922 · Tutankhamun's tomb** is opened.
- **1949 · Radiocarbon dating** is first published.
- **1974 · The Terracotta Army** is found in China.
- **1990 · NAGPRA** US law on returning Native American remains and objects.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know archaeology the way a strong first year of a university archaeology degree expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain archaeological methods:** survey, excavation, stratigraphy and dating.
- **Describe major turning points:** human origins, farming, cities and states, on several continents.
- **Read material evidence:** what objects, bones and landscapes can and can't tell us.
- **Weigh the ethics:** who owns the past, looting, and the rights of descendant communities.
- **Explain new techniques:** ancient DNA, satellites, lidar and scientific analysis.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Archaeologists working on several continents, and representatives of descendant communities, will confirm them before the path is published.
