# Epidemiology

How we learn what causes disease and what prevents it: measuring health in populations, study designs, causation and bias, infectious disease modelling and outbreak investigation, and field practice and research. About 200 to 250 hours over eight to ten months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free courses from the CDC, WHO, universities and open textbooks cover the core. You need school maths; basic statistics helps from Stage 2. The Public health path gives the wider picture; this path goes deeper into methods.

# About epidemiology

Epidemiology studies how diseases and health are distributed in populations and what causes them. It is the basic science of public health: it showed that smoking causes lung cancer, tracks epidemics and tests whether treatments and policies work.

This path begins with gripping stories and a classic study, teaches how to measure disease, explains the main study designs, tackles causation, confounding and bias, studies infectious disease with models and outbreak investigations, and ends with field practice, reporting standards and research.

**Trivia**
- In 1662 John Graunt analysed London's weekly death records, the “bills of mortality”, in one of the first works of statistics and epidemiology.
- Smallpox is the only human disease ever eradicated; the WHO declared it gone in 1980.
- In 1747 the naval surgeon James Lind compared six treatments for scurvy on sailors; the two who ate citrus fruit recovered fastest.
- The British Doctors Study, begun by Richard Doll and Austin Bradford Hill, followed doctors' smoking and health for 50 years.

### Timeline
- **1662 · John Graunt** analyses London's bills of mortality.
- **1747 · James Lind** runs a controlled trial on scurvy.
- **1854 · John Snow** links a London cholera outbreak to a water pump.
- **1948 · The Framingham Heart Study** begins; the MRC streptomycin trial is published.
- **1950 · Doll and Hill** report that smoking is linked to lung cancer.
- **1965 · Austin Bradford Hill** sets out his viewpoints on causation.
- **1980 · The WHO** declares smallpox eradicated.
- **1981 · The first AIDS cases** are reported.
- **2020 · COVID-19** is declared a pandemic.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know epidemiology the way a strong introductory postgraduate course expects. You will be able to:

- **Measure disease:** incidence, prevalence, rates, risks and standardisation.
- **Choose study designs:** cohort, case-control, cross-sectional and randomised trials.
- **Reason about causes:** confounding, bias, chance and causal inference.
- **Study infectious disease:** transmission, R0, models, outbreak investigation and genomics.
- **Read and report research:** with critical appraisal and reporting guidelines.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Epidemiologists and public health educators from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages makes you a capable student of epidemiology. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Epidemiology** certificate, you also complete:

1. **The grand quiz.** 40 game-style questions across all six stages. Pass mark 80%, with unlimited retakes.
2. **Calculation set.** Compute rates, risks, odds ratios and test accuracy from unseen data.
3. **Study protocol.** Design a study to answer a real question, with its biases and how you would reduce them.
4. **Capstone essay.** 2,500 words on one of the Stage 6 questions. Strong essays are read by an expert reviewer.
5. **Explain it.** Answer three unseen questions as if to a friend. For example: “Does correlation ever prove causation?”

The certificate needs every stage passed.
