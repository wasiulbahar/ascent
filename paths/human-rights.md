# Human rights

The rights everyone has because they are human: where the idea came from, the treaties that define them, how they are defended and violated, the debates about universality, justice after atrocity, and new frontiers from privacy to climate. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. All the key treaties and many reports are free from the UN and rights groups. The path includes defenders, critics and voices from the Global South. **It is not legal advice.**

# About human rights

Human rights are the basic rights and freedoms that belong to every person: life, liberty, equality, freedom of thought and expression, fair trial, work, health, education and more. Human rights studies looks at their history and philosophy, the law that protects them, the movements that fight for them, and the arguments about whether they are universal.

This path begins with the founding texts and stories of why rights matter, teaches the history and core treaties, examines rights in practice and the groups that monitor them, weighs the great debates, studies justice after atrocity and the rights of refugees and Indigenous peoples, and ends with new frontiers: privacy, the environment and business.

**Trivia**
- The Universal Declaration of Human Rights, adopted on 10 December 1948, is among the most translated documents in the world.
- Its drafters included Eleanor Roosevelt (United States), P. C. Chang (China), Charles Malik (Lebanon) and René Cassin (France).
- In 2022 the UN General Assembly recognised the right to a clean, healthy and sustainable environment.
- 10 December is marked each year as Human Rights Day.

### Timeline
- **1215 · Magna Carta**
- **1789 · The Declaration of the Rights of Man** in France.
- **1948 · The Universal Declaration of Human Rights**
- **1951 · The Refugee Convention**
- **1966 · The two International Covenants** civil and political; economic, social and cultural.
- **1979 · CEDAW** on the rights of women.
- **1993 · The Vienna Declaration** Rights are universal, indivisible and interdependent.
- **1998 · The Rome Statute** creates the International Criminal Court.
- **2007 · The Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples**
- **2011 · The UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights**

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know human rights the way a strong first year of a university human-rights course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain the history of rights:** from early declarations to the UN system.
- **Read the core treaties:** the Universal Declaration, the Covenants, CEDAW and others.
- **Analyse rights in practice:** monitoring, reporting and campaigning.
- **Weigh the debates:** universality and culture, critiques from the Global South, and whether rights work.
- **Explain new frontiers:** privacy, the environment, business and accountability.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Human-rights scholars and practitioners from more than one continent will confirm them before the path is published. Knowledge only: not legal advice.
