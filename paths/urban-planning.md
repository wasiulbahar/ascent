# Urban planning

How cities are shaped and for whom: the case for cities, the classic planning ideas, transport and housing, power and the right to the city, sustainable and resilient cities, and new ideas for the cities of the future. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. UN and city-network reports are free, and your own city is the best case study: walk it, map it and question it. Cities from every continent are included. This path is not a professional planning qualification.

# About urban planning

Urban planning decides how cities and towns grow and change: where homes, jobs, streets, parks and public transport go, how land is used, and how places are governed. More than half the world's people live in cities, so planning shapes daily life, health, wealth, inequality and the climate.

This path begins with books on why cities matter and what makes them good to live in, reads the classic planning ideas, studies transport, parking and housing, examines power and politics in cities, looks at sustainability and resilience, and ends with new ideas such as smart cities and the 15-minute city.

**Trivia**
- More than half the world's people have lived in cities since about 2007; the UN expects about two-thirds by 2050.
- Curitiba in Brazil opened one of the world's first bus rapid transit systems in 1974, copied by cities around the world.
- Brasília, Brazil's capital, was planned from scratch and built in about four years, opening in 1960.
- The sociologist Ruth Glass coined the word “gentrification” in 1964, describing London.

### Timeline
- **c. 2600 BCE · Mohenjo-daro** A planned city with grid streets and drains.
- **1850s–70s · Haussmann** rebuilds Paris with wide boulevards.
- **1898 · Ebenezer Howard** proposes garden cities.
- **1933 · The Athens Charter** Modernist planning principles.
- **1960 · Brasília** opens.
- **1961 · Jane Jacobs** *The Death and Life of Great American Cities*.
- **1974 · Curitiba's bus rapid transit**
- **2007 · The world becomes mostly urban**
- **2016 · The New Urban Agenda** is adopted at Habitat III in Quito.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know urban planning the way a strong first year of a university planning degree expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain what makes cities work:** density, mixed use, streets, public space and access.
- **Read the classic ideas:** garden cities, modernism, Jacobs and the image of the city.
- **Analyse transport and housing:** public transport, parking, informal settlements and affordability.
- **Understand urban politics:** who decides, who benefits, and the right to the city.
- **Weigh the future:** climate, resilience, technology and new planning ideas.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Planners and urban scholars from more than one continent will confirm them before the path is published. Knowledge only: not a professional qualification.
