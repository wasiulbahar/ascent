# Sport and Exercise Science

How bodies move, train and perform: exercise physiology, biomechanics, training and recovery, sport psychology and nutrition, coaching and data, and the ethics of doping, fairness and inclusion. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free textbooks, public health guidelines and research summaries cover the core. The path separates evidence from hype, covers sport across many countries and abilities, and presents debates about fairness fairly. **It is not medical, training or nutrition advice for you personally.**

# About sport and exercise science

Sport and exercise science studies how the human body responds to physical activity, and how athletes and ordinary people can train, perform and stay healthy. It combines physiology, biomechanics, psychology, nutrition and data analysis.

This path begins with lively books on what makes athletes great, teaches exercise physiology and biomechanics, explains training and recovery, explores the mind and fuel of sport, studies coaching and data, and ends with doping, fairness and physical activity for everyone.

**Trivia**
- The WHO recommends adults do at least 150 to 300 minutes of moderate aerobic activity a week.
- Roger Bannister ran the first recorded sub-four-minute mile in 1954; the record has since fallen by about 16 seconds.
- VO₂ max, the maximum rate of oxygen use, is one of the strongest measures of endurance fitness.
- Eliud Kipchoge ran a marathon in under two hours in 2019, in a specially arranged event not eligible as a record.

### Timeline
- **1920s · A. V. Hill** measures oxygen use in running, founding exercise physiology.
- **1954 · Roger Bannister** runs a sub-four-minute mile.
- **1960s · Muscle biopsy studies** reveal how glycogen fuels endurance.
- **1968 · Kenneth Cooper's *Aerobics*** popularises fitness.
- **1999 · The World Anti-Doping Agency** is founded.
- **2000s · GPS trackers and data analytics** spread through professional sport.
- **2010 · WHO global recommendations** on physical activity.
- **2016 · The McLaren Report** exposes state-sponsored doping in Russia.
- **2019 · The sub-two-hour marathon** Eliud Kipchoge.
- **2020 · Updated WHO guidelines** on physical activity and sedentary behaviour.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know sport and exercise science the way a strong first year of a university course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain exercise physiology:** energy systems, the heart and lungs, and muscles.
- **Explain biomechanics:** forces, levers and how technique affects performance.
- **Explain training principles:** overload, specificity, recovery and periodisation.
- **Explain the mind and fuel of sport:** psychology and nutrition, with evidence.
- **Weigh the debates:** doping, fairness, inclusion and data.
