# Graphic Design

How to communicate with type, image, colour and layout: the principles of design, typography and grids, identities, signs and symbols, posters, print and information graphics, digital and motion design, and the history, ethics and craft of the profession. About 150 to 200 hours over six to eight months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free guides, museum collections, design systems and open-source tools such as Inkscape cover the core; a few classic books are worth borrowing. Design something at every stage. The path includes design traditions and writing systems from around the world, not only Western ones.

# About graphic design

Graphic design is the craft of arranging words and images to communicate: posters, books, logos, signs, websites, apps, packaging and information graphics. Good design is clear, purposeful and often invisible.

This path begins with how designers see and the tools to start, teaches visual principles, grids, colour and typography, explores identities, signs and symbols, covers posters, print and information design, moves to digital, inclusive and motion design, and ends with history, ethics, global scripts and building a portfolio.

**Trivia**
- Helvetica, released in 1957, was first called Neue Haas Grotesk.
- Google's Noto fonts aim to cover every writing system; the name comes from “no more tofu”, the blank boxes shown for missing characters.
- Otl Aicher's pictograms for the 1972 Munich Olympics influenced the signs in airports and stations worldwide.
- NASA retired its red “worm” logo in 1992, then brought it back in 2020.

### Timeline
- **c. 1450 · Johannes Gutenberg** prints with movable metal type in Europe.
- **1919 · The Bauhaus** opens in Weimar.
- **1925 · Otto Neurath** begins developing the Isotype picture language.
- **1927 · Paul Renner** releases the Futura typeface.
- **1957 · Helvetica** is released.
- **1964 · Designers** publish the “First Things First” manifesto.
- **1965 · British road signs** adopt Kinneir and Calvert's design.
- **1972 · Otl Aicher** designs the Munich Olympics pictograms.
- **1985 · Desktop publishing** arrives with affordable computers and laser printers.
- **2014 · Google** publishes Material Design.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will design the way a capable junior graphic designer does. You will be able to:

- **Use visual principles:** hierarchy, contrast, alignment, grids and Gestalt.
- **Set type well:** choosing, pairing and arranging typefaces for reading.
- **Design identities and systems:** logos, signage and consistent brand guidelines.
- **Design for print and screens:** posters, information graphics, interfaces and motion.
- **Work as a professional:** with history, inclusivity, ethics and a strong portfolio.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Graphic designers and design educators from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.

# Final mastery check

Finishing all six stages makes you a capable graphic designer. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Graphic Design** certificate, you also complete:

1. **The grand quiz.** 40 game-style questions across all six stages. Pass mark 80%, with unlimited retakes.
2. **Identity project.** Design a full identity with logo, type, colour and guidelines for a real client or cause.
3. **Portfolio.** Six finished pieces across print, screen and information design, with process notes.
4. **Capstone essay.** 2,500 words on one of the Stage 6 questions. Strong essays are read by an expert reviewer.
5. **Explain it.** Present and defend your portfolio to a reviewer or peer group.

The certificate needs every stage passed.
