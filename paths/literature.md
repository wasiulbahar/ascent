# Literature

From great stories that grip anyone to how critics read, argue and decide what counts as literature. About 280 to 340 hours over twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Many classics are free online in older translations. This is world literature, not one country's: it moves between Greece, the Arabic and Persian worlds, Japan, China, Africa, Latin America, Europe and North America, in translation where needed.

# About literature

Literature is writing that rewards rereading: epics, poems, plays, novels and stories that people return to across centuries and languages. Studying it means two things: reading closely, noticing how words, form and structure make meaning; and reading widely, seeing how works speak to their times and to each other across the world.

This path begins with stories almost everyone loves, teaches you how to read like a critic, travels through classics from several traditions, follows the rise of the novel, reaches the theory and criticism taught at university, and ends with the arguments about the canon and how literature should be studied.

**Trivia**
- *The Tale of Genji*, written in Japan about a thousand years ago by a woman of the imperial court, Murasaki Shikibu, is often called the world's first novel.
- Emily Wilson's 2017 *Odyssey* was the first published English translation of the poem by a woman.
- Chinua Achebe took the title *Things Fall Apart* from a poem by W. B. Yeats.
- The Kenyan novelist Ngũgĩ wa Thiong'o stopped writing his novels in English and began writing them in Gikuyu, his first language.

### Timeline
- **c. 2100 BCE · Gilgamesh** The earliest surviving great work of literature, from Mesopotamia.
- **c. 8th century BCE · Homer** The *Iliad* and the *Odyssey*.
- **c. 1000 · The Tale of Genji** Murasaki Shikibu's novel of the Japanese court.
- **c. 1320 · Dante** *The Divine Comedy* is completed.
- **c. 1600 · Shakespeare** *Hamlet*.
- **18th century · The Story of the Stone** China's greatest classical novel.
- **1813 · Austen** *Pride and Prejudice*.
- **1958 · Achebe** *Things Fall Apart*.
- **1967 · García Márquez** *One Hundred Years of Solitude*.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know literature the way a strong first and second year of a university literature degree expects. You will be able to:

- **Read closely:** explain how a passage's words, images and form create meaning.
- **Know major works from several traditions,** and how they connect.
- **Explain how the novel developed,** from Genji to the modernists and beyond.
- **Use the main critical approaches,** from formalism to postcolonial and feminist criticism.
- **Weigh the debates:** the canon, world literature, and close versus distant reading.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Scholars of more than one literary tradition will confirm them before the path is published.

Finishing all six stages makes you a capable student of literature. To earn the **Ascent Mastery: Literature** certificate, you also complete:
