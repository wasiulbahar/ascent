# Political science

From why politics matters to how states, elections and parties work around the world, the great political thinkers, and the debates about democracy's future. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Many sources, such as constitutions, data sets and open courses, are free. The path presents several political traditions and regions, and asks you to weigh arguments rather than adopt one side.

# About political science

Political science studies power: who gets what, when and how. It looks at states and governments, elections and parties, protest and revolution, public policy, and the ideas that justify or challenge authority. It uses history, philosophy, statistics and fieldwork.

This path begins with books that show why politics matters, explains how states and regimes differ, covers elections, parties and participation, compares political systems across the world, reads the great political thinkers, and ends with today's debates about democracy, populism and the future of the state.

**Trivia**
- The word “politics” comes from the Greek *polis*, the city-state.
- In 2022 more of the world's people lived under autocracies than under democracies, according to the V-Dem Institute.
- Australia has had compulsory voting for federal elections since 1924; turnout is usually above 90%.
- Switzerland holds several national referendums every year, more than any other country.

### Timeline
- **c. 508 BCE · Athenian democracy** Cleisthenes' reforms.
- **c. 350 BCE · Aristotle's *Politics*** compares constitutions.
- **1377 · Ibn Khaldun's *Muqaddimah*** on the rise and fall of states.
- **1513 · Machiavelli** *The Prince*.
- **1651 · Hobbes** *Leviathan*.
- **1787 · The US Constitution** and the separation of powers.
- **1848 · Revolutions across Europe**
- **1945 · The United Nations** is founded.
- **1989–91 · The end of the Cold War** A wave of democratisation.
- **2010s · Democratic backsliding** studied around the world.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know political science the way a strong first year of a university politics degree expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain how states work:** power, legitimacy, institutions and the rule of law.
- **Compare political systems:** democracies and autocracies, presidential and parliamentary, federal and unitary.
- **Analyse elections and participation:** voting, parties, electoral systems and protest.
- **Read the classic thinkers:** from Aristotle and Confucius to Hobbes, Locke, Rousseau, Mill and Arendt.
- **Weigh the debates:** democratic decline, populism and the future of the state.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Political scientists from more than one region will confirm them before the path is published.
