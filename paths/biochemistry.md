# Biochemistry

The chemistry of life: water and the building blocks of cells, proteins and enzymes, metabolism and energy, DNA, RNA and gene expression, cell signalling, and biochemistry in medicine and biotechnology. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free textbooks, molecular viewers and university courses cover the core. You need school chemistry and biology; the path revises them first. **It is knowledge only, not medical advice.**

# About biochemistry

Biochemistry studies the molecules and chemical reactions of living things: how proteins fold and catalyse reactions, how cells extract energy from food, how DNA stores and passes on information, and how cells signal to each other.

This path begins with books that make molecules vivid, revises the chemistry of life, studies proteins and enzymes, explores metabolism and energy, examines DNA, RNA and gene expression, and ends with signalling, medicine and biotechnology.

**Trivia**
- An enzyme can speed a reaction up by a factor of millions or more.
- Every cell in your body (except red blood cells) contains about 2 metres of DNA, packed into a nucleus a few thousandths of a millimetre across.
- AlphaFold, an AI system, predicted the structures of over 200 million proteins, and its creators shared the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.
- Your body recycles roughly its own weight in ATP every day.

### Timeline
- **1828 · Friedrich Wöhler** makes urea in the lab, blurring life and chemistry.
- **1897 · Eduard Buchner** shows cell-free yeast extract ferments sugar.
- **1926 · James Sumner** shows an enzyme is a protein.
- **1937 · Hans Krebs** describes the citric acid cycle.
- **1953 · The double helix** of DNA is described.
- **1958 · John Kendrew** determines the first protein structure.
- **1961 · Peter Mitchell** proposes chemiosmosis.
- **1977 · DNA sequencing** methods are invented.
- **1983 · PCR** is invented by Kary Mullis.
- **2020 · AlphaFold 2** predicts protein structures accurately.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know biochemistry the way a strong first year of a university biochemistry course expects. You will be able to:

- **Describe biomolecules:** carbohydrates, lipids, proteins and nucleic acids.
- **Explain protein structure and enzymes:** folding, catalysis and regulation.
- **Explain metabolism:** glycolysis, the citric acid cycle, oxidative phosphorylation and photosynthesis.
- **Explain the central dogma:** replication, transcription, translation and gene regulation.
- **Apply biochemistry:** to signalling, disease, drugs and biotechnology.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Biochemists and science educators from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published. Knowledge only: not medical advice.
