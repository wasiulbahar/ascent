# Cognitive Science

The interdisciplinary science of the mind: perception, attention and memory, language and thought, reasoning and decision-making, learning and development, the brain and computation, and minds in bodies, cultures and machines. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free courses, open textbooks and online experiments cover the core. The path draws on psychology, neuroscience, linguistics, philosophy, anthropology and AI, includes cross-cultural research, and is honest about studies that have failed to replicate.

# About cognitive science

Cognitive science studies how minds work: how we perceive, remember, learn, speak, reason and decide. It combines psychology, neuroscience, linguistics, philosophy, anthropology and computer science, treating the mind as an information-processing system while asking whether that view is enough.

This path begins with lively introductions, studies perception and attention, memory and learning, language and thought, reasoning and decisions, and ends with development, culture, embodiment and artificial minds.

**Trivia**
- George Miller's 1956 paper suggested short-term memory holds about “seven, plus or minus two” items; later research suggests closer to four chunks.
- In the “invisible gorilla” experiment, about half of viewers counting basketball passes failed to notice a person in a gorilla suit walking through.
- Children learn roughly ten new words a day between ages two and six, on some estimates.
- Most psychology research has studied people from Western, educated, industrialised, rich and democratic (WEIRD) societies, a small slice of humanity.

### Timeline
- **1879 · Wilhelm Wundt** opens the first psychology lab.
- **1936 · Alan Turing** defines computation.
- **1956 · The “cognitive revolution”** begins (Miller, Chomsky, early AI).
- **1959 · Chomsky** attacks behaviourism's account of language.
- **1974 · Tversky and Kahneman** publish on heuristics and biases.
- **1982 · David Marr's *Vision*** proposes levels of analysis.
- **1986 · Connectionism** (parallel distributed processing) revives neural networks.
- **1991 · *The Embodied Mind*** launches embodied cognition.
- **2010 · The WEIRD critique** questions psychology's samples.
- **2015 · The Reproducibility Project** replicates fewer than half of 100 psychology studies.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know cognitive science the way a strong first year of a university course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain the mind's main functions:** perception, attention, memory, language and reasoning.
- **Use cognitive science methods:** experiments, brain imaging, modelling and cross-cultural study.
- **Compare frameworks:** computational, connectionist, embodied and predictive.
- **Explain development and culture:** how minds grow and differ across societies.
- **Weigh evidence critically:** replication, WEIRD samples and claims about AI minds.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.
