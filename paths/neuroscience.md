# Neuroscience

How the brain and nervous system work: neurons and circuits, memory, emotion and behaviour, brain disease, consciousness, and the new technologies that read and stimulate the brain. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. A free online textbook, free short videos and free fact sheets cover the core. Popular neuroscience is full of overclaims, so the path flags debated findings. **It is not medical advice**: for a health concern, see a qualified professional.

# About neuroscience

Neuroscience studies the nervous system: how about 86 billion neurons in the human brain signal to each other, how circuits produce perception, movement, memory and emotion, how the brain develops and ages, what goes wrong in disease, and how all this gives rise to the mind.

This path begins with stories of unusual brains, teaches neurons and brain anatomy, explores memory, emotion and behaviour, covers methods and brain disease, weighs theories of consciousness, and ends at the frontier: brain–computer interfaces, the history of ideas about the brain, and neuroethics.

**Trivia**
- The human brain has roughly 86 billion neurons, according to a 2009 count by Suzana Herculano-Houzel and colleagues.
- Santiago Ramón y Cajal and Camillo Golgi shared the 1906 Nobel Prize, though they disagreed about whether neurons are separate cells; Cajal was right.
- Henry Molaison, known for decades as “H.M.”, could not form new long-term memories after surgery in 1953, and taught science much of what it knows about memory.
- An fMRI study once found “brain activity” in a dead salmon, a famous lesson about statistical errors in brain imaging.

### Timeline
- **c. 1600 BCE · The Edwin Smith Papyrus** Egypt: the earliest known description of the brain.
- **1848 · Phineas Gage** survives an iron rod through his frontal lobe.
- **1861 · Broca** links speech production to a region of the left frontal lobe.
- **1906 · Cajal and Golgi** share the Nobel Prize.
- **1952 · Hodgkin and Huxley** explain the nerve impulse.
- **1953 · H.M.'s surgery** and the science of memory.
- **1990s · fMRI** lets scientists see brain activity.
- **2005 · Optogenetics** Controlling neurons with light.
- **2010s–2020s · Brain–computer interfaces** restore communication to paralysed people.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know neuroscience the way a strong first year of a university neuroscience course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain how neurons work:** action potentials, synapses and neurotransmitters.
- **Find your way around the brain:** its main regions and systems.
- **Explain memory, emotion and behaviour:** and what case studies and experiments show.
- **Judge brain research:** what imaging and other methods can and can't tell us.
- **Weigh the debates:** consciousness, free will, and the ethics of neurotechnology.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Neuroscientists and clinicians from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published. Knowledge only: not medical advice.
