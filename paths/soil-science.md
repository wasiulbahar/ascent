# Soil Science

The living skin of the Earth: how soils form and are classified, their physics, chemistry and engineering behaviour, the life underground, how soils are lost and restored, and their role in food and climate. About 120 to 160 hours over five to seven months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free atlases, global maps, classification manuals and extension guides cover the core. You need school science. A spade, a jar and a patch of ground make simple experiments part of every stage.

# About soil science

Soil science studies the thin layer of minerals, water, air, organisms and organic matter that covers the land and supports almost all food production.

This path starts with the wonder of soil, learns how soils form and are classified, studies their physics, chemistry and engineering behaviour, explores soil life, examines erosion and degradation, and ends with soils and climate and current research.

**Trivia**
- Forming a single centimetre of topsoil can take hundreds of years.
- Soils hold more carbon than the atmosphere and all plants combined.
- Charles Darwin's last book was about earthworms and how they make soil.
- Some dark Amazonian soils, enriched by people centuries ago, are still more fertile than the soils around them.

### Timeline
- **1840 · Justus von Liebig** explains plant needs for mineral nutrients.
- **1881 · Charles Darwin** publishes his study of earthworms and soil.
- **1883 · Vasily Dokuchaev** publishes *Russian Chernozem*, founding soil science.
- **1888 · Hellriegel and Wilfarth** show legumes fix nitrogen with root nodules.
- **1925 · Karl Terzaghi** founds modern soil mechanics.
- **1930s · The Dust Bowl** strips topsoil from the US Great Plains.
- **1941 · Hans Jenny** describes the factors of soil formation.
- **1998 · The World Reference Base** offers an international soil classification.
- **2015 · The International Year of Soils** is marked and the 4 per 1000 initiative launches.
- **2015 · FAO** publishes the first global report on soil resources.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know soil science the way a strong undergraduate course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain soil formation:** the factors and processes that make soils.
- **Describe and classify soils:** horizons, texture and international systems.
- **Explain soil physics and chemistry:** water, nutrients, pH and strength.
- **Describe soil life:** microbes, fungi, fauna and nutrient cycles.
- **Assess soil threats and solutions:** erosion, salinity, carbon and restoration.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Soil scientists from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
