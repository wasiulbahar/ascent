# Accounting

The language of business: double-entry bookkeeping, financial statements, management accounting and budgets, auditing and fraud, tax, international standards, and accounting for sustainability. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. Free textbooks, standard-setters' materials and company annual reports cover the core. The path uses international standards (IFRS) and examples from many countries, and covers scandals and critiques as well as techniques. **It is knowledge, not a professional qualification or financial advice.**

# About accounting

Accounting records, summarises and reports the financial activity of organisations so that owners, managers, lenders, governments and the public can make decisions. It is often called the language of business.

This path begins with the surprising history and stories of accounting, teaches double-entry bookkeeping and the financial statements, explains management accounting, studies auditing, fraud and scandals, covers tax and international standards, and ends with sustainability reporting and the future of the profession.

**Trivia**
- Luca Pacioli published the first printed description of double-entry bookkeeping in Venice in 1494.
- The basic accounting equation is: assets = liabilities + equity.
- Enron's collapse in 2001 also brought down Arthur Andersen, then one of the world's five largest accounting firms.
- Over 140 jurisdictions require or permit International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS) for listed companies.

### Timeline
- **c. 3000 BCE · Mesopotamian clay tokens and tablets** record goods.
- **1494 · Luca Pacioli** describes double-entry bookkeeping.
- **1854 · The first professional accountancy body** is founded in Edinburgh.
- **1929–34 · The Wall Street Crash** leads to US securities laws requiring audited accounts.
- **1973 · The International Accounting Standards Committee** is founded.
- **2001 · Enron** collapses.
- **2002 · The Sarbanes–Oxley Act** tightens US corporate governance.
- **2005 · The EU** adopts IFRS for listed companies.
- **2018 · Carillion** collapses in the UK, prompting audit reform.
- **2023 · The ISSB** issues global sustainability disclosure standards.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know accounting the way a strong first year of a university accounting course expects. You will be able to:

- **Record transactions:** with double-entry bookkeeping.
- **Read financial statements:** the balance sheet, income statement and cash flow statement.
- **Use management accounting:** costing, budgeting and decision-making.
- **Explain auditing and fraud:** how audits work and how scandals happen.
- **Explain standards, tax and sustainability reporting:** and their debates.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Accountants and accounting academics from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published. Knowledge only: not a professional qualification or financial advice.

**How stages work.** Each work ends with a short quiz. Score 70% or more across a stage's quizzes to pass it. If you fall short, you can retake the quizzes, or continue anyway: the stage then stays marked **Not finished** until you come back and pass it.
