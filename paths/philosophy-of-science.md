# Philosophy of Science

What science is and why we trust it: induction and falsification, paradigms and revolutions, explanation and evidence, realism, values and objectivity, the replication crisis, and science in society. About 250 to 300 hours over ten to twelve months.

**Access and balance.** Every title is labelled **Free**, **Free + paid** or **Paid**. The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy and many classic papers are free. The path presents defenders and critics of science's methods fairly, includes non-Western science and feminist philosophy of science, and distinguishes healthy scepticism from denial.

# About the philosophy of science

Philosophy of science asks what makes science different from other ways of knowing, how evidence supports theories, whether science describes reality or just useful models, and how values and society shape research.

This path begins with lively introductions, studies the classic debates over induction and falsification, explores Kuhn's revolutions and their critics, examines explanation, evidence and realism, looks at values, objectivity and the history of science worldwide, and ends with the replication crisis and science in society.

**Trivia**
- David Hume argued in the 1730s–40s that no amount of past observation can logically guarantee the future: the problem of induction.
- Karl Popper proposed that a theory is scientific only if it could be proved false by some observation.
- Thomas Kuhn's *The Structure of Scientific Revolutions* (1962) made “paradigm shift” an everyday phrase.
- A 2015 project tried to replicate 100 psychology studies and succeeded in fewer than half.

### Timeline
- **c. 1011–1021 · Ibn al-Haytham's *Book of Optics*** stresses experiment.
- **1620 · Francis Bacon's *Novum Organum*** champions induction.
- **1739–48 · David Hume** poses the problem of induction.
- **1920s–30s · The Vienna Circle** develops logical positivism.
- **1934 · Karl Popper** proposes falsification.
- **1962 · Thomas Kuhn** describes scientific revolutions.
- **1975 · Paul Feyerabend's *Against Method*** challenges the idea of one scientific method.
- **1980s · Feminist philosophy of science** examines bias and objectivity.
- **2005 · John Ioannidis** argues most published research findings are false.
- **2015 · The Reproducibility Project** reports psychology's replication rates.

# What you will be able to do

At the end of this path you will know the philosophy of science the way a strong first year of a university course expects. You will be able to:

- **Explain how evidence supports theories:** induction, falsification and confirmation.
- **Explain how science changes:** Kuhn, Lakatos and their critics.
- **Weigh realism and anti-realism:** does science describe reality?
- **Discuss values and objectivity:** including feminist and global perspectives.
- **Evaluate science in society:** replication, expertise, denial and trust.
- **For the Mastery certificate:** have written the Scholar challenges and passed the final mastery check.

These goals are a draft standard. Philosophers and historians of science from more than one country will confirm them before the path is published.
