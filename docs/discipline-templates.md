# Ascent: path templates by kind of subject

Dostoevsky is one example. A path on particle physics or neurosurgery cannot be built the same way: the core material is different, the skills are different, and so is what "mastery" can honestly mean. Every path keeps the shared skeleton from `depth-framework.md` (About, goals, First Encounters, stages, quizzes, final mastery check). This file sets out how each kind of subject fills it.

## At a glance

| Kind of subject | Core material | First Encounters looks like | Quizzes test | Mastery means | Typical length |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Author or literary tradition | The works themselves, read in order of accessibility | Short, gripping works | Plot, meaning, debates, critics | Read the whole canon, know the context and critics | 6 to 12 months |
| Music and the arts | Listening, viewing, looking | Famous, joyful pieces | Recognising periods, forms and styles by ear or eye | Know the repertoire across periods and how it works | 6 to 12 months |
| Humanities and social sciences | Key books, primary sources, competing schools | A gripping narrative book or documentary | Schools of thought, key texts, applying ideas to cases | Explain and argue the main schools; analyse a real case | 6 to 12 months |
| Natural sciences | Textbooks with problem sets, plus prerequisites | Wonder-driven popular science by real scientists | Numbers and problems, not only facts | Solve standard problems; read research papers | 1 to 3 years |
| Mathematics and computing | Exercises, proofs, code | Puzzles and visual intuition | Worked problems, proofs, code that runs | Prove and build at university level | 1 to 3 years |
| Medicine, law and other professions | Textbooks, cases, atlases, primary research | History and human stories of the field | Case vignettes, image labelling, terminology | Informed understanding, never a licence to practise | 1 to 2 years |

## 1. Authors and literary traditions

Example: `paths/dostoevsky.md`.

- **Order:** accessible works first, then the masterpieces, then the complete canon, then criticism.
- **Quizzes:** plot and characters early; interpretation, debates and critics later.
- **Special rules:** recommend a translation for every work, and a free one where it exists.

## 2. Music and the arts

Example: the classical music path (to be rebuilt in this format).

- **Order:** famous, uplifting pieces; longer works with strong melody; emotional range; structure; the twentieth-century frontier.
- **Companions:** a listening guide for each piece (what to listen for, with timings), basic score reading from the middle stages, and history of each period.
- **Quizzes:** short audio clips: "Which period is this?", "Which instrument has the melody?", "Is this a fugue or a sonata?". Match composer to work, put periods in order.
- **Mastery:** recognise the main periods, forms and composers by ear; follow a score; explain how a symphony, fugue or sonata is built.

## 3. Humanities and social sciences

Examples: geopolitics, philosophy, history, economics.

- **Order:** a narrative entry book; the core introductory textbook; the founding texts of each school; contested debates; current research.
- **Special rules:** present every school of thought in its strongest form, with sources from more than one region and political tradition. Mark clearly where content is opinion or contested.
- **Quizzes:** match thinker to idea, identify which school a quotation belongs to, apply a theory to a real case ("How would a realist explain this alliance?").
- **Mastery:** explain and argue the main schools, read primary sources, and write an analysis of a real case.

## 4. Natural sciences

Example: particle physics.

- **Prerequisite ladder first.** Before Stage 2, a placement check covers the maths and physics the path needs. Particle physics needs calculus, linear algebra, differential equations, classical mechanics, electromagnetism, quantum mechanics and special relativity. A learner who lacks them is routed through short bridge paths first, or continues with a warning (the same rule as failed quizzes).
- **Order, sketched for particle physics:**
  1. First Encounters: Richard Feynman, *QED: The Strange Theory of Light and Matter* (1985), CERN's public videos and visits.
  2. Undergraduate foundations: David Griffiths, *Introduction to Elementary Particles* (2nd edition, 2008), with problem sets.
  3. Core: Mark Thomson, *Modern Particle Physics* (Cambridge University Press, 2013); Francis Halzen and Alan Martin, *Quarks and Leptons* (1984).
  4. Quantum field theory: Michael Peskin and Daniel Schroeder, *An Introduction to Quantum Field Theory* (1995), or a gentler alternative. [verify: recommended gentler QFT text]
  5. The research frontier: review articles, reading arXiv papers, and an analysis using CERN Open Data.
  - Free course companions where available, such as MIT OpenCourseWare. [verify: name the specific courses]
- **Quizzes:** numerical answers with units, short derivations, estimation questions ("Roughly how much energy does the LHC give each proton?"), and diagram reading (Feynman diagrams).
- **Mastery:** solve standard textbook problems, read a current paper and explain its result, and reproduce a simple analysis from open data.

## 5. Mathematics and computing

- **Order:** visual intuition and puzzles; core exercises; proofs; advanced topics; open problems.
- **Quizzes:** worked problems with step-by-step checking, proofs assembled from given steps (a "put in order" game), and code exercises that run in the browser.
- **Mastery:** solve and prove at a strong undergraduate level, and build working programs.

## 6. Medicine, law and other professions

Example: neurosurgery.

- **What the path promises.** It gives informed understanding, roughly the knowledge of a well-read medical student. It never teaches how to perform procedures, never replaces clinical training, and never gives medical advice. Every page carries that notice, and the Mastery certificate says "knowledge path, not a professional qualification."
- **Reviewers:** health and law paths need reviewers who practise in the field, and are reviewed again on a fixed schedule because practice changes.
- **Order, sketched for neurosurgery:**
  1. First Encounters: history and human stories, such as the life of Harvey Cushing and memoirs by neurosurgeons.
  2. Foundations: neuroanatomy and physiology, with an atlas and image-labelling games.
  3. Core neuroscience: Eric Kandel and colleagues, *Principles of Neural Science* (6th edition, 2021).
  4. The specialty: the main conditions (tumours, trauma, vascular disease, spine), how they are diagnosed and treated, using a standard handbook. [verify: recommended handbook and edition]
  5. Evidence and ethics: landmark trials, reading journal articles, consent and risk.
- **Quizzes:** label the structure on a brain scan, case vignettes ("A patient presents with... Which region is most likely involved?"), and terminology matching.
- **Mastery:** explain the major conditions and treatments, read a clinical paper critically, and discuss the ethics of surgical decisions.

## How a new path chooses its template

1. The editor picks the template that fits the subject, or combines two (history of science may borrow from both humanities and natural sciences).
2. The draft follows that template's order, quiz types and mastery standard.
3. Expert reviewers check the template choice as part of their review.
